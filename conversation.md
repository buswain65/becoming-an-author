> **The idea in plain language**
> 
> A strong protagonist is built on a chain of five pieces:
> 
> 1. **The wound:** something in his past that hurt him.
> 2. **The lie:** the false belief he took from it ("I'm not worth much").
> 3. **The want:** what he consciously chases (to go home).
> 4. **The need:** the truth he must learn to let go of the lie.
> 5. **The flaw:** how the lie makes him act, which causes him trouble.
>

> 
> **Example: Sam Vimes in *Guards! Guards!* (Terry Pratchett)**
>

When a dragon starts terrorizing the city, he wants to survive and keep his head down. What he needs is to believe that doing the right job well matters, even when nobody's watching. His flaw, cynicism, nearly keeps him in the bottle. By the end, he's sober and proud of his ragged little Watch. It's also very funny, which is the point: the flaw generates the jokes *and* the arc.

>
