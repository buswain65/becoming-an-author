# Chapter 20

The next morning, I arrived at the cafe before the rest of Plan B. I ordered eggs and sausage and waited for the others.

I didn't have to wait long. Orrin showed up just as my plate arrived. He tried to grab a sausage, but I shooed him away.

"Get your own," I said.

He ordered an omelet, and before he'd finished, Nara and Vagid had arrived and ordered too.

"I learned something last night," I said. "I was talking with Tired Guy, and I found out that time passes differently here than in my world. One year here is one day there."

"How does that even work?" asked Orrin. "You've been here about a month, so what is that in your world?"

"A few hours at most," I said.

"Evan... are you saying goodbye?" asked Nara.

"You're thinking about going home, aren't you?" asked Vagid.

"Honestly, yes. I am," I said. "I could go home, and only a few hours would have passed for them. It'd be like I never left."

"Well... are you going?" asked Orrin.

"No. He's not," said Vagid.

"Why not?" asked Nara.

"I can't. Not until we find a way to fix the problem the summoning causes," I said.

"I really want to go home, but it wouldn't matter. If the summoning doesn't stop, both worlds will be destroyed. What good would it do to get back just to die?" I asked.

"Oh," said Nara. "I forgot about that."

"Way to kill the mood, Evan," said Orrin.

"Then why tell us at all?" asked Vagid.

"Because you're my friends, and you deserve to know," I said. "And I can't just abandon either world. There are people I care about in both."

"Yeah, I figured," said Vagid. "I just wanted to make sure you knew why, too."

"We're with you, Evan," said Nara.

"Mfm, Evmfh," said Orrin around a mouthful of eggs.

We finished the rest of breakfast in comfortable silence. When Nara reached into her pack, Vagid glanced around the cafe. "Not here." We paid and walked down to the landing under the bridge, where I'd first told them everything. Then Nara handed me the bundle of papers. "Here are the notes we found. I can't read most of them."

I started on the first page. It was in English, so I read it aloud to Plan B. It was an introduction from an Otherborn the kingdom called the Plague Mage. Her name was Rachel Lin, and she'd been a nurse in my world.

"Evan, what's a nurse?" asked Orrin. "Does she feed babies or something?"

"A nurse is my world's version of a cleric. A healer," I said.

"So she was a healer in your world, and they brought her here and made her into someone who spread disease and death?" asked Nara, horrified.

"That's barbaric," said Vagid.

"She must have hated her life here," said Orrin.

"I know I would," I said.

Rachel had noted the date she was summoned, first in English, then in Hallish. By Earth's calendar, it was about six months ago.

"She was summoned a hundred and seventy-five years ago," said Vagid. "The date is right there."

I kept reading the English pages aloud. Most of it was a nurse's careful records: dates, towns, symptoms, how long each case lasted. Except the symptoms were ones she'd been ordered to cause. Near the end, one line was underlined twice: *I won't do it again.*

Eventually, I came to a page written in Hallish. *Looks like some kind of treasury writ for buying something.* I couldn't make sense of the legalese, so I passed it to Nara.

> **Writ of Disbursement, Royal Treasury of Sunhallow**
>
> *Account: Chamber Provisions*
>
> Authorized: the purchase of four hundred units, of sound condition, for delivery to Thornspire before the next preparation cycle.
>
> Rate: eleven rays per unit, delivered. Units arriving unfit for use shall not be paid for. Transport and holding costs to be charged to Consumables (Ritual).
>
> Approved: *\[seal\]*, for the Chancellor. Countersigned: *\[clerk's mark\]*

"Evan!" said Nara. "This is for the sacrifices."

*Four hundred units. I should have caught it sooner. It's the same number as my summoning. Four hundred people, used up to curse Rachel with a horrible ability. They didn't even get to reincarnate. All for the sake of the kingdom.*

"Is this real?" I asked.

"It has the Chancellor's seal and a clerk's mark. And there's a note filing it in the Chancellor's archive," said Vagid. "The Plague Mage must have stolen this from the archives in Lumenhold."

"Could someone else have stolen it, and Rachel found it later?" I asked. "Just because she had it doesn't mean she stole it."

"That's fair," said Vagid. "But until we have proof either way, Rachel stealing it is just as likely."

"Maybe there's more there," said Orrin.

Vagid stared at Orrin.

"What?" asked Orrin. "Did I say something weird?"

"For a change, no," said Vagid. Then he looked at me. "It could hold information about all of the summonings. Successes. Failures. Chimeras."

"It might have more about my summoning," I said quietly. Then, louder, "We just need to figure out how to get in and back out without being caught."

"If Rachel managed it without getting caught, we should be able to as well," said Nara.

"Before we try that, we need a way to hide what we already have," I said. "Any ideas?"

"I don't like the idea of setting traps like Rachel did," said Vagid. "Can we hide it without one?"

"What if we hid it at the bottom of the river?" I asked. "That would make it hard to get to without a trap."

"But how would you keep the paper from falling apart if water got in?" asked Orrin.

"What if we hid it somewhere there's already lots of paper?" said Nara. "Like the Guild archives. Or a library?"

I stared at Nara, then said, "Cuthbert would file them, cross-reference them, and never read a word."

I thought for a minute, then said, "But we'd want to leave a clue. If we fail, the next Otherborn should know where to look."

"Nara! Could you carve a mural into a boulder by the river? Something that would point the next Otherborn toward the Guild archives?" I asked.

"I could, but what would it show?" asked Nara. "Everyone in Sunhallow can read Hallish, so I can't write anything."

"Yes, but nobody here can read English," I said. "I'll write out what it needs to say, and you can copy it like a drawing."

"Here, look at this," I said, and wrote out:

> IF YOU'RE FROM EARTH, YOU'RE NOT ALONE. ASK THE GUILD FOR PLAN B'S FILE.

"That's pretty, but what does it say?" asked Nara. So I told them.

"Could you copy that onto the boulder?" I asked.

"I can try, but you'll need to check it so I don't miss anything," said Nara.

"Before you get started, let's go see Cuthbert and have him log the notes in our file," I said.

We got to the Guild and joined Prue's line. When we reached the front, Prue said, "I'm still waiting on that dinner, Evan."

"Uh... I've got a lot going on right now, but soon," I said. "Anyway, we need to see Guild master Cuthbert."

Nara was smiling at my discomfort. Vagid was smirking. And Orrin waited until Prue started leading us up to Cuthbert's office, then said with a snicker, "Yeah, when are you going to take her out to dinner?"

As usual, Prue didn't slow down at the closed doors. She grabbed one of the handles, flung the door open, and said, "Cutty! Plan B wants to see you."

Cuthbert was facing the doors this time. He looked up from his ledger. "Ah, the members of Plan B. Excellent. I wanted to make sure you'd picked up the payment for the Barley Ford job."

"That's right, I completely forgot," I said. "But we came for a different reason. Could you add some documents to Plan B's Guild records?"

"Of course," said Cuthbert, holding out his hand.

I handed him all of the notes, which made quite a stack by now. Cuthbert glanced at the first page, then stamped it. He walked over to a filing cabinet, pulled out a drawer, and thumbed through the files inside. Then he held one open and slid the notes into it.

"Please see Prue on your way out. She will have your payment for the slime core," said Cuthbert as he walked back to his desk and returned to his ledger.

We filed out of Cuthbert's office and went back downstairs. I joined Prue's line while the rest of Plan B waited by the doors.

When I got to the front, I said, "Hi, Prue. I came to pick up the payment for the slime core."

"Yep," she said, and headed for the cashier's office. A few minutes later, she came back with four large bags on a cart.

As she set the bags on the counter, she said, "Don't forget."

"You bet." It was all I could think of.

"Um, this will be hard to carry around. Is there somewhere we can store it?" I asked Prue. "And still get to it if we need it?"

"Yeah. The Guild offers accounts. It's free. We issue you a card with an account number on it. Bring it back here, and you can take out some or all of the money whenever you want," said Prue.

"Great. Please set up an account for me, and I'll put in my whole share from today," I said. I still had money from the Hollin's Cross job, so I didn't need to keep any of the slime money.

Prue sighed at the bag she'd just hauled out, then took it back to the cashier's office. A minute later, she returned with a dull brown card bearing the Guild sigil and a long number.

"Here," said Prue as she tossed the card to me.

I fumbled the catch and had to stoop to pick it up.

*A Guild card. Finally. It wasn't shiny, but I'd take it.*

Then I looked at the card more closely.

*My name isn't on it. Just a number. Some things in this world never change.*

All of Plan B went down to the river, to the spot where I talked with Tired Guy. Nara found a boulder that was quite large and said, "I'll need a lot of sand. This little beach would work, but I'd probably use up all the sand here. We should get sand from the bottom of the river or from another calm area nearby."

"Eddy," I said. "The calm areas are called eddies."

"Ed-dies?" she said.

"Yeah," I said.

Vagid sent Orrin upstream to find sand, while he and I went downstream.

Vagid and I found a large sandbar just offshore, a few houses' lengths downriver. Neither of us had a bucket, so we spread out my cloak and scooped the sand into it. Then we carried it back up to where Nara was waiting. We got there just as Orrin was setting down two large buckets of sand.

*Where did he even find buckets?*

"Whew," said Orrin. "That's great exercise." He dumped out the buckets and headed back upstream for more.

Vagid and I dumped our sand onto the pile Orrin had started, then turned back downstream to get more.

On the second trip back with sand, I saw some of the writing taking shape. It wasn't very deep yet, but it was looking more and more like writing in English.

After the third trip back with sand, Nara was clearly tiring. Keeping the wind going nonstop was hard on her. "If I see one more grain of sand today," she said, "I'm carving your face again."

It was late afternoon, so I suggested dinner at the cafe, my treat. Nobody argued.

On the way back to the chapel, I stopped by Nara's boulder. She'd gotten about halfway.

> IF YOU'RE FROM EARTH, YOU'RE NOT ALONE.
