# Chapter 21

That night, I sat by the river and stared at the half-finished boulder.

*Tired Guy?* I asked.

*Yes, Evan,* said Tired Guy.

*We made it back from Barley Ford,* I said. *Another Otherborn left her notes there. Her name was Rachel Lin.*

There was a pause, then Tired Guy said, *I have some of her memories. She was a nurse in your world. She loved helping people when they were hurt.*

After a moment, he added, *She had a cat. A tortoiseshell named Mittens. Her memories of this world are not... good.*

*Yeah. What happened to Rachel was wrong. But she had the willpower to say, "No more!"* I said. *So why did she create the trap at the farmhouse in Barley Ford?*

*She said no to the kingdom,* said Tired Guy. *And the people who came after her died at that farm.*

*On the way to the farm, her only thoughts were of how much harm she'd caused and what she could do to make amends.* A pause. *Why she built the trap, I can't tell you. Her memories stop at that farm. Whatever happened to her there, I never saw it.*

*I hope she found peace in the end,* I said. *No matter what, everyone deserves that.*

*Do you have any memories of Lumenhold?* I asked. *Maybe Xavier or Rachel had some.*

*I cannot see into Lumenhold. Too much death has occurred there,* said Tired Guy. *But they do have memories of it. Xavier only remembers the entrance and the banquet hall. Rachel's memories of Lumenhold are tinged with hatred and fear. She remembered hidden passages, but I cannot see clearly how to reach them.*

*I remember a movie where two of the heroes slipped out through a hidden door beside the main gate and counterattacked the army breaking through the portcullis,* I said. *Maybe there's something like that.*

*A sally port would make sense, but reaching it would be difficult,* said Tired Guy. *When there is no siege, guards patrol the area around the main gate. If you have another option, I would take it.*

*I'll talk to the rest of Plan B about it. Maybe one of them has an idea,* I said.

*If you go into Lumenhold, remember that I will not be able to see you or help you if you run into trouble,* said Tired Guy. *So please be careful.*

*I will,* I said.

I sat there a while longer, looking at the boulder. Half finished, like everything else.

In the morning, I met Plan B at the cafe for breakfast. I was the last to arrive.

"Hey," I said.

"Good morning, Evan," said Nara, spreading preserves on a slice of buttered toast.

"Ugh," said Orrin, rubbing his stomach. He had clearly gorged himself on breakfast.

"Hi," said Vagid, finishing off a sausage.

I ordered breakfast and ate quickly. When Vagid started to ask about Lumenhold, I shook my head. "Not here. Let's walk down to the river landing."

Once we arrived at the landing under the bridge, I said, "I talked to Tired Guy about going to Lumenhold."

"Did he have any information on it?" asked Vagid.

"Not much. Too many people have died in Lumenhold. He can't see anything inside it, and he can't help us in there," I said. "He said Rachel remembered hidden passages, but he couldn't see how to get to them."

"My brother Dorran mentioned a Guild job from Lower Lumenhold," said Vagid. "He was on his way back from there and crossed paths with a cobbler. The cobbler said he'd just put in a job request with the Guild."

"What's Lower Lumenhold?" I asked.

"It's the village outside the walls of Lumenhold," said Nara. "A lot of the people who work in the castle live there. The ones who aren't allowed to live inside the walls, or can't afford to. Most people just call it the Lower."

"This could be the perfect opportunity to scout the area without raising suspicion," I said. "We should check in with Mara to see what the job is about."

"Don't you mean Prue?" asked Vagid, smirking. "And don't you still owe her dinner?"

"That's why I said Mara. So you could go ask while I stand by the door with Nara and Orrin," I said.

"Safe," said Orrin.

"Exactly," I said.

"You mean hiding, right?" asked Nara.

"Strategically positioned," I said.

We left for the Guild. Nara, Orrin, and I took up our strategic position by the door while Vagid joined Mara's line.

When Vagid got to the front of the line, Mara said, "Hi, Vagid! It's so good to see you again!"

"Hi, Mara. My brother mentioned that there was a Guild job in Lower Lumenhold," said Vagid.

"Yes, there are some kobolds causing the people of Lower Lumenhold problems. No one has taken the job yet, because it doesn't pay well," said Mara.

"We'll take the job," said Vagid.

"Oh! You are so sweet to help out those poor people, Vagid," said Mara, smiling brightly.

From the doorway, I could see Vagid's ears turning red. Mara gave him the job slip. "Okay. Have fun!" she said, waving as he left.

Vagid had just started walking back to our group when I heard a voice right behind me.

"Leaving town?" said Prue. "Convenient."

"Eep! It's a job! Kobolds!" I said, a bit too quickly.

"Then you'd better come back. You still owe me dinner," said Prue, turning and heading back to her counter.

Vagid walked up, chuckling, and said, "So the strategic positioning didn't work out, huh?"

"I saw Prue walking over, but I thought it would be more fun to watch what happened," said Nara. "And it was."

Orrin just laughed and pointed at me.

Vagid read us the job slip. It was simple enough: about a dozen kobolds were causing trouble for the people of Lower Lumenhold. He finished with, "...and ask for Tobin the cobbler. Pay: four sparks." Mara was right. The pay wouldn't even cover the coach fare to get there. But it gave us an excuse, and that was what we needed.

"Four sparks? For a dozen kobolds?" asked Orrin. "That's like three kobolds per spark."

"Well, they don't have much, so it's probably all they can afford," said Vagid. "Somebody has to take it."

Vagid led us to an inn where we could arrange the coach ride. Nara, Orrin, and Vagid each paid their three rays, two for the ride and one for the cushion. Then it was my turn. *I might as well be comfortable too.* I handed over my three rays.

The coach left around noon, and we were all very comfortable on our cushions. By dusk, we had made it to a small town with an inn, where we stopped for the night. Dinner was rabbit stew and hard bread. *Rabbit stew. Again. Rabbits really are plentiful in every world.*

The common room was full of travelers. At the next table, a carter hauling wine casks to the castle was complaining to anyone who would listen.

"Third trip this month," he said. "They take the casks at the gate. Never let you past it. Twenty years I've been hauling for Lumenhold, and I've never seen the inside."

"Nobody does," said the woman beside him. "Things go into Lumenhold. Not much comes out."

Orrin leaned over to us. "Cheerful."

*I knew at least two people that was true for.*

We slept in the inn's common room. In the morning, we each paid two sparks for a light breakfast of eggs and cheese. Our pay from the job would only cover half our breakfast.

The coach set off on the last leg of the journey to Lumenhold. I watched the scenery go by: fields where farmers were bringing in the harvest, and small stands of trees along the road. As the sky dimmed for evening, we came around a small hill, and Vagid said, "That's Lumenhold."

It was a large castle, its white walls catching the last of the sunset. From here, it looked like a postcard. Katie and I had bought a dozen just like it in Europe. Just the highlights.

Below the walls, on the side without a gate, a few cooking fires flickered in the dark. That would be Lower Lumenhold.

*Somewhere in that castle, someone decided I was defective.*
