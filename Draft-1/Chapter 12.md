# Chapter 12

We left the berry patch and started back to the farmer's house. We agreed that the farmer and his family deserved to know the nightmare in the berry patch was gone. We also agreed that we shouldn't tell them what it had been.

On the walk back, no one said a word. Orrin, who usually filled up the silence, hadn't said anything since the berry patch, which worried me more than anything else.

We decided to camp at the little bridge over the river. We could all use some rest and something to eat. I pulled out some jerky and handed it around.

Without saying anything, I took the first watch. At least everyone looked like they were able to get to sleep. Then Nara said in her sleep, "No. Please, no." I could see Vagid tossing and turning. Orrin sat bolt upright at one point, looking around wildly, before lying back down.

I woke Nara for her watch and went to the river to get cleaned up.

*Tired Guy?*

*Yes, Evan.*

*You knew. You knew what it was.*

*Yes.*

*Why didn't you tell me?*

*I knew that the chimera was a tortured soul, but nothing else until the man's soul was freed. Then I was able to commune with him and learn his tale.*

*What happened to him?*

*The person responsible for the summoning ritual thought that it was too costly to pay for slaves to sacrifice and decided to use monster cores.*

*Slaves?* My stomach turned, but I made myself keep going.

*Monster cores?*

*Yes. They have power, but using them for this ritual caused side effects. The monster cores corrupted the person that was being summoned. It turned them into a combination of all the monsters the cores came from, a chimera.*

*You said to be kind if I could, but we killed him.*

*Yes, you did. But that was also a kindness. He was trapped in that body. Unable to control it or keep it from consuming all of those people. And yet completely aware of what was happening.*

*Ugh. I feel sick.*

*Any sane person would feel sick. I know that you do not want to hear this, but by killing the monster, you were able to free that man's soul. He was able to be reincarnated, all thanks to your party.*

*Yeah**.** **I just **d**o**n**'**t fe**el good** about k**i**l**l**i**n**g**.*

*You would not be you if you did.*

*Is it just chimeras?*

There was a long silence.

*Good night, Evan.*

At first light, we all got ready and left the camp site. It didn't take long to reach the farmer's house. "It's dead," Vagid told them. "It won't hurt anyone else." He paused. "We still don't know where the bodies are coming from."

I could see the relief in Sadie's face, and Miles didn't look as standoffish. I saw a small face at the window, watching us. Her eyes were full of tears.

Sadie thanked us twice. Miles shook Vagid's hand and didn't let go for a long moment. Then we started back to Millbrook.

We camped at the same site we used on the way down. When we got there, everyone split off to do the same jobs as before. I was able to get the fire going on my own this time.

When Orrin came back with another rabbit, Vagid took it and started cooking a stew.

*I guess rabbits are plentiful **n**o** **m**atter** **w**h**a**t** **world you're in.*

While the stew was cooking, Orrin said, "You told the miners there was no such thing as ghosts."

"Yeah."

"So, what was that?"

I couldn't think of any answer that they would believe.

"I don't know. But I think that there are others like him out in this world, and I want to find them."

"Where would we look?" asked Nara.

"Places that are haunted. Places that are desecrated. Anywhere a lot of people have died."

"Why desecrated?" asked Vagid.

"Just a hunch."

Vagid looked at me a moment longer than he needed to. Then he said, "The Guild keeps records of hauntings. People post jobs to get rid of them."

"When will we get back to Millbrook?" I asked.

"Not until dinnertime," said Orrin.

"Then first thing in the morning, we meet at the café and head to the Guild," I said.

The others nodded.

We didn't say much for the rest of the trip, but it was a different kind of quiet.

The next morning, we met at the café, ate a quick breakfast, and headed to the Guild.

When we got there, Vagid said, "You should probably ask about the hauntings. You know more about this than I do."

I stood there for a second, then nodded and joined Prue's line, which was, as always, the shortest. When I reached the front, Prue greeted me with, "What do you want?"

"I'd like to know about any haunted ruins, or desecrated sites," I said.

"Weird, but whatever," said Prue. "Give me a minute to check."

Prue opened a huge ledger and started paging through. After a moment, she stopped, picked up a blank piece of paper, wrote something down, and folded it.

She held the paper out to me with an expectant expression.

"For me?" I asked innocently.

Prue just sighed and rolled her eyes.

I took the paper and walked back to Plan B.

I unfolded the paper and read it.

"Haunted, desecrated church near the border. No takers."

"No takers. That's a good sign," said Orrin.

"Or not," said Vagid.

"Near the border. That'll be a three- or four-day walk," said Vagid.

"I wonder if we can afford a coach," said Nara.

"I can't," I said.

"We'll need to resupply before we go," said Orrin.

We left the Guild and started working through our list of tasks. By lunch, we were done, so we went our separate ways until the morning.

I went back to the chapel. I needed to let Halden know I'd be gone longer this time.

I entered the chapel and found Halden.

"I'm leaving on another trip with Plan B. It's to a haunted church near the border, so I'll be gone ten to fourteen days," I said.

"Fine. I'll add it to the—" Halden stopped. "Which church?"

"The old chapel near Hollin's Cross," I said.

"The old chapel near Hollin's Cross?" Halden's face lost all expression. "That's not even in Sunhallow anymore."

Halden thought for a second while staring at me, then said, "That chapel is desecrated. That means the gods can't see it, and anyone who dies there doesn't reach them."

"I know," I said.

Halden's eyes narrowed. "How?"

"...Lucky guess?"

"Hmm," said Halden. "I've heard that even the priests won't go near it, and the ones who did came back wrong, or didn't come back."

"It's kind of the reason we're going," I said.

Halden looked at me for a second, then said, "Don't die there."

He turned to go. Halfway to the door, without looking back, he said, "I'll add it to the tab."

Plan B met at the café at first light. We got our breakfasts wrapped to go and set off for Hollin's Cross. I was in high spirits for the first day of walking. We made good time, and when we stopped for the day, we each took to our normal jobs.

*I really hope Orrin can find something other than rabbit.*

I got the fire going, and when Orrin said, "Nice fire," I was ready and only jumped a little bit.

I turned around to see what Orrin had found for dinner and was surprised to see he had several fish with a branch through their gills.

"Does this mean we're going to have fish stew?" I asked.

"Fish stew? No. You cook fish on an open fire," said Vagid. "Rub them with some salt, garlic, or onions, and let them cook."

I nodded because I had no idea how to cook fish.

The fish were done soon, and we all ate our fill.

I still had first watch, which was uneventful. I woke Nara for her watch and went to bed.

"Evan, time to get up," said Orrin.

"Uhh," I said, and tried to sit up. That was the precise moment my back decided to lock up like a board. "Eep."

"Back troubles, old man?" asked Vagid.

"I'm not that old," I said, trying to stand up straight.

"You should stretch out before going to bed," said Orrin.

Oh, yeah. I did remember seeing each of them run through a short stretching routine before bed. I made a mental note to ask them for some pointers.

"Uh. My feet are killing me," I said. "What do you do for sore feet?"

"I usually soak them in a cool stream or the river," said Nara. "Not for too long, but it really helps."

The second day was just a repeat of the first. Walking, resting, eating, walking, resting, eating. When we stopped for the evening, it was near a small stream, no more than three or four feet across, and you could see it wasn't very deep.

After a dinner of something that wasn't rabbit (my guess was wild boar), I gave Nara's suggestion a try. Sitting on the bank, resting my feet in the cool water, I said, "Ah. This feels much better."

"See, I told you," Nara said from right behind me.

I would have jumped, but I was too tired to do anything other than flinch. "You were right," I said.

We sat there soaking our feet for a few more minutes, then I went back to start my watch. While everyone was asleep, I tried some light stretching and thought I'd done a pretty good job. At least my back wasn't hurting as much.

When my watch finished, I woke Nara and went to sleep.

On day three, I was able to get up without as much pain in either my back or feet. This was definitely going to become a routine for me.

"We should reach the border by lunch today," said Vagid. "Hollin's Cross is a few hours past that."

We ate a light breakfast and set off.

Just before lunchtime, we got to the border. I wasn't sure what I'd expected to see. Turnstiles? A wall that went from horizon to horizon? I didn't expect to see a man sitting in a chair with a clipboard by a small wooden fence. There was a short line of people waiting, and we joined the queue.

I could hear the conversations as we got closer. The man in the chair asked the same three questions: Name? Where are you coming from? Where are you going?

For some reason, all of Plan B lined up behind me, so I was the first one from the party to reach the man.

"Name?" he said.

"Evan McIntyre."

He looked at me. "Pft." Then he wrote something short. Probably just "Evan."

"Where're ya from?"

"Millbrook," I said. He scribbled that down.

"Where're ya goin'?"

"Hollin's Cross," I said.

Then the pattern changed. "Why?" he asked. Not in a bored way, but the way you'd ask a friend who'd just told you he wanted to slam his hand in a car door.

"Job?" I said.

He stared at me for a second, then wrote it down and waved me on. I stepped forward and turned to see all of Plan B snickering. I figured that was why they'd let me go first.

Vagid was the last of us through. He walked up and said, "Welcome to Dravia."

We continued on after a short stop to rest and get something to eat. It was midafternoon when I saw earthen walls with houses beyond them.

"This is Hollin's Cross," said Vagid. "The church should be about half an hour past here. We should get a room at the inn and rest today. We can go to the church after breakfast."

Hollin's Cross seemed nice. It was smaller than Millbrook, but the few people I saw looked just like the people in Millbrook. Their clothes were different colors, maybe, but they still seemed like good, honest people.

"This is the inn I was talking about," said Vagid as we approached a large building.

Its sign was just a picture of a bed.

Vagid led us into the inn. It was an open room with a bar along part of the back wall. Simple round tables were spread out evenly between the door and the bar. Vagid walked us up to the bar, where a friendly-looking woman stood.

"Evening, my lovelies. I'm Mave, and I run the Long Rest. What can I offer you? Some dinner? Mead? Or maybe a room?" she asked.

"We'll take a room and dinner for all of us," said Vagid.

"That'll be three rays and two sparks, please."

Vagid paid, and Mave said, "Thank ya kindly. Your room is up the stairs, fourth on the left. Dinner will be ready in a couple of hours."

As we turned to go up the stairs, I heard a deep, rumbling voice behind us say, "What kept you?"

I knew that voice.
