# Chapter 16

The next morning, Mave was waiting for us with breakfast again. *I could get used to this.*

"I just wanted to say thank you again for taking care of the chapel. It's been a problem for as long as I can remember," said Mave.

"We were happy to help, Mave," said Orrin, his mouth stuffed with eggs and bacon.

"What he said," said Nara.

"Indeed," said Vagid.

"Hmf," said Halden, nodding.

When we'd finished breakfast, we gathered in front of the inn. Mave came out and gave each of us another hug, which we gladly accepted. Then we climbed into the coach. To make up for the walk to Hollin's Cross, I paid for cushions for all of Plan B. I had just enough saved from my share of the goblin job. Halden could take care of his own.

Halden had been right about the coach. It was quick and pleasant.

Once we'd unloaded our gear, Halden headed back to the chapel, and Plan B went to the Millbrook Guild to report on the job. By unspoken agreement, Nara, Orrin, and I waited by the door while Vagid joined Mara's line.

After a few minutes, he reached the front of the line, and Mara said, "Hi, Vagid! It's so nice to see you again!"

"Hi, Mara. We finished this job," said Vagid, handing her the job slip.

"Oh. This is an old one. Let me check on what we need to do. Hey, Prue?" said Mara.

I heard an overly dramatic sigh, then Prue said, "Yes?"

"Plan B finished the Haunted Chapel job in Hollin's Cross. How do we confirm it?"

Another overly dramatic sigh, louder this time. "You have to send someone out to verify. It'll probably take a couple of days," said Prue.

Vagid frowned and said, "Wouldn't that be more like three to five days?"

Prue sighed again. "Whatever."

"Okay. Thank you, Prue! Bye!" said Mara, even perkier than usual, as if she could fight Prue's gloom with sheer perkiness.

We all wanted to get back to our own places and rest, so we agreed to meet at the cafe in the morning.

I walked down to the little eddy below the chapel and sat on the old stone bench. I just listened to the river for a while. Then I thought, *Tired Guy?*

*Yes, Evan. It looks like you survived your trip.*

*Yeah, and we found the notes. How can I get the information to you?*

*You can just read them to me.*

*Oh. Okay.*

I took out the notes and started to read them out loud.

*Evan?*

*Yeah?*

*You don't have to read them out loud. Just read them normally. We're not talking out loud right now, are we?*

I closed my mouth.

I started over, this time reading them in my head, the way I talked to Tired Guy. He didn't say anything until I'd finished. Then Tired Guy said, *What have you figured out from this information?*

*Our deadline is nine months. Vagid and I think MC stands for monster cores. And they only kept track of the summonings where someone actually arrived in this world.*

*Why do you think "nine months to a year" was circled in pink, with a line to that odd phrase?* asked Tired Guy.

*Good question. I wish I could find someone who could read it.*

*That reminds me of a technique Xavier used, called raking light. Are you familiar with it?*

*Xavier? He's with you?*

*He came to me after he passed on. I saw everything he knew.* Tired Guy paused, then said, *Then I sent him home.*

*Home? Like to Earth?*

*A new life. He won't remember any of this.*

*That's probably a good thing, considering what happened to him here.*

*It is. I'd forget most of what I've seen, if I could,* said Tired Guy.

*Raking light. Hm. Never heard of it. How does it work?*

*You shine a light across the page from a very low angle, and you can see the indentations the pen made when the words were written.*

*Hm. Okay. I'll borrow a candle from Halden and give it a try tonight.*

*Please let me know what you find.*

*Of course! We're in this together. Good night.*

*Good night, Evan.*

I went back to the chapel to find Halden. He was in the main room. I said, "Can I borrow a candle?"

"They're in the cupboard. Why do you need one?" asked Halden.

"Just trying to find something out," I said.

"Hmf." He turned away.

*If I find something, I'll let him know.*

I got the candle and went back to my room. I set it in the holder, lit it, and held the paper up at a sharp angle to the light. The first time, I made the mistake of looking toward the candle with the paper in between.

Once my vision cleared, I tried again, this time with the candle off to one side and the paper at a sharp angle to it. Sure enough, I could see the indentations the quill had made for all the text on the page. Then I shifted the paper so the pink writing was closer to the light. Now it looked like "bmonths."

*What does that mean?*

I decided to ask Halden about it. Maybe it meant something in this world that I didn't know about.

I found Halden back in the main room. "Halden, can you take a look at this?" I said, and showed him the page with the pink writing in the margin.

"Hm. I still can't read that funny language," said Halden.

*Oh. Right. It's in English.* So I took a blank piece of paper and wrote out what it said in Hallish, starting with the parts I could still see in pink.

"These are the letters that are still pink, but there were more that faded away. That's what I needed the candle for," I said. As I talked, I filled in the missing letters. "This is the whole thing. Do you know what it means?"

Halden looked at it for a few minutes and said, "Six months." Then he turned and walked back toward his room.

I stared at the paper. Where I'd copied the "b," my hand had written this world's number six. In English, a sloppy six looks a lot like a lowercase "b."

*Six months.* Suddenly, my heart was racing, and I was dizzy. I grabbed the door frame to steady myself.

"Crap. Six months," I whispered. Then I ran from the chapel to the river. I had to tell Tired Guy. Fast.

I lost track of how long it took me to run the mile and a half back to the eddy where I'd first talked to Tired Guy, but I got there completely out of breath.

*Tired Guy!*

*Evan, what is it?*

*I figured out the pink writing! It says six months. Six months, not nine months to a year,* I said quickly.

For a long moment, there was only the sound of the river.

*That changes things,* said Tired Guy. *What will you do?*

*First, I need to tell Plan B. We need to speed things up, or we'll miss our chance. Then we'll start finding more places like the chapel in Hollin's Cross.*

*Good luck,* said Tired Guy.

I sat on the bench until my breathing slowed. The river didn't care about deadlines. I envied it.
