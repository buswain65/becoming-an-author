# Chapter 11

As I lay down to sleep, I thought about the boy we'd saved from the river. His mother had said, "I thought for sure he was going to end up like those bodies they keep finding in the nets."

The Guild had closed that job. But if bodies were still showing up, we needed to look into it. I just wasn't sure the others would agree, since we'd be doing it "off the books."

The next morning, I woke up early, mostly because my back felt like someone had used it as a drum. After getting dressed, I ran to the café as fast as my back would let me. When I arrived, only Nara was there.

"What's the hurry, Evan?"

"Huff. Huff. The boy. Huff. From the river. Huff. His mother said, 'like the bodies they keep finding.' Huff."

"Breathe, Evan," said Nara.

I took a few deep breaths. "The Guild closed the job, but the bodies are still showing up. I think we should look into it. I know it won't pay anything, and I understand if—"

Nara tilted her head, like I'd said something in English again. "Evan. We found *you* looking for those bodies. Did you think we stopped caring about them when the Guild stopped paying?"

I opened my mouth, then closed it.

*They'd spent their monster cores to heal a stranger they pulled out of a river. Of course they'd say yes.*

Once Orrin and Vagid arrived, Nara and I told them about the bodies and asked what they thought. Orrin nodded before I'd even finished.

"We were going to bring that up today," said Vagid. "The job is closed, but the problem still exists. We'll need to gather supplies and plan to be out in the field for at least a week. If we haven't found anything by then, we can come back, resupply, and go out for another week."

"We can forage in the forest, and fish, trap, or hunt for meat," said Orrin.

*My entire wilderness experience was one kayaking course and a lot of survival videos. This should be fun.*

The weather was starting to turn colder, so we made sure we had the right gear. Once that was done, we stopped by the Guild to let them know we'd be out of town for a week.

It wasn't like the Guild had a rule about it, but one member of our party didn't want to be gone that long without telling a certain receptionist.

Nara, Orrin, and I waited by the door to the Guild while Vagid got in Mara's line. When he got to the front, he said, "Plan B is going out of town for a week, and we just wanted to let you know."

"Hi, Vagid! It's so good to see you again!" said Mara.

"Hi. As I said, Plan B will be going out of town for a week, and we just wanted to let the Guild know."

"Okay. Have fun!"

*Still stuck in a loop. Someone really needs to add a stop condition.*

We left the Guild and headed downstream. We needed more information from the farmers. Maybe there was a pattern we'd missed before.

As we walked downstream, I asked, "Are the bodies all turning up at one village, or at several villages along the river?"

"We checked three villages. Only one said they were finding bodies in their nets," said Vagid. "The farmers weren't very friendly, so getting any information out of them was difficult."

"Did you only talk to the farmers?" I asked.

"That's who the job request said to talk to. We went to the farmer who put in the request, and he pointed us to the others."

"Did you talk to any of the farmers' wives or children?"

"Why would we?"

"Wives and kids are the ones who notice everything," I said. "My guess is that one of the wives made her husband put in the request. He probably would have just let it go on."

Vagid thought about it for a moment, then said, "Okay. We'll give it a try. Talking to the farmers didn't get us any closer to an answer, so we should try something else."

The rest of the day was uneventful. As evening came on, Orrin said, "This is near where we camped last time. We should probably pitch camp here for the night."

"Okay," said Vagid. "Orrin, pick the campsite, and we'll start setting up. Once you've picked it, see if you can catch something for dinner."

"This is the spot we used last time," said Orrin. "I'll go see what dinner is going to be."

Orrin slipped into the woods without a sound.

*For someone so loud,** **t**hat guy is spooky good at that.*

"Evan, can you get the fire started?" asked Vagid.

"Yeah, sure," I said. "I think."

"Do you have a starter or flint?" asked Nara.

My blank look must have given me away, because she said, "A starter is a little tool that strikes the spark for you. It uses flint, and it isn't very expensive, but not everyone has one. If you don't, you can use flint, or a hard white stone, and a knife."

"Um. I don't have any of that. Sorry," I said.

"Don't worry. You can use my starter, and I'm sure Orrin would be happy to show you what the white stone looks like and how to start a fire with it," said Nara.

She handed me something that looked like a large pair of tweezers with a little box at the hinge. I held it like tweezers, and Nara laughed.

"Not like that, silly," she said. She took it back and showed me how it worked. You held the tweezer "arms" and squeezed them together, like scissors, and sparks shot out of the box at the hinge.

*Wow. That's actually kind of cool.*

Nara helped me gather twigs and dry leaves to use for tinder, then sticks and small dead branches to help build the fire up. Lastly, we found some larger pieces of wood to use as fuel, which I had to chop up using a hand axe. Nara and Vagid were stifling laughs watching me use the starter. Apparently, I was supposed to blow on the tinder where the sparks landed. Eventually, we had a roaring fire.

Nara and I were watching the fire when Orrin said, "Hey. Nice fire!"

I jumped up and whirled around to find him standing right behind me, holding a butchered rabbit.

Nara sighed. "Really, Orrin."

"Here's dinner," said Orrin.

"Okay, who's the best cook?" I asked.

"Vagid," said Orrin and Nara in unison.

"They've both tried cooking," said Vagid. "So, how about some rabbit stew, since it's chilly out?"

Everyone was in favor of the stew, and before long, we had bowls filled with small pieces of rabbit and some wild vegetables Orrin had foraged.

We each took a shift on watch, so no one had to stay up for more than about three hours.

Around three in the morning, I woke up needing to go to the bathroom. When I got up, I saw Nara using her wind magic to shape little sculptures out of sand. I was so entranced watching her work that I completely forgot why I'd gotten up.

"Wow, that's incredible," I whispered.

"No. It's just something I do to pass the time," she said.

"But you could make some amazing art with this," I said.

"No one would want them," Nara said. She let her magic go, and the sculptures slumped into little piles of sand. "Not when they look like this without the magic."

"Well, couldn't you make sculptures out of wood or stone?"

"How would I do that?"

"With what you already have here," I said.

Nara clearly didn't see what I meant, so I explained.

"Find a piece of wood or stone, and a pile of fine sand. Then use your wind to blast the sand against it. The sand wears it away a little at a time, so you can carve it into whatever you want. Where I come from, it's called sandblasting."

Nara thought about it for a moment, then said, "Hm. This might work." She tilted her head. "Oh. What woke you up, anyway?"

It took me a second to remember. Then I jumped up and hurried into the woods.

*Ah, sweet relief.*

When I came back, Nara asked, "Whose watch is next?"

"Orrin, then Vagid," I said.

"Good. Get some sleep, Evan."

"Good night, Nara."

I lay back down and fell asleep almost immediately.

"Hey, Evan. Time to get up," I heard Vagid say.

"Five more minutes, Dad."

"Dad? I'm younger than you."

"Huh? Oh. Okay. Sorry, I'm getting up."

I stretched, got up, and started packing my things. As I worked, I looked around at the others going through the same routine. All except Nara. Her things were already packed and waiting, but there was no sign of her.

"Hey, where's Nara?" I asked.

"Over by the river. She's been there a while," said Orrin.

Once my things were packed, I checked whether Orrin and Vagid needed any help. They didn't.

"I'm going to go check on Nara," I said.

"Okay. Let her know we need to get moving soon," said Vagid.

I followed the sound of the river and found Nara sitting on a boulder, resting. The river was loud here, rushing over the boulders that formed the rapids.

"Good morning, Nara!" I yelled.

"Good morning, Evan!" she yelled back.

"Did you stay up all night?"

"I did. I was interested in your idea."

"Oh. Did you try it out?"

"You didn't see it?"

"See what?"

Nara looked down at the boulder and said, "This."

I took another look. It didn't look like a typical boulder. It had grooves in it, or channels of some kind, on the side facing the river. I had to wade out into the shallows to get a good look. There, on the boulder Nara was sitting on, with the river flowing past it, was a carved face. It wasn't Michelangelo, but it was very good.

"You did this last night?"

"I did."

"I can't get out far enough to tell whose face it is," I said.

"It's yours," she said.

"Mine? Why me?"

"You gave me the idea, so it only seemed right."

*Great. Now there's a permanent record of my face looking confused.*

I looked at it again. Someone had stayed up all night carving my face into stone. Something meant to last.

*Huh.*

"Evan! Nara! We need to get going!" Vagid yelled from up the trail.

I helped Nara down from the boulder, and we hurried back up the game trail.

"What was taking so long?" asked Orrin.

"I wanted to show Evan something I found," said Nara.

"Oh. Is it important?"

"Not really. It was just interesting."

*Something she found.*

Vagid led the rest of the way. On the walk, he told us that only the farmer who'd put in the request had talked about the bodies. The others had barely talked at all.

"Barely talked, or wouldn't?" I asked.

Vagid thought about it. "...Wouldn't. They looked scared, now that I think about it."

We came out of the forest, and I saw a small farm with fenced-in fields to our right.

"That's the farm. And it looks like the farmer is over by the barn," said Vagid.

Vagid led us toward the house, and the farmer started our way as soon as he saw us.

"What do you want now?" he snapped. He planted himself on the path to the house, arms crossed.

Vagid, who came up to the farmer's chest, didn't even slow down. He just walked right past him toward the house.

"Wait, what?" the farmer sputtered, hurrying to get in front of him again. "Where do you think you're going?"

"To talk to the person who knows the most about things around here," said Vagid, walking around him again.

Vagid knocked on the door. After a few seconds, it opened, and a frightened older woman looked out.

"What do you want?" she asked quietly.

"We wanted to ask you about the bodies that have been showing up in the nets around here," said Vagid. "I think you know more about them than your husband does, or at least more than he's willing to share with us. We want to find out what's putting the bodies in the river, so we can put a stop to it. We don't want your children finding things like that, and we don't want anyone here being afraid anymore."

The farmer's wife glanced past Vagid to her husband, who was shaking his head frantically.

I could hear children playing inside the house. The wife looked back over her shoulder, then stepped outside and closed the door. She sat down on the porch steps and took a deep breath.

"They show up two or three at a time. Every few weeks," she whispered.

"Quiet, Sadie! You want—" the farmer hissed, then stopped himself.

"We can't keep going on like this, Miles! The children are scared. I'm scared. All the time. This is no way to live," Sadie said.

Miles stared at the house for a long moment, then at Sadie. He took a calming breath and said, "...Tell them."

"They're all torn up. Like a wild animal attacked them. But the claw marks are too big to be from anything around here," said Sadie.

"Almost three fingers between each claw," said Miles, holding up three fingers pressed together. He shivered. "And some of them look like part of them was eaten."

Vagid's face was grim. "Thank you both," he said. "It took a lot of courage to tell us this."

"Has anyone seen whatever's doing this?" I asked quickly.

Sadie hesitated. "Our oldest did. She won't say much about it. Just that it was big, and it was wrong. Like it was put together from pieces of different animals."

"Did she say where she saw it?" I asked.

"No. She won't talk about it."

I thought for a moment, then said, "Is there anywhere she avoids now? Maybe somewhere she used to go all the time, but now won't go near?"

Sadie and Miles looked at each other. "The old berry patch," said Miles slowly. "Up the creek, past the bend. She used to go every day this time of year. She hasn't been back since spring."

"Thank you," I said. "And tell your daughter thank you too. She's braver than she knows."

With Miles and Sadie's help, Vagid drew a rough map, and we left for the berry patch right away.

On the way, we came to a little wooden bridge over the river. Before we crossed, I asked if we could take a quick break. Everyone was on edge, so stopping seemed like a good idea, especially if we were heading into something dangerous.

We rested under the trees on the farm side of the river. I closed my eyes and thought, *Tired Guy?*

*Yes, Evan?*

*We're about to go into something really dangerous, and I just wanted to let you know first. I don't know what's going to happen.*

*Oh? Where are you going?*

*There's a beast that's killing people. It looks like it's eating them, too.*

*Hm. Do you know what it looks like?*

*The only description we have is that it looks like it was put together from pieces of different animals.*

There was a long pause. Then: *Oh, no.*

*Wait! What is it?*

*B**e **c**a**r**e**f**u**l**,** **E**v**a**n. A**n**d**.**.**. **b**e** **k**i**nd to **i**t**,** **i**f** **y**o**u** **can.*

And then Tired Guy was gone.

*Kind? What does Tired Guy mean by that?*

"Evan, you ready?" asked Vagid.

No, I was definitely not ready. But I stood up anyway.

We crossed the river and continued toward the berry patch.

After a short while, Vagid signaled for us to stop. We gathered by a large tree, and he whispered, "No more talking. Hand signals only until we know what we're dealing with."

We all nodded. Vagid pointed at Orrin, then up the creek, and Orrin slipped off in that direction without a sound.

*Orrin**:** scout ahead.*

Vagid pointed at Nara and held up his hands like he was shielding himself. She nodded.

*Na**r**a**:** **make a **shield.*

Last, he pointed at me, then at himself, walked two fingers through the air, and pointed ahead.

*And we're the bait.*

I nodded, and we started forward as quietly as we could, which was only slightly quieter than the river.

As Vagid and I came over a hill, we saw the berry patch. Or what was left of it.

It had become a huge mud pit, ringed with broken berry bushes. The trees around it had been snapped off just above six feet. Not uprooted. Not cut. Snapped, like twigs.

*Just at my head height.*

Vagid signaled, and we moved closer. Now I could see bones sticking out of the mud. What looked like a femur. *And is that a spine?*

Then we heard it: a wet crunching sound, off to our left. We turned and saw... something. It was hunched over, gnawing on whatever it had. I couldn't see what. I didn't want to.

I tried to get a better look by stepping around the tree I was hiding behind. With my eyes locked on the thing, I didn't pay attention to where I was putting my feet, and I stepped on a broken branch.

*SNAP.*

*Oh, crap.*

The thing's head whipped around toward us. At least, I thought it was looking at us. Sadie's daughter was right. It was wrong. There were arms sticking out of it in strange places, and horns growing out of its stomach. One arm ended in a single massive claw, while the other had more fingers than I could count. It hurt my brain just to look at it.

There didn't seem to be any eyes where I expected them. There was something round and bluish on one side of its head, and three small, round blobs on the other. Were they eyes? I had no idea.

It looked like there were markings on its skin, but I couldn't tell what they were from this distance.

It leapt up and charged straight at my tree. Out of the corner of my eye, I saw Vagid peel off and circle wide.

*Crap, that thing is fast!*

*Thunk. Thunk. Thunk.* Arrows sprouted from the thing's head. It didn't even slow down.

When it was fifteen feet away, it lunged straight at my tree. I dove to the side and rolled. When I stopped rolling, I looked back at the tree I'd been hiding behind.

It was snapped off. Just at my head height.

*I really wish I was the same height as Vagid right now.*

To my right, the thing skidded through some trees, then bounded back toward me. I didn't have the time or the distance to get out of its reach.

*Great. I finally start to like it here, and now I'm going to die. Again.*

The thing swung its claw at me. There was no way to get out of the way. Then the huge claw slammed into something in midair. It looked like heat haze shimmering above me, but it wasn't hot.

Then I heard Nara shout, "Wind Scythe!" A spinning disk of sand whirled over my head and sheared off the clawed arm.

The arm fell right at my feet, and I saw that the markings I'd noticed were actually words.

"No Regrats."

*That's odd.*

The thing had stopped to stare at the stump of its arm. I saw Vagid dart in from the side, duck under its leg, and stab upward through its groin. He gave the sword a strange twist as he pulled it out. I didn't know what he'd done, but blood came down in buckets right where he'd been standing a moment before.

*Femoral artery.* Somewhere in my brain, a nature documentary narrator nodded approvingly.

The thing screamed. It was an awful sound, like hundreds of animals all screaming at once.

"Wind Scythe!" Nara yelled, louder this time.

Another spinning disk of sand streaked over my head. Blood sprayed everywhere, and I was soaked in it. I was staring down at my clothes when I heard a *BOOM*. I looked up to see the thing's head lying on the ground a few feet from its body.

*Mental note**:** seriously**.** never upset Nara. Ever.*

Orrin stepped out from behind a tree a few feet away. I'd had no idea he was anywhere near me. Nara was sitting on the ground, leaning against another tree. Vagid came around from the other side of the body.

I stared down at the severed arm. That writing seemed familiar.

"Do any of you know what this says?" I asked.

"I can't even make out any letters. It just looks like squiggles and lines to me," said Vagid.

"Can't make out any letters? Really? It's right th—" I stopped.

*Vagid can't read it. But I can.*

"Oh, no," I said, and scrambled to a bush to throw up.

After I finished emptying my stomach, I turned back to the others and saw that something was happening to the thing. It was starting to glow. Just a little at a time, but it was spreading.

"Any of you know what that's about?" I asked, pointing at the corpse.

"What the hell is going on?" said Orrin, ducking behind a tree. He kept an eye on the thing, just in case.

It looked like the light was dripping off the thing, but instead of falling to the ground, it was rising.

The drops of light slowly merged into the figure of a man. He looked completely normal. He was wearing a T-shirt, jeans, and boots, and a baseball cap. *I wish I knew more about baseball.** **M**a**y**b**e** **I** **c**oul**d tell **w**h**e**r**e** **he was **from.*

On his arm was a tattoo: "No Regrats."

The others were staring where the man was.

"Are you seeing this?" I asked. I needed to be sure.

I looked at the others. Nara had her hand pressed to her mouth. She was crying, but she whispered, "Yes." Orrin stood frozen, his mouth hanging open. Vagid looked stunned, but he nodded.

He looked down at the thing, then at his hands. He was crying and smiling, like it was the best day of his life. He raised a hand toward us.

Then he faded away.

Then I remembered what Tired Guy had said: *Be kind to it, if you can.*

*Was this what he meant?*

"Have you seen anything like this before?" I asked. I hoped they'd say yes. I knew they wouldn't.

"Never," Nara whispered.

Orrin was still frozen.

Vagid shook his head.

*I really need to talk to Tired Guy.*
