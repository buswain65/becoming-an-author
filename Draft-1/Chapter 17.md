# Chapter 17

The next morning, Plan B met at the cafe. I got there just as Orrin did.

"I went through the notes again last night and discovered something," I said.

"Oh?" said Nara.

"Mmph," said Orrin, his mouth full of eggs.

Vagid nodded for me to continue.

"Remember the pink ink on that one page?" I asked.

"Yeah, and the strange writing in the margin," said Vagid.

"I was able to figure out what the writing said," I said. "Six months."

"Six months for what?" asked Orrin.

"Six months is how long it actually takes to prepare the ritual. Not nine months," I said.

Orrin started coughing and sputtering on his eggs, then said, "Six?!"

Vagid rubbed his chin while he thought, then said, "That's a much shorter window than we thought we had."

"Yes," I said. "I think we'll need to step up our search for more notes. And that's not the only problem. My guess is they started over the day they got rid of me."

Vagid counted on his fingers, then said, "You've been here around a month, so my best guess is we only have five months left."

"Five months!" squeaked Orrin, spraying bits of the eggs he still had in his mouth.

"Orrin, you shouldn't talk with food in your mouth," said Nara. "You'll make a mess."

Vagid looked around the table, then said flatly, "Too late."

I smiled a little at this amazing party I was lucky enough to be part of. Even with a world-ending deadline looming, they hadn't lost their cool. Well, except for Orrin.

We finished breakfast quickly, then went straight to the Guild. Vagid motioned for me to go ahead, so I joined Prue's line. In no time, we were at the counter, and I asked, "Hi, Prue. Are there any other haunted jobs, or anything involving desecrated places, that we can take?"

Prue sighed. "It's you again. You must have a real thing for haunted places."

"Um, yeah, sure," I said. There wasn't really anything else I could say to that.

Prue pulled out the ledger and flipped through it until she stopped on a page. Then she wrote something on a slip, folded it, and handed it to me.

"Thanks," I said, and headed over to where Plan B was waiting.

I unfolded the paper and read it to the team: "Maybe we should get dinner sometime. P."

*Wait, what?*

Vagid shook his head with a smirk.

Nara giggled.

Orrin whispered, "And I can't get a date with any of them."

"You forgot this," I heard Prue say from right behind me.

I turned and saw her holding another slip of paper out to me.

"Did she just hear you rea—" Orrin said loudly, until Nara's elbow silenced him.

"Uh. Thanks, Prue," I said.

Prue winked at me and walked back to the counter.

*What was going on today?*

"Evan, what does it say?" asked Vagid.

"Didn't you hear him when he read it a second ago?" asked Orrin. "I thought it was pretty clear."

"What does the *job slip* say?" Vagid said with a sigh, looking at Orrin.

I unfolded the second slip. This one was all business.

*Investigate blighted farm. Report findings. Two days south. Follow the road until everything's dead.*

The smile left my face.

"It's a blighted farmhouse, two days south of Millbrook," I said.

Nara said, "Like the chapel?"

"Maybe," I said. "But it could be different too. We should keep our guard up."

Orrin, for once, didn't say anything. He just nodded.

Vagid said, "Two days isn't too bad. It should be less if we take a coach." Then he looked at me with his eyebrows raised.

I smiled and said, "I'm broke until we get paid for the Hollin's Cross chapel."

"For the sake of the job, I think we can loan you the two rays," said Vagid.

"At an affordable interest rate," said Nara, smiling.

Orrin was trying to hold back a laugh. Vagid said, "I wonder if they give a discount if Evan rides with the gear?"

I deserved the teasing after the Hollin's Cross coach mix-up.

"That'll get us there in a day, which helps," said Vagid.

"Isn't Barley Ford the closest town?" asked Vagid.

"I hope there's an inn. Mave was amazing," said Orrin.

"If there's an inn, we can stay there. Otherwise, we'll be camping out," said Vagid.

The next morning, we boarded the coach to Barley Ford.

"Ah, these cushions are so comfortable," said Vagid, looking at me as I bounced around on the wooden seat.

"I could almost fall asleep it's so cushy," said Nara, smiling at me.

Orrin was actually asleep, so he just snored, which was somehow even more painful.

We didn't get to Barley Ford until close to dinnertime. As the coach pulled up outside the town walls, the teamster said, "You're lucky you're only going this far. I don't go any farther toward that farmstead than this."

He unloaded our gear without another word, then turned around and went back the way we'd come.

As we walked up to the town's earthen walls, the guard at the gate asked our business.

"We're adventurers on a job," said Orrin. "We're here to investigate the—"

"Farmhouse?" the guard finished with a sigh.

I looked at the rest of Plan B.

Vagid looked worried. "Have you had many adventurers come into town for this job?"

"Every few months," the guard said. "They all disappear. It gets quiet for a while, then more come."

"They never return?" asked Nara. "Could they have just left without letting you know?"

"We thought that too, until we went out to the farmhouse," said the guard. "Each of them had set up a camp. None of the camps had been disturbed. They were just left there."

"Creepy," whispered Orrin. "But it can't be any worse than that chapel in Hollin's Cross."

"I heard about that chapel," said the guard. "It sounded really scary. You went there and came back? Then you're probably fine." He paused, then said, "Probably."

I saw Vagid shoot a quick glance at Orrin and shake his head. Orrin had forgotten we were supposed to be keeping a low profile.

"Is there an inn where we can stay tonight?" I asked.

"Not an inn, but there's a chapel of Korrath. They have barracks-style rooms, and most are empty at the moment, so you could probably stay there. It's the largest building on the west side of town," said the guard.

"Thanks for the information," said Vagid. "We'll get out of your way."

"Good luck," said the guard. He didn't sound like he meant it.

Vagid led the way to the west side of town, toward the largest building.

"I've never been to a chapel of Korrath before. Is it very different from Amaranth's?" I asked.

"Korrath is the god of war, so his chapels are run more like a militia. They're very concerned with keeping order and punishing wrongdoers," said Orrin. "So not the opposite of Amaranth's, but close."

As we got closer to the doors, we could hear the priest's voice: "Korrath, shield of the faithful, sword against the wicked. Keep our walls strong and our hands steady. And if the world should again grow rotten past saving, grant us the strength to stand at the Great Reset, and the courage to begin anew."

*The Great Reset?* I wasn't sure what he meant, but I didn't like the end of that prayer. "The courage to begin anew" sounded a bit sinister.

*I'd have to ask Tired Guy.* Then I remembered where I was. Not in here.

Vagid led the way into the chapel of Korrath, where we saw an older man with salt-and-pepper hair kneeling before the altar. He wore light armor and was very fit, but not musclebound.

The priest stood up and, without turning around, said, "You are strangers in Barley Ford. What is your business in this chapel?"

"We are adventurers. We've accepted the Guild job to investigate the farmhouse near Barley Ford. We're looking for a place to stay the night and hoped we might find accommodations here," said Vagid.

"Normally, the barracks here would be filled with the acolytes of Korrath. But at the moment, there are none left," said the priest. "You may stay in the barracks, but keep your area tidy and clean."

"Thank you. We are members of the party named Plan B. I am Vagid, their leader," said Vagid. "The lady is Nara, the overly tall man with the bow is Orrin, and the other man is Evan."

"I am Warden Marek, the priest of Korrath here in Barley Ford," he said.

"May I ask you a question?" I said.

"Of course," said Warden Marek.

"You said there are none left. What happened to them?" I asked.

Marek paused for a moment, then said, "The farmhouse. Like everyone else."

"Oh," I said.

"What is it that you believe you can do at the farmhouse that all the others have failed to do?" asked Marek.

"We hope to discover why everyone has been disappearing, and find a solution," said Vagid.

"Hm," said Marek. "That seems... unlikely."

Marek showed us to the barracks, then quietly left. Inside were dozens of neatly arranged bunks, each bed made in what I could only describe as perfect military fashion.

*Someone had made every one of them. Someone was still making them.*

Plan B took beds in the same area, near the door. We said our good nights and lay down. Nara seemed to drift off quickly. Orrin kept shifting under his blanket, like a child being forced to go to bed early. Vagid lay on his back with his eyes closed. I took a moment to absorb everything we'd learned, then quietly wished I could talk to Tired Guy before we left for the farmhouse.

We woke up early and remade the beds we'd used. Nara used some focused wind magic to clear the dust from the floor and rafters.

We went to the chapel proper to thank Marek, but we couldn't find him. On a table with several seats around it, we found bowls of fruit set out for us. We ate in silence, then left for the farmhouse.

Prue's job slip said, "Follow the road until everything's dead," so that was what we did.

From the chapel, we followed the road. The farmland around us seemed healthy, if overgrown from being abandoned.

As we continued down the road, the farms went from overgrown to sparse, and finally to clearly dead. There was little vegetation left, and what we could see was a sickly brown.

As we came around a group of dead trees, we saw a lone farmhouse straight out of a pioneer painting. It was a single-story building, maybe about the size of my two-bedroom apartment back home. As we walked up, I could see a side entrance to a root cellar.

Like at the Hollin's Cross chapel, it was eerily quiet. No birds or insects made any noise here. The only sound was the wind rustling through the dead remains of the crops.

I was reminded of the time I went to a haunted house for Halloween with some friends from work. They'd arranged lights all over the outside to make it look creepy, but this farmhouse beat it without even trying.

We cautiously walked around the farmhouse, scouting for a campsite. We could see where several other groups had made camp. They had all set up in front of the house.&#32;

Some of the campsites were clearly older, with just the tent poles left. The cloth had rotted away long ago. Others were newer, and the tents looked like they'd just been set up. A few had no tents at all, only bedrolls laid out around the ashes of an old fire.

At one of the campsites, Nara spotted a pack with a sigil on it. "Korrath," she said quietly. "This camp must have been from the chapel in town. We should return these to Marek when we leave."

"I'm not sure we should join the other groups and camp near the front," said Vagid. "There's no sign these campsites were attacked, but it still seems like a bad idea."

"What about the root cellar?" I asked. "Could we set up camp near the entrance?"

"Orrin, scout around the root cellar entrance and make sure there isn't anything over there that we need to worry about," said Vagid.

Orrin walked off to scout and came back a few minutes later. "It seems clear."

We began setting up camp on the side of the farmhouse where the root cellar entrance was. As we passed near the cellar door, I caught a sweet, rotten smell coming from it.

*The farmer must have stored fruits and vegetables there before all of this happened.*

"I want to get into the farmhouse while we still have daylight," said Vagid after we'd set up camp.

"Agreed," said Orrin, Nara, and I.

Vagid motioned for Orrin and me to try the root cellar doors. We each took a handle and pulled. The sweet, dead smell grew much stronger as the doors swung open. The light showed steps leading down into the dark root cellar.

Vagid approached the cellar entrance and looked down. "Orrin, do you see any traps on the steps?"

"No. They look rickety and old, but there aren't any traps," said Orrin.

"Check it out," said Vagid.

Orrin took a step toward the cellar entrance, but I said, "I'll go," and stepped down onto the first step. I saw Vagid's expression cloud over at me volunteering without asking, but I was the most useless person on the team. I didn't want my friends getting hurt if I could help it.

The steps creaked loudly when I put my weight on the first one. Then I heard something, almost like breathing, but not healthy breathing. More like a wet wheeze.

When I put my weight on the second step, the whole staircase collapsed. I had a moment of weightlessness, then hit the sloped wall of the root cellar and slid the rest of the way down. I splashed into something at the bottom.

*Is it flooded? Thank God. Er, thank Tired Guy I left Xavier's notes in my pack.*

I stood up and looked around. It took my eyes a moment to adjust to the darkness. Then I saw what looked like a minivan-sized blob of Jell-O jiggling in the center of the root cellar.

*Is this a slime?*

"Evan? What happened? Are you okay?" Plan B yelled from the cellar door. The light dimmed as they crowded around the opening to see what was happening.

"It looks like a slime lives down here," I yelled back. "I'm just wet from landing in some water."

"Water?" said Vagid. "Evan, get out of there right now!"

"Grab my hand!" Orrin yelled. He lay flat at the entrance and reached down into the root cellar as far as he could.

I felt an itching where the water had splashed onto me as I reached up for Orrin's hand. He couldn't get a good grip on my wet hand, so he grabbed my armor instead. Then I heard Nara's voice, "Uplifting Gale!" and I was flung out of the root cellar and into the light.

I hit the ground hard and rolled a few times. Luckily, there were no rocks for me to hit this time.

The itching had turned into something more painful, like my skin was getting really hot. I sat up and looked at my hand. It had gotten puffy and red.

Nara, Orrin, and Vagid ran over to where I'd landed and started taking off my armor. "Are you trying to get yourself killed!?" yelled Orrin as he pulled off my leather chest piece. I glanced at my hand and saw little blisters forming, like a bad sunburn.

"Orrin, keep going. We'll get the water," said Vagid. Then he and Nara left.

I couldn't close my fingers because of the swelling, and the smell was making me lightheaded. I tried to stand up to take off my pants, but I only got as far as shifting my weight before I fell forward. After that, everything blurred until it started getting dark. I hadn't passed out, but the pain seemed to be everywhere, and it was especially bad in my nether regions.

Orrin kept talking to me while he took off the rest of my armor and clothes. "Damn it, Evan! Why did you have to do that?"

"It made sense," I said. "I'm the most useless one here. If someone had to go first—"

"Do you think that way about me?" asked Orrin.

"What? No. You're an amazing ranger!" I said.

"Then. Stop. Thinking. It. About. Yourself," said Orrin, poking me with each word. Each poke landed on a blister. I think it was on purpose.

Nara and Vagid came back with several buckets of water and used them to wash me off. "That water in the cellar must have had something in it," I said with a weak chuckle.

"It's the slime's digestive acid. It secretes it while it's digesting something, kind of like slobbering," said Vagid. "If there's that much down there, it must have been here for a really long time."

Once Plan B had finished taking care of me, they went about our nightly routine. Only this time, they were one short. Nara made the fire, and Vagid prepared vegetables while Orrin cut up some jerky we'd brought.

Nobody said much during dinner. Nara had to help me, since I still couldn't close my hands enough to pick up a spoon or hold a bowl without spilling stew all over my bandages. I felt like a mummy, only half wrapped.

After we finished dinner, Vagid said, "We need to take out the slime's core. Evan, did you see the core while you were down there?"

"I'm not sure what a slime core looks like. I've never seen one before," I said.

"It should look like a different-colored blob inside the slime's body," said Orrin. "Based on the size of that slime, it should be about as big as a large cabbage."

*Okay, so about the size of a basketball.* I still couldn't remember seeing anything like that. "No. I don't remember anything like that. It was hard to make out details. It just looked really big, and it was in the middle of the cellar."

"Okay, so we need a way to light up the cellar so we can find the core," said Orrin. "If someone ties a rope around me so I don't fall in, I can shoot some flame arrows into the cellar walls or shelves."

"If the notes are down there, shooting fire arrows into the wood might burn them. We need a way to light it up that doesn't burn anything," I said.

Then Vagid said, "The easiest thing is to lower lanterns in from the cellar entrance, or maybe through the floor above."

"Isn't the floor above where the other groups went in? Wouldn't that mean there's a trap there?" I asked. "I mean, all the campsites were near the front of the farmhouse."

"It does point to a trap, but I don't want to walk on the floor, just cut a hole through it. We can lean in through the windows and use hatchets," said Vagid. "For that matter, this is an old farmhouse. Just setting lanterns inside, above the cellar, should send some light down. The floorboards probably don't fit together very well."

After that, we all settled in for the night. I wouldn't be able to take my watch, which meant the rest of Plan B had to take longer ones.

I lay there thinking about what had happened. Volunteering without letting the party respond. Falling into the trap.

*Tired Guy?*

Nothing. Not even hold music.

Orrin's words kept coming back to me. *Then. Stop. Thinking. It. About. Yourself.*

Maybe I'd give that a try.
