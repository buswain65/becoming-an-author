# Chapter 9

The next morning, I greeted Halden on my way out of the chapel. "Good morning!"

"Morning," said Halden.

*Ever the stoic.*

"I'm meeting up with Plan B for our first Guild job together this morning," I offered.

"Have fun. Don't die. Don't forget your tab," said Halden, and it looked like he had a raised eyebrow.

*I**s** th**at** a joke?*

They'd told me to meet them at the café they always went to. It had outdoor seating, and as long as I walked from the chapel to the town center, I'd pass right by it. Sure enough, Nara, Vagid, and Orrin were waiting for me out front.

"Good morning!" I said as I arrived.

"Hey, Evan," said Orrin.

"Hello, Evan," Nara chimed in.

Vagid didn't say anything. He just nodded, his mouth full of toast.

I took the last seat at the table. I still didn't have any money, so I didn't order anything, but the food did look good.

"Evan, I know you don't have any magic, but do you have any other skills?" Vagid asked. "I mean, even Orrin can shoot a bow."

"Hey! I do a lot more than that!" Orrin protested.

"I don't think so, but I don't really know," I said. "I learned a lot of things in school and college, but most of it isn't useful in this world."

I shrugged. "Where I come from, nobody needs to know how to fight with a sword or use a bow. But we learn other things that are helpful. It's a 'you don't know what you don't know' problem."

"What's a 'call-edge'?" Nara asked as she pushed a slice of toast over to me.

"It's a place where they teach you a lot of useless information, which you then use to get a job you hate," I offered.

*Man, I've only been working for a little while, and I'm already so jaded.*

"Okay. We'll use this first job to find out what you're good at," said Vagid.

Vagid led us to the Guild. Instead of heading for Mara's line, we turned and walked over to a board covered in slips of paper.

"This is the Guild job board," Vagid explained. "Jobs can be anything from 'lost cat' to 'banshee busting.'"

*Wait. They have banshees here?*

"Banshee busting?" I said. "That sounds like something illegal."

"Each adventurer is expected to know their limits and not take jobs that are too difficult for themselves or their party," said Vagid. "For your first job, we want something with no chance of anyone getting hurt. It won't pay much, but it'll let us figure out how you fit into the party."

*Dang. So no ranks or shiny cards.*

"This job," Vagid said, taking down a slip, "is for clearing out some goblins that have been raiding a farmer's crops. It says there are four of them. This is perfect for our purposes."

"Goblins," I said. "You said no chance of getting hurt."

"For us," said Vagid.

*Great. So I'm the one who gets hurt.*

"We could handle that many goblins with just Nara and me," Orrin piped up.

Vagid took the job slip over to Mara's line to register for it, while Nara, Orrin, and I waited by the door.

"Here," said Orrin, handing me what looked like a Roman short sword. "This is one of my spares."

"Don't you use a bow?" I asked.

"Bows are a ranged weapon, and unless I can guarantee that everything I fight will be over two houses away, I need to be able to use a short-range weapon. Hence, I have short swords," Orrin said, in the tone you'd use with a toddler. "Plus. Girls love guys with swords!"

*Yeah. That tracks for him.*

As I took hold of the sword, Orrin said, "The pointy end goes toward the monsters."

"The most common mistake with this kind of sword is making wide, sweeping slashes," Orrin explained. "It's meant to stab. The edge is sharp, but it dulls quickly. The point still works even when it's dull."

"Also, remember it's not very long, so you'll need to get close to your target," said Vagid, rejoining us. "The farm isn't too far from town. We should be able to get there before noon."

We headed north out of town and followed the river. After several miles, we came to the farm that had posted the job. We didn't see anyone in the fields, so Vagid led us toward the barn.

The barn door was open, so we walked in. "Hello?" Vagid called. "We're here about the goblin request."

We all listened for a response, but none came.

"Let's try the house," said Vagid. "Maybe he's resting, or hurt and can't work today."

Vagid knocked on the door and waited. After a few seconds, the door opened, and a gray-haired man peered out cautiously. "What do you want?"

"We're here for the goblin-clearing job," said Vagid.

"Just the four of you?"

"Well, the job is for just four goblins, so we should be fine."

"Four? FOUR? I told them forty! Not four!" the old man screamed.

Right then, a group of little green people walked around the corner of the barn on the side opposite the field. They weren't that tall, maybe three feet at most, but that wasn't the problem. The problem was that there were a lot of them. Like forty or more.

We all saw the group at the same time. While my brain was still processing the horde, Vagid barked, "Orrin, roof, cover. Nara, wind shield. Evan, keep them from getting to Nara. Farmer, hide."

Still catching up, I asked, "What are you going to do?"

"Figure out how to get us all out of this mess," said Vagid.

From the roof, Orrin was already loosing arrows. Every goblin that edged forward got one at its feet, and the whole group hung back by the barn, chattering angrily.

I looked around at the fields. The empty, golden fields.

"Farmer! What are your crops?" I yelled.

"Wheat," the farmer replied from somewhere inside the house.

"Do you have any flour?"

"There are several sacks of flour in the house, ready for market. What are you planning to do, make bread?"

*Snarky, but reasonable.*

"No. I'm planning to kill all the goblins."

Vagid looked at me like I'd grown another head.

"Have you ever seen what happens when you fill the air in a small space with flour and light it?" I asked him.

"Yeah, it makes a big explosion. But how does that help us? The goblins are outside, by the barn."

"It'll take a bit of work, but here's my idea. If Nara can create a small tornado right where the goblins are, we can dump the flour sacks into it. Then Orrin shoots a fire arrow into the middle."

"But will it explode?"

"No, but it should still make a really big fireball. That should take care of the goblins."

Vagid thought for just a second, then nodded and began issuing orders to Nara and Orrin. I ran into the house to start moving the sacks of flour.

As I came out with the first sack of flour, I heard Nara say, "Little Cyclone," and saw exactly that appear right on top of the goblins.

*Mental note: do not make Nara mad. Ever.*

I stumbled toward the barn. The wind made it hard to keep my footing. I needed to keep the sacks far enough away that they wouldn't get pulled into the cyclone right away, so I left this one leaning against the barn and ran back for the next.

Vagid came running out of the house with another sack as I got close. "Put that with the other one!" I yelled as we passed.

I grabbed the third sack and took off for the barn. As I came out of the house, I could see Vagid struggling to hold down the two sacks against the wind. I made my way over to him. "Go get the next sack! I'll finish this!" I had to yell just to be heard over the roar of the cyclone. Vagid nodded and took off.

I watched him run toward the house, and that's when I noticed Nara. She was straining to keep the cyclone going. Sweat poured off her, and she was leaning heavily on her staff, one hand held out toward the cyclone.

*Crap. If Nara goes, we're in serious trouble.*

I drew the short sword and slashed the sacks open, letting the wind tear the flour away. One. Two. Three sacks.

With each sack, the cyclone became whiter and whiter.

Then I ran back toward the house, yelling, "Orrin! Shoot the fire arrow now!"

Orrin was quick. The arrow was in flight before I'd taken more than a few steps, and it flew straight into the heart of the cyclone.

*Orrin is an amazing archer.*

I was still running when I heard a *WHOOMPH* and felt heat on my back.

*Maybe it worked.*

Then came something I'd forgotten about: the shock wave. In movies, people always get thrown through the air by explosions. I'd always assumed that was exaggerated.

I was still running when I was lifted off my feet and started going a lot faster toward the house.

*This looks like it's going to hurt.*

Lucky for me, the building codes in this world don't require reinforced walls. Otherwise, instead of punching through the side of the house, I would have splatted against it.

I was groggy and disoriented, but I was pretty sure I was inside the house. It was hard to see, because one of my eyes wouldn't open properly. I squinted around the room with my one good eye. "Did it work?" I asked, not really expecting an answer.

"How the hell would I know? I've been hiding the whole time!" the farmer yelled. "You're the one who's all white like a ghost!"

Still looking around the room, I heard Orrin say, "Guess it worked." I turned and saw him and Vagid peering through the new window I'd installed for the farmer. Nara was leaning against Orrin, looking exhausted.

Vagid came in through the door, like a civilized person, and helped me outside. "Where are you hurt?" he asked.

"I'm bruised, but nothing's broken. I can't seem to open my left eye, for some reason."

"Looks like your tears mixed with the flour. It's just stuck."

Vagid guided me over to the well and splashed some water on my face, which freed my stuck eyelid.

*Yay! The last thing I need is a nickname like Mister Cyclops.*

Now that I could see again, I looked over as Vagid said, "Nara, you rest. Orrin, Evan, and I will go count the goblins. We need an exact count, so the Guild doesn't just pay us for four."

As we neared the barn, I noticed it now had a slight lean, away from where the fireball had gone off.

*Physics! Gotta love it!*

Goblins were scattered all over the place, and some were still walking around in circles, too dizzy to make a run for it. That meant we had to kill them.

"We need to collect the goblins' left ears to prove to the Guild that we killed them," Orrin said. "If any are still alive, just stab them in the neck or the heart."

*Oh, yay! Now I'm a murderer!*

The first goblin I came across was still alive, but knocked out. It took me several seconds to work up the courage to kill it. What finally did it was that the goblin woke up, looked at me, and immediately tried to bite me. At that point, it was just self-defense.

I got up to move to the next goblin, but I couldn't get my hands to stop shaking. "I'll just take a second to catch my breath," I said, then sat down much harder than I'd intended. I stayed there for a minute or more, just shaking.

"If it helps, they kill and eat the farmers around here," said Vagid. "For a goblin horde to get this big, they've killed dozens of people."

It did. A little.

Two hours later, we finished the count.

"Forty-seven goblins!" said Orrin. "There would have been more, but we couldn't find the left ears on some of them."

Vagid walked over to the farmer, holding out the job slip. "Can you sign that the job was completed?"

In a daze, the farmer signed it, muttering the same words over and over. "My house. My barn."

*He's fine.*

Then Vagid said, "Let's head back to town."

The trip back took longer, because we were all tired and needed to take breaks, but we finally reached Millbrook right around dinnertime.

Once we reached Millbrook, we went our separate ways, promising to meet at the café in the morning.

When I walked into the chapel, I ran into Halden. I was covered in flour and bruises, but all he said was, "I'll add it to the tab."

I went to my room, changed, and passed out on the bed.

The next morning, we met at the café for a quick breakfast and then headed to the Guild. Vagid got in Mara's line while the rest of us waited by the stairs. A little while later, Mara and Vagid came over and led us to Cuthbert's office.

"Boss, Plan B has stated that the goblin-clearing job had an error," said Mara meekly.

"A discrepancy?" said Cuthbert. "That seems unlikely. We have rigorous checks and balances to prevent this from happening."

*Says the guy who wrote "rocks" in his ledger as every cause of injury.*

Cuthbert pulled out his ledger and paged through it until he found the entry. He turned it around and tapped the page. "See? Goblin count: four."

"What was on the actual request form?" asked Vagid.

"Fetch the request form, Mara," said Cuthbert.

Mara left and came back a minute later with a crumpled piece of paper, which she handed to Cuthbert.

Cuthbert smoothed out the paper and examined it. "See? The number box just says four." He slid the paper across the desk to Vagid, who took a close look at it.

"Why does it look like a chunk of the form is missing?" asked Vagid.

We all crowded around the form. Sure enough, a small piece had been torn out right where the number field was. The four sat at the very edge of the tear.

"So it could have said forty, not four, but we can't know, because a piece is missing," said Vagid.

Cuthbert stroked his whiskers. "That seems to be a fair assessment. Mara! Bring me the update forms! We will need to get this straightened out immediately."

"While you take care of the paperwork, we'll drop off the ears to be recorded against the job," said Vagid.

"Yes, that is acceptable," said Cuthbert.

We left Cuthbert and Mara scribbling frantically behind a growing stack of forms and went downstairs to turn in the ears.

When we got downstairs, Vagid headed for Mara's line out of habit, stopped, and switched over to Prue's.

"Here are the ears for this job," said Vagid, handing over a large sack that squished disturbingly.

"I hate my job," was all Prue said before she took the sack and started counting.

She confirmed forty-seven goblin ears and noted it on the job form.

"Since there's an issue with the number on the request, it will take a while before we can pay you. Do you want to wait or come back?" Prue said.

"We'll come back tomorrow," said Vagid. Then he turned to the rest of us. "Let's go celebrate Evan's first job!"

*Yay me!*

Vagid led us back to the café, which worked out well, since I'd only had a piece of toast for breakfast and it was getting close to lunchtime.

*Man, being poor sucks.*

"Sorry, I still don't have any way to pay for this," I said.

"Don't worry about it, Boom Guy," said Orrin. "We can cover it!"

*Well, this nickname isn't as bad as some of the others.*

After we ordered, Vagid asked me, "How do you think you did on the job?"

*Huh? Isn't that for you guys to answer?*

I stared at my hands for a second, then said, "Things could have gone better. I forgot about the shock wave after the fireball. I didn't check with you before getting Orrin to shoot. I left Nara unguarded. The fireball tilted the barn. I punched a hole in the farmer's house with my face. And I used the sword to slash."

"I *saw* that," said Orrin.

"But at least no one died."

I was expecting to be yelled at. At every job I'd ever had, any mistake, no matter how minor, got you yelled at. What I didn't expect was laughter.

Nara burst out laughing. Orrin snorted into his drink. Vagid just smirked.

"What?" I asked.

Vagid leaned forward. "You saw the problem: forty-plus goblins. You came up with a plan, even if it was risky. You saw it through, even though it got you hurt. And why?"

"Well, the spell was really taking a toll on Nara, and if she went down, we were all going to get hurt. I couldn't let that happen!" I didn't start out yelling, but somehow by the end of my sentence I was yelling.

Vagid had been smirking the whole time. "Because your friends might get hurt, right?"

"Well, yeah," I mumbled.

"Then we only have one thing to say to you."

The pause was killing me.

*Say something, please.*

"Welcome to the party!" all of Plan B yelled at the same time.
