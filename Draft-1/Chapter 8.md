# Chapter 8

As we headed downstairs, Vagid said, "Nara, you collect the reward. Orrin and I will keep an eye on Evan."

This time, Mara's line was the shortest.

*Must be the adventurers' lunch hour.*

When Nara reached the front, Mara beamed. "Hi, Nara! It's so good to see you again!"

*Yeah, she definitely needs a **s**t**o**p* *condition.*

Vagid, Orrin, and I stood in silence. I just looked at the floor.

Nara came back with three small pouches. She handed one to Vagid and one to Orrin, and kept the third.

"Come on," said Vagid, leading the way out of the Guild.

"Can we have our talk by the river?" I asked.

Vagid sighed and rubbed his face with one hand. "Is there some reason an alehouse won't work?"

"No. I just like the sound of the river. And what I have to say isn't for anyone else's ears."

Vagid led us to the bridge over the river and took a narrow walkway I'd missed before. It led down to a small landing at the river's edge.

I walked over to stand closer to the water, while Plan B stayed by the steps.

I took a calming breath. *Please let me get through this, Tired Guy.*

I wasn't expecting an answer, but I got one.

"I can't see the future, remember? That one's yours," said a tired voice. "But I'm here."

I turned to face Plan B. "Have you ever heard of people who just appear out of nowhere, with way too much power?"

"You mean the Otherborn? We've all heard about them. I don't think any of us have actually met one, though," Vagid replied, looking at Orrin and Nara, who both shook their heads.

"Yeah. Well, you actually have. You just didn't know it," I said, looking at each of them.

*I really wish this was easier.*

For several seconds, no one said anything. Then Orrin burst out laughing. "You don't have any magic, so how could you be an Otherborn?"

Nara and Vagid were looking at me with suspicion and worry. I could practically hear them thinking it: *Maybe he really did hit his head on the rocks.*

I decided to try something. Switching to English, I said, "I hoped you would believe me, but it's a long way from the Nantahala and pizza to here."

Orrin stopped laughing. "What did you just say? Peet-sha?"

"Pizza. It's a food. From home." I paused. "Have you heard about it before?"

Orrin had gone pale. "Yeah. You said it while we were tying you to my back, after we found you!"

"I remember. I asked if anyone knew the language," Vagid said quietly.

I glanced at Nara, but I couldn't tell what she was thinking. The worry in her face seemed stronger. The suspicion seemed weaker.

I took a deep breath, hoping it would make this easier. Then I told them. Everything. The Nantahala. Falling asleep and waking up on a stone table. The torture, or at least some of it. I choked up more than once on that part, but I made it through.

I looked at each member of Plan B. Their faces gave nothing away, but at least I was telling my friends the truth now.

I steadied myself and began the last part. About Tired Guy. About our deal, what I had to do, and what would happen if I failed.

"That's ridiculous!" Orrin yelled. "Gods don't talk to people! They never have!"

I had no answer for that.

Then I remembered what Tired Guy had said to me in the river. "I'm not asking you to believe me today. Just to accept that I've been through some really weird things. I still want to be your friend, but like everyone, I have baggage."

*Lots and lots of baggage.*

After that, I didn't have anything left to say. I sat down at the edge of the landing and waited for the sound of their footsteps on the stairs, leaving for good.

"Your story is very hard to believe," I heard Vagid say calmly. "You're an Otherborn without magic. You talk to a god none of us have ever heard of. You were tortured—well, that part is clearly true. But the rest is just too unbelievable. So, no. We don't believe most of your story."

He paused. "But we believe in you."

I looked over at them.

Vagid didn't look worried or angry. He looked at ease. Orrin didn't look happy, but he wasn't angry either. Nara still looked anxious, but she also had a small smile.

"Besides," Vagid said, "Cuthbert's ledger already says Plan B has four members. It'd be a shame to make him fix it."

I smiled at that. "Yeah. That would be a real shame."
