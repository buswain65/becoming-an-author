# Chapter 22

The coach let us off on the road just before Lumenhold. The road on the gate side was paved and lit with lanterns. By the time we'd walked around the wall to Lower Lumenhold, it was dirt and darkness.

We found a small bar near the edge of town, and Vagid went in to ask where Tobin the cobbler's shop was. A short time later, he came back with a man just slightly taller than Vagid, wearing a stained work apron.

"This is Tobin the cobbler," said Vagid. "Tobin, this is my party, Plan B."

Tobin looked surprised to see us. "I wasn't expecting anyone to actually take the job," he said. "Usually it just gets worse and worse until several people die. Then the guards finally do something about it."

"My brother's a guard. He said you walked all the way to Millbrook to post it. That's a long way to go for nobody to come," said Vagid.

"I was worried about my son," said Tobin. "He's only three summers old, and he was playing outside when one of them kobolds came around. My wife and I ran it off, but I don't know nothing about fighting, so I didn't follow it far. Just far enough to see the rest of them. I ran back to the house, and my wife and I worried about it all night. In the morning, we took all our savings, and I went to the Guild and used it to post the job."

Orrin looked at his feet. Nara looked worried. Vagid's expression hadn't changed.

After a moment, Vagid said, "Well, we've taken the job. Can you show us where their lair is?"

"Well, yeah, but can we do it in the morning? I don't want to get caught out there at night," said Tobin. "It ain't safe."

"Of course," said Nara. "Is there somewhere we can pay to stay the night? I don't think there's an inn here."

"Oh! You can stay in my workshop. It's just behind my house. It might smell a bit 'cause of the leather and stuff, but it's clean and has enough room for all of you," said Tobin.

"Thank you," said Vagid. "If it's all right, we'll rest tonight and head out to the lair in the morning."

Tobin led us to a small house, really more of a sturdy hut. Behind it was his workshop. It was small, like the house, but well built. It reminded me of a shed more than anything. A large shed, but still a shed.

"Is it okay if we start a fire outside the shed?" asked Orrin. "We can make a fire pit so it doesn't get out of hand, and we'll make sure to put it out before we turn in."

"Sure. There's already a small fire pit there, so's I can warm my hands in the winter," said Tobin.

We dropped our packs on the workshop floor. Then I gathered wood while Orrin disappeared into the surrounding trees to hunt for dinner. Vagid began prepping some vegetables, and Nara went off to fill a bucket with water.

"Can you give us any more information about the job?" asked Vagid. "We can listen while we work."

"Uh, sure. It started a few months ago. Small things went missing. A mallet I use for my work. A shovel I left by the shed one night. Some clothes that were hanging out to dry. Then cats and dogs from around the village started disappearing. The last thing was right before I put in the job request. My son Ronar was playing outside my workshop while I was working. I heard him talking to someone, and when I went out to see who, there was a kobold standing there."

"Did the kobold say anything?" asked Vagid.

"We don't know. Ronar only said the lizard was nice," said Tobin. "But he says everything's nice."

"I caught dinner," said Orrin from right behind me.

"Jee-ah!" I said, trying not to look like I'd been about to climb a tree to get away.

Orrin had caught three rabbits, more than we usually had for dinner. He must have felt bad about his comments on the pay, because the extra was clearly for Tobin's family. He skinned the rabbits and prepped them for a stew. Vagid had cut up a lot more vegetables than we normally ate, too, and from somewhere he brought out a bigger pot. Nobody said anything about it. The pay was low, but it was everything Tobin's family had. Dinner was something simple we could give back. *Rabbit stew. Again. For once, I **didn't mind**.*

"Anything happening in the Upper?" asked Vagid. "My brother doesn't tell me anything."

"Cook's been losing cheese and candles for months, even a whole ham. Let a girl go over it. Said she must've been sneaking it home," said Tobin. "Guards on the bottom cells say there's scratching in the walls at night. Half of 'em think it's rats. The other half think it's ghosts. Nobody wants the night shift down there."

"I can't blame them," I said. "It could be anything."

*Or something with a shovel.*

When the stew was ready, Vagid said, "Tobin, we seem to have made too much stew. Can you do me a favor and take the extra off our hands? We don't really have a good way to take it with us."

Tobin looked at the pot, then at Vagid. He smiled. "I'm sure my wife and Ronar will like it. Thank you."

Tobin took the stew in to his family, and we sat quietly for a little while.

"That gossip about the dungeon sounds promising," I said. "The kobolds might have found a way in. Or it could just be rats."

"Or a ghost," said Orrin. "Like Xavier."

"If it is, I hope he's as polite as Xavier," I said.

"Let's hope it's rats," said Vagid. "Rats don't need an exorcist."

"I'll use Search at the cave mouth tomorrow," said Nara. "If something's moving under that castle, I'll know."

We set our normal watches, with mine first. With nothing to do during my watch, I looked around the workshop. Rows of boots from the castle guards lined every wall, each pair with a scrap of leather tied on and a name scratched into it. Most were caked in ordinary road mud.

A few were different: damp, crusted with a reddish clay that looked nothing like the dirt outside.

"Those are from the guards on the bottom cells," Tobin had told us. "Always that red muck. They track it everywhere."

I checked the name tags to see if Dorran's boots were here, but I couldn't find them.

Once my watch was done, I woke Nara and slept until morning.

In the morning, we packed up our gear and had a light breakfast of bread and cheese while we waited for Tobin.

When Tobin arrived, he led us up the hill toward the stretch of castle wall above the village. Before long, I could see a cave entrance ahead of us.

"This is as far as I go," said Tobin. "It's just too dangerous for someone who doesn't know how to fight."

"Agreed, and thank you, Tobin," said Vagid. "We hope to finish this today and come back with proof before dinner, but it could take longer. If it does, we'll camp in the cave. Either way, we'll stop by when we're done."

"Be careful," said Tobin. Then he turned and walked back to his workshop.

We walked the rest of the way to the cave entrance without spotting any kobolds.

"Search," said Nara. She was quiet for a moment. "There isn't anything nearby. The caves twist and turn, so it's hard to sense very far into them." She frowned. "But there's something deeper. Under the castle. It's faint."

"Let's get started, then," said Vagid. "Orrin, you lead. Nara, you're behind him. Evan, you're next, and I'll take the rear." He turned to Nara. "Can you give me a general idea of which branches of the cave to take? Otherwise we'll have to search every one."

"Yes. Once we reach each intersection, I think I can narrow down which branch to follow," said Nara.

We had only traveled a short distance into the cave when Orrin stopped. "Got a trap here," he said, barely above a whisper. "Let me disable it."

I leaned toward Vagid. "Why is Orrin whispering?" I asked, keeping my own voice low.

"Sound carries in a cave. We don't want the kobolds to know we're here," said Vagid.

Orrin knelt for a few seconds, then got up and continued down the cave. As I walked past the spot, I could see twine stretched across the cave floor, leading to a stack of stones. A tripwire, to topple the stones and warn the kobolds of intruders.

We went deeper into the cave, and Vagid lit a torch as the light from the entrance faded. The cave kept going downward, then made a sharp turn. Orrin motioned for us to wait while he checked around the turn for traps or kobolds. After a moment, he came back carrying something and held it out, and Vagid raised the torch so we could get a good look. It was a set of small bones. I couldn't tell what they belonged to, but each one was covered in strange scratches.

"What are those scratches from?" I asked.

Orrin paused. "Teeth," he said. "The kobolds ate whatever this was."

Vagid, Nara, and I looked at Orrin. "Do you know what the bones came from?" I asked.

"No. They're too small to tell. Probably a foot or a paw," said Orrin.

"Do either of you know?" I asked Nara and Vagid.

"I don't spend a lot of time staring at bones, unless they're part of dinner," said Nara.

"Your guess is as good as mine," said Vagid.

Vagid nodded for Orrin to continue, and Orrin motioned for us to follow him around the turn. Past it, the only light we had was the torch.

"Will the torch give us away?" I asked.

"Kobolds don't see well, so they use torches too. They probably won't notice our light until we're right on top of them," said Orrin.

We reached an intersection, and Vagid nodded to Nara. "Search," she said softly. "Take the right branch."

After walking for a minute or two, Orrin stopped and held up his fist, our party's signal to stop. He knelt and pointed to another tripwire. We waited for him to disarm it, then continued.

We came to another intersection. This time there were three directions to choose from. Vagid nodded to Nara. "Search," she said, then pointed to the center branch. Orrin started toward it, then stopped and held up his fist again.

He stepped to the side of the cave and knelt to pick something up. He brought it back and held it out in the torchlight for us to see. It looked like a small rib bone. Like the others, it had teeth marks on it. It was also broken in half.

"Marrow," said Orrin.

Vagid nodded to Orrin, and we continued. At the next intersection, Nara cast Search and pointed to the left branch.

Orrin started toward the branch, then held up his fist. We all stopped. He knelt and picked something up again, then walked back into the torchlight to get a better look. When he held it up, I thought at first it was a white stone, a little larger than a softball. Then he turned it over. It was a skull. A human child's skull, and it had been gnawed on.

Nara gasped and looked away. Orrin clenched his jaw. Vagid stared at the skull for a moment, then closed his eyes. I saw a tear roll down his cheek. My stomach turned, and I took a few deep breaths to keep from throwing up. Vagid opened his eyes and nodded to Orrin to continue.

*Three summers old. Ronar is just three summers old. This child can't be any older than him. Whose child was this?*

Orrin led us on, and at the next intersection, Nara sent us right. A few minutes later, Orrin held up his fist again and knelt. We all stopped. He turned toward the rest of us and motioned for us to kneel too.

We knelt. He held his hand to his ear, and we all listened. At first I didn't hear anything, then I heard a sound I couldn't make out. I tapped Orrin on the shoulder, pointed to my ear, and held my hand palm up. *I hear it, but what is it?*

Orrin pointed ahead, then pointed at his bow and pantomimed shooting an arrow. *Must be some of the kobolds.*

I nodded and quietly pulled out my short sword.

I looked back at Vagid and saw that his sword was already out. Nara had her staff in both hands.

Vagid pointed to Orrin, then in the direction of the noises, and pantomimed shooting a bow. *Go set up so you can snipe them.*

Then he pointed at Nara and moved his hands in small circles. *Be ready with your magic.*

Finally, he pointed to me, then himself, then motioned that we would be walking down toward the noise. We all nodded.

Orrin disappeared into the darkness without a sound. Vagid led me toward the noise. We tried to be quiet like Orrin, but we just didn't have the knack. We heard a grunt, then what sounded like a growl, the kind a dog makes when it's warning you to go away.

We stepped around a large boulder on the cave floor, right into a small group of kobolds. They were shorter than goblins, with scales instead of skin. Vagid and I tore into them. There was no room to swing, so I stabbed, the way Orrin had taught me. It wasn't quiet work, and more of them heard the noise and came running. They hit a wall of air about five feet away. *God bless you, Nara!*

I was watching the kobolds at the front get squished against the air wall when several of them seemed to sprout arrows from their bodies and heads. *Great shooting, Orrin!*

Vagid raised his arm and snapped his fingers. Nara dropped the air wall, and the remaining kobolds came forward in a rush. There were only five or six left, and Vagid and I killed them quickly.

The cave went quiet. I looked down at my sword. For once, I didn't feel sick about it. Then I heard a bird call and looked over to see Orrin walking up. *I didn't know he could do bird calls.*

Nara came up and sighed. Vagid looked at Orrin. Orrin just nodded, then pointed farther into the cave, and we all followed him. A little way on, the branch came to an end. A few torches were stuck into the ground, and there was a small fire for cooking.

On the cave floor were animal skins, broken bones, and torn cloth. Orrin walked over to the cave wall and pointed to something hanging there. It was a man's jacket, a bit colorful, but definitely a man's. Then he pointed to another thing hanging on the wall. It was a small shoe. It was hard to tell in the light, but it looked like it had a flower drawn on it.

Vagid pointed to Nara. "Check the area," he said.

"Search," said Nara. She took longer this time. "Nothing nearby. At least not back to the last intersection."

"Orrin, check the area for anything we can take back to town besides the jacket and the shoe," said Vagid. "Evan, you and I will collect the kobolds' left ears. Nara, be ready to shield us if anything else comes along."

We all set to our jobs and finished after several minutes. We had twenty-three left kobold ears.

"Twenty-three?" I asked Vagid.

"The job slip said about a dozen," said Vagid. "The others must have still been in the cave."

Orrin had found a larger human skull and some small trinkets. He wrapped the two skulls and the shoe in the jacket, then placed them carefully in his pack with the trinkets. Then we headed back to the last intersection.

At the intersection, Nara cast Search again and still didn't find anything. While we were standing there, Orrin knelt and pointed.

"Those are kobold tracks," said Orrin.

"Well, we did just kill a bunch of them," I said.

"But these tracks lead off down a branch we didn't take. There are a lot of them, overlapping, going there and back. What were they doing?" asked Orrin.

"We should find out," said Vagid. He nodded to Orrin.

Orrin led us down the other branch, which seemed to climb gradually upward. After a few minutes, I could smell something foul, like sweaty river gear left in a car in the hot sun for several weeks.

"Whew, that's ripe," I said.

We stopped for a moment to catch our breath, and I happened to look down.

"Red clay. Like on the guards' boots," I said, pointing.

"We should keep going," said Nara.

Orrin led us farther up the branch. After several minutes, we came to what I thought was another dead end. Once we got close, I could see what the kobolds had been doing. They had broken into Lumenhold. Through the hole in the cave wall, I could see a wooden door. It looked like the kobolds had broken into a small storage closet. Orrin stepped into the room and listened at the door for a second, then opened it and slipped into the space beyond. After a minute, he came back.

"It looks like we're in the dungeon. Probably the lowest level. This is a storage closet, which explains the torture implements hanging on the wall," said Orrin. Now that I looked around, the closet did seem to have a lot of pointy metal tools.&#32;

"Are any of the cells nearby occupied?" asked Vagid.

"No. They're all empty," said Orrin.

"Nara, use Search to make sure," said Vagid.

"Search," said Nara softly, her eyes closed. After a few seconds, she opened them. "There's no one on this level or the one directly above us. There are several people on the next two levels above that. That's as far as I can sense, though."

"What should we do now?" I asked.

"We head back to Millbrook and report the job to the Guild," said Vagid. "I don't like walking into an unknown dungeon without doing some investigating first."

"One job at a time," said Nara.

"We should probably hide the break in the storeroom wall, though," said Vagid. "Orrin, can you find some crates or something we can stack in front of the opening?"

"I saw some old crates stacked up farther down the hallway that should work," said Orrin.

"Was there a large animal skin in the kobold den?" I asked. "About as wide as the opening?"

"There probably is, but why?" asked Nara.

"If we stack the crates on it, we can use the skin to pull them up against the opening from this side," I said.

"Both of you go check," said Vagid.

Nara and I made our way back to the kobold den and searched through the pile of animal hides. We found two that looked like they'd work. When we got back, Vagid had already explained my plan to Orrin, and they were just waiting for us to bring the hides.

We laid the hides so they reached a couple of feet into the storeroom. Then Orrin and Vagid set the crates on the ends inside the storeroom, leaving themselves room to slip past.

Once they were out, we used the hides to pull the crates up against the hole.

I asked if we could take a short break before heading out. No one argued, so we all sat down to rest. I closed my eyes and leaned against the cave wall.

*Tired Guy?* I asked.

*Yes, Evan,* said Tired Guy.

*We found a way into Lumenhold,* I said. *And we found a little girl. The kobolds got to her first.*

*I'm sorry, Evan,* said Tired Guy. *And remember: past that wall, I can't see you.*

*I'll check in with you once we're back in Millbrook,* I said.

After our rest, Orrin led us back out of the cave. By the position of the sun, it was midafternoon.

Our plan was to head back to Millbrook, but first we had to tell Tobin.

The walk back to Tobin's workshop didn't take very long, and we got there while Tobin was cleaning a pair of guard boots.

"Tobin, we're back," said Vagid. "We took care of the kobolds."

"Really? That's great news!" said Tobin. "But you don't look happy. Why not?"

"We found some things we want to show you," said Vagid. He nodded to Orrin.

Orrin took out the jacket and laid it gently on Tobin's workshop table.

I heard Tobin suck in a breath. "Mardin," he said. "Oh no."

Orrin untied the jacket and opened it. The skulls and the shoe lay inside.

"No! Not Sava too!" Tobin fell to his knees and cried like his world had ended.

"Were they friends?" asked Vagid.

"Mardin was a traveling peddler. He was always friendly and happy," said Tobin. "He brought his daughter Sava with him this last time so I could fix her shoes. I drew the flower on them."

Orrin took out the trinkets and placed them on the jacket with the shoe. "That's all we found," he said.

Vagid and Nara helped Tobin back to his house while Orrin and I closed up his workshop. We heard a woman scream, and then there was a lot of crying. After a few minutes, Vagid and Nara came back out.

"Will they be okay?" I asked. "Is there anything we can do to help them?"

"We brought Mardin and Sava home. Now they need time to grieve," said Vagid.

Behind us, the crying went on. I thought about Ronar, safe somewhere in that little house, and about a pair of shoes with a flower drawn on them.
