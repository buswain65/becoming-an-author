# K1

The highest military rank in the Sunhallow Kingdom was general, and it was the only rank that couldn't be bought.

Garrick Ashford had worked his way up from recruit to soldier, then sergeant, lieutenant, captain, commander, and finally general. He hadn't bought a single one of them.

It had been a good career. Not stellar, perhaps, but noteworthy.

Lately, though, he'd been reading reports like this one: bodies turning up in farmers' nets on the Wendway, downstream of Millbrook.

It had been written by a constable who clearly didn't care whether the bodies kept floating downstream or not.

The last line read: "Matter investigated by the Millbrook Guild and closed. Cause: rocks."

"Rocks, indeed," Garrick said, and put the report down.

The Wendway ran down from Thornspire. He knew that better than the constable did.

There was a knock at the door.

"Enter," Garrick said.

The door opened, and a soldier stepped in.

"Sir!" the soldier said, snapping to attention.

"Report," said Garrick.

"The Chancellor has requested your presence at Lumenhold, General Ashford."

"Very well."

Garrick got up from his desk, buckled on his sword, fastened his cloak, and set out for Lumenhold.

Garrick arrived at Lumenhold a short time later and was shown into the chancellor's office.

The office was two stories tall, with books from floor to ceiling. A walkway ran along the walls halfway up, and a spiral staircase led up to it from the floor.

Between the shelves hung fifteen-foot-tall paintings of Silas Malgrave in various poses: on horseback, or seated on what looked like a throne. All of them were tastefully done, but on a scale that bordered on tacky.

Garrick took a seat facing the large, highly polished desk and waited for the chancellor.

After a few minutes, a side door opened, and Silas walked in. He was thin to the point of looking skeletal, nothing like the muscular man in the paintings.

"Thirteen was disappointing."

Garrick remembered a young man on the chamber floor, shouting the name of a kingdom that didn't exist. He had walked out when the knife came out.

"Yes, my lord."

"Four hundred sacrifices. Nine months of preparation. And we have nothing to show for it."

"My lord, has Pryce had any success advancing his students?"

Loric Pryce had led the summonings for as long as Garrick had served.

"Not notably," Silas said. "Pryce insists his students need practice. Apparently practice floats."

Garrick sat still, his hands resting on his knees. The only change was a tightening of his jaw.

"Yes, my lord."

"Garrick, I want the next ritual done as soon as possible. Get me more sacrifices. You have nine months. I want at least five hundred."

"My lord, that may be difficult. We may need to contact slave traders to meet that number."

"So you want more money? Very well. I will have the King sign a treasury writ for you. Do you know how much you will need?"

"My lord, will we want to limit our request to adult males?"

"Why would we? I want whoever they can get us in time."

Garrick paused for a moment. "Then, my lord, the price will depend on how many are adults, elderly, or children. I will reach out to the slave traders and make the arrangements. With such a large order, we should be able to get a better price. I believe that no more than two suns per slave would be sufficient."

"A thousand suns? Very well. I will have the writ delivered to you today."

"Yes, my lord."

"You may leave."

"Yes, my lord."

Garrick got up, quietly left the office, and returned to his own meager one. Everything was as he'd left it: the river report still squared to the edge of the desk, his chair exactly where he'd pushed it back.

He walked behind his desk and looked out the window toward the Wendway, then toward Thornspire.

There was a knock at the door.

"Enter," Garrick said.

The door opened, and a soldier came in, left a piece of paper on his desk, and went out again.

He picked it up and read it. It was the writ Silas had promised, signed by the king, who had probably never even read it.

Garrick turned back to the window and stared at Thornspire.

"Nine months. Again."
