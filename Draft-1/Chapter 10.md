# Chapter 10

*If there's one thing I've learned here, it's that I really hate toast. And being poor.*

We met early in the morning at the café for a quick bite to eat.

Right after breakfast, we went to the Guild. Nara, Orrin, and I waited off to the side while Vagid got in Mara's line. When he reached the front, there seemed to be a little drama, but it was worked out quickly. Vagid came over holding four pouches, and not tiny ones.

"What was up with you and Mara? Did you finally ask her out?" Orrin teased.

"No. They had the reward split three ways. I made sure they split it four ways instead, and had Mara make a note that from now on, all of Plan B's rewards get split that way," said Vagid.

"This seems like a lot of money," I said hesitantly.

"Yes, well, a goblin horde that size would normally take at least ten to fifteen adventurers to clear without anyone getting hurt or killed," said Vagid. "Since we did it with just four, each share is a lot bigger."

*I'm really a member of the party now.*

"Do you think I could afford a sword of my own now? And maybe some light armor?" I asked.

"Well, it's not as much as you'd think. Armor and weapons are expensive," said Orrin. "But we should be able to find you some gear at a secondhand shop here in town. And you can keep the short sword you borrowed. Girls love guys with swords, remember?"

Orrin, Vagid, and Nara took me to a secondhand equipment shop, where I got some light leather armor that was only slightly used. The shopkeeper swore the previous owner hadn't died in it. I chose to believe him. The armor left me with enough money for a week of meals, and not much more.

*Definitely more jobs in our future.*

\* \* \*

The next job we took was at a mine at the foot of a mountain called Thornspire. I didn't know where that was, but it seemed simple enough. The request said "Haunted Mine Shaft" and asked us to "get rid of the ghost."

After picking up some basic camping gear, we set off. It took two days of walking upriver to reach the mine. Vagid found the foreman and got the details of the "haunting," along with the names of the survivors.

We tracked down the survivor, a kid, really. "Rory and I were working in Shaft Five," he said. "It was a normal morning. Rory was playing with a coin from his first wages, and he dropped it into a crack in the floor. He had to lie down to reach in after it. But once he lay down, he stopped moving. I asked him what was wrong, but he never answered." He swallowed. "He was dead."

The story sounded very familiar. I'd once watched a Discovery Channel show about carbon dioxide in mines, and these were exactly the same symptoms.

"I think we have enough information. Let's go to the mine," I suggested.

"Don't we need something to repel the ghost?" asked Orrin.

"There is no ghost, and I can prove it."

"Okay. I can't wait to see this," said Orrin.

"What tools do you need?" asked Vagid.

"Just two things: a pair of torches, and a mouse in a small cage," I said.

"That shouldn't be a problem, but it's a really odd list," said Vagid.

"Just wait. You'll see."

We went back to the mine and asked the foreman and a few of the miners to come with us to Shaft Five. It took a lot of convincing, but they finally agreed. Once we were down in the shaft, the foreman showed me where Rory had been found. I turned to face him and the miners.

"Have you ever run into bad air in a shaft?" I started.

"Yeah. If we hit a pocket of bad air, it can catch fire or explode," said the foreman.

"Right. So you already know air can be bad even when you can't see it. This is the same thing, just a different kind of bad air. Let me show you."

I lit one of the torches and held it out in front of me, like I was pointing at something. Then I slowly crouched, keeping the torch as level as I could. Nothing happened. The foreman crossed his arms. One of the miners snorted.

*Okay. Lower. It has to be lower.*

I crouched until I was nearly sitting on my heels and eased the torch down toward the crack. When it was about a hand's width from the floor, it suddenly went out.

"This is where the gas starts," I said. "You can't smell it, and breathing it will kill you."

"If we can't smell it, and it kills us, how are we supposed to know it's there?" the foreman demanded.

"Let me show you." I held up the small cage so the foreman and miners could see that the mouse was very much alive. Then I crouched again, slowly lowering the cage. Once it was about a hand's width from the floor, the mouse keeled over. I lifted the cage. A few seconds later, it twitched, then staggered back to its feet.

Nara stepped forward, gripping her staff. "If it's just bad air, I can blow it out of the shaft."

"That would work," I said, "but only for a little while. The gas is seeping up out of the rock. As soon as you stop, it'll start filling the crack again. We need something that lasts."

"There are three things you can do," I said. "First, every group of miners should take a mouse with them. If the mouse keels over, leave immediately. Second, no one lies down in a shaft or puts their head near the floor. Third, keep the air moving with fans or bellows. Do all three, and no one else has to die."

"So a mouse, no lying down, and moving air keeps the ghosts away?" asked the foreman. Behind him, the miners nodded seriously.

I sighed. *Well, if it keeps them alive, who cares what they think is doing it?*

"Yes. That keeps the ghosts away."

When we left, the mouse had a name, a bigger cage, and its own shelf in Shaft Five.

\* \* \*

Next was a suspected theft at a merchant's shop in town. The job came to us through Rafe, Orrin's father, who was friends with the merchant. Vagid only knew the merchant by reputation, so Rafe came along to introduce us. The merchant said he was losing money and was sure someone was stealing from him. It had been going on for over a year, and if it didn't stop soon, he'd have to start letting workers go. We asked how he thought the theft was happening, but none of his ideas seemed possible.

"Maybe we can find proof in the ledger," I said. "Can I borrow it to go through the entries?"

"Not the current one, since we're still using it. But you can borrow last year's."

"Great. I'll also need a price list for everything you sell."

"Done!"

We took the ledger and the price list back to the chapel, and I started going through them. On the first day, I found what looked like massive theft, until I realized there were twelve sparks to a ray, not ten. I had to start over. Even in a fantasy world, my first bug was my own.

By the end of the second day, I had the answer. One assistant always recorded bulk sales at the wrong price. Say an inn ordered two hundred plates that were supposed to cost six sparks each. The assistant charged the inn six sparks, wrote down five in the ledger, and pocketed the difference. It wasn't much each time, but it adds up over, say, a year.

*Ohhh! I'm debugging. In a fantasy world. With a quill. This is so awesome!*

Once I knew how the theft was happening, the next step was figuring out why. Just because someone steals from a business doesn't mean they're evil. There could be extenuating circumstances, and they deserved a chance to explain.

We talked through our options, then asked Rafe and Lida what they thought.

It was Lida who had the best suggestion: "Ask him why before you tell anyone else." So, as a party, that's what we decided to do.

We found out where the assistant lived and when he worked. The next day, we knocked on his door and explained who we were and the job we were doing for the merchant.

"Why're you here, then?" he asked nervously.

Vagid looked at me and nodded.

"Because I know you're the one taking the money," I said. "And how you're doing it."

His face went white.

"B-b-but—" he stammered.

"What I want to know is why. You aren't taking a lot, but over time, it's starting to hurt the business. So why are you doing it?"

"Because of Marth. She took ill, and I couldn't afford the healing. I couldn't let her die. I just needed a way to pay for it. Please! Please don't turn me in to the constable! They'll send me off to prison, and Marth will die!"

"And Marth is?"

"My wife. She's all I have left."

*Plan B had paid for mine. Nobody had paid for hers.*

"Okay. I think I can see a way to work this out without involving the constable," I said. "But I need to run the idea past the merchant first, so don't do anything rash until you hear from us."

We left the assistant's house, and Nara asked, "How are you going to fix this without involving the constable?"

"Where I come from, this kind of thing happens too," I said. "The assistant is sorry, which helps a lot. Now we need to help the merchant see all of his choices."

"What choices does he have?"

"I see two. One, he fires the assistant. Then the assistant can't pay for the healing, and he'll probably turn to worse ways of getting the money."

"And the other choice?"

"Promote him."

"Promote him? That sounds crazy!"

"Back home, companies hire the people who break into their systems to keep everyone else out."

"Your world is very strange," said Nara.

"It's just a different way of looking at the problem. If he's promoted to catch anyone who tries this, the theft stops, the merchant gets someone who knows every trick, and the assistant can afford Marth's healing."

"Hmm."

"In the long run, promoting him will cost less than the theft, or than letting workers go."

We explained our idea to Rafe and Lida, and I went over all the pros and cons. In the end, they both agreed it was an unusual solution, but the best one.

The next day, Rafe and Lida went with us to the merchant's office. We laid out the same proposal, talked it through, and smoothed over any concerns. The merchant was willing to try it.

*Another Guild job completed. And nobody punched a hole in anything with their face.*

\* \* \*

Our next job wasn't a Guild job at all. It was a rescue. We were walking through town near the bridge when a woman screamed, "He fell in! Someone help him!"

We ran to the bridge and saw a small boy in the river, trying to swim back upstream. Orrin started stripping off his armor and weapons. "I'll go!"

"Hang on a second," I said. "Does the river pass any other bridges downstream? Anywhere we can get ahead of him?"

"The next bridge is two streets over," Vagid said quickly.

"Keep your feet downstream and lie on your back!" I yelled to the boy, and hoped he could hear me.

"Lead the way! Quick!" I said to Vagid.

As we ran, I looked for rope and saw some lying on a cart beside the road. I grabbed it without stopping. "Sorry! It's an emergency!"

We made it to the next bridge. I could hear the boy crying, still upstream. Luckily, there was another set of stairs leading down to a landing. The boy's cries were getting weaker. I made a snap decision, tied the rope around my chest, under my arms, and handed the other end to the others.

"No matter what, do not let go of this line. Let out slack until I yell 'Hold!'"

When the boy was about fifty feet upstream, I jumped in, swimming just hard enough against the current to slow myself down so he could reach me. As soon as he did, I wrapped my arms and legs around him and yelled, "HOLD!"

Suddenly, we stopped moving downstream. Then the rope jerked, and we slid another few feet. Somewhere above the roar of the water, I heard Orrin yell. The line went tight again and held. Now the challenge was to keep from being pulled under while the current swung us toward the shore.

Holding on to the boy, we slowly started to swing toward the shore. Water sprayed up around my head, but at least the boy and I could still breathe. We swung faster and faster, and through the spray, I saw the rocks piled along the shore.

*Crap.*

As we came toward the rocks, I twisted to keep the boy from being slammed into them. "AAAH!" I screamed when we hit.

Vagid and some townspeople picked their way carefully over the rocks and reached out. I handed the boy to Vagid and grabbed the hand of a man who looked like a blacksmith: huge and muscular.

*Well, hello, Mister Blacksmith.*

He yanked me completely out of the water with one arm.

"Evan, he's not moving," Vagid said.

I rushed over and checked the boy. He'd been crying not long ago, so he probably hadn't drowned. *Is his heart beating?* I pressed my ear to his chest and heard: *bump-bump, bump-bump.*

*Oh! Thank you, Tired Guy! Or whoever is listening.*

He hadn't drowned, his heart hadn't stopped, and he wasn't hypothermic. All good things. He was probably just exhausted from swimming upstream against the current. I called to the gathered townspeople, "I need dry towels, right now! As many as you can find!"

I started stripping off the kid's wet clothes. He needed to get out of them and into something dry. The towels arrived seconds later, and we wrapped him up like a mummy. Just then, a woman ran up, crying, and grabbed the boy. At the sound of his mother's voice, his eyes fluttered open. "Mommy," he said. Then he drifted back to sleep.

"He should be fine as long as you keep him dry and warm," I told her. "He's just really tired."

"Thank you! Thank you!" she cried. "I thought for sure he was going to end up like those bodies they keep finding in the nets."

*Keep finding? The Guild closed that job.*

Only then did I look back at my friends. Orrin was soaked to the waist, kneeling at the very edge of the landing, the rope still wrapped around his forearm. His palms were raw and bleeding.

"You didn't tell us to tie it to anything," Nara said quietly. "It nearly pulled us in. Orrin wouldn't let go."

*I hadn't told them to tie it off. I'd forgotten the most basic thing.*

Orrin just grinned at me. "Told you I'd go in."

Now that the boy was safe, I needed to get out of my own cold, wet clothes.

"Can you get me one of Halden's robes?" I asked Orrin. He turned and disappeared into the crowd. He hadn't even bothered to wrap his hands. Then I asked if I could borrow a room to change, and Mister Blacksmith took me to his shop. It didn't have a separate room, so I just changed in the main one. As I pulled off my shirt, I heard Mister Blacksmith mutter, "By Amaranth."

"What is it?" I asked.

"You've got a huge bruise on your back, and a lot of old scars. Were you a soldier or something?"

"Or something," I whispered. Then, louder, "No. Just lucky."

He studied me for a long moment, then tossed me another towel. "Name's Bram."

*Goodbye, Mister Blacksmith. Hello, Bram.*

I didn't have anything else to wear, so I just wrapped myself in towels until Orrin arrived with the tent-robe.

Back at the chapel, Halden had me drop the robe, then took one look at my back. "What happened?"

"Rocks."

He raised an eyebrow.

*For once, it really was rocks.*

"I'll add it to the tab."
