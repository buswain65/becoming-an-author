# Chapter 18

In the morning, I woke up still half mummified in bandages and very tired. I could hobble around, but walking took effort. To avoid the pain, I walked with my feet wide apart, like I'd been riding a horse all day.

After a light breakfast of bread and cheese, Vagid, Orrin, and Nara approached the porch that wrapped around the farmhouse. Vagid tested the wood to make sure it wasn't rotten, then stepped onto the porch. Orrin and Nara followed, testing each board before putting their full weight on it. I stayed by the fire and watched, which was about all I was good for right now.

Vagid leaned into one of the windows and said, "Well, not all of the parties that came here went in through the front door. It looks like one tried coming through this window. There's a hole in the floor a few steps in that probably goes into the cellar."

"Same for this window," said Nara. "The hole is several steps in, and there are bits of thread caught on the broken boards."

"Yep, same for this window," said Orrin.

Vagid took out the lantern he'd scavenged from one of the abandoned campsites and lit it. Then he tied a rope to the lantern's handle and lowered it through the hole.

"I can see the light through the hole over here, so it's working," said Nara. "But I can't see the slime from this angle."

"I'll tie off to that dead tree," said Orrin. "Then I'll go in and find the core."

"All the trees around here are dead. Will it hold?" asked Vagid.

"It's big enough that it won't budge," said Orrin.

Orrin tied a rope around the tree, secured the other end around his upper chest, and slipped into the house through the window. I heard the floorboards creak loudly, but nothing crashed.

"How's it going?" I asked.

"I can see the slime, but not the core. We need to get it closer to this side of the cellar," Orrin said.

Then I heard scuffling from Orrin's window.

"Orrin? Is everything okay?" I asked.

"Yeah. Just getting out the leftover jerky," said Orrin.

"I don't think this is a good time for a snack," I said. "Maybe later?"

"It's for the slime," said Orrin. "Bait."

Through all of this, I could barely hear the slime's wet breathing. Now it sped up and grew louder near Orrin's window.

"There it is," said Orrin.

His bow thwanged, and a wet, wheezing scream rose from the cellar. It was loud at first, then slowly died away. Then silence.

"Orrin?" I asked. *Please be okay!*

"Yeah," said Orrin. "The core's cracked. Slime looks dead, and its body's dissolving. Ugh. That stinks."

"Do we still get paid if the core is cracked?" I asked. "Didn't they want the core brought back as proof?"

"Yes. The core is the proof, but we get paid whether it's in one piece or many," said Nara. "A slime core is the usual way to prove it was a slime and not a ghost or something worse. But a whole slime core is hard to come by."

"Orrin, what can you see from your position?" asked Vagid.

"There are big piles of armor around the sides of the cellar. Not sure how you missed those when you fell in, Evan," said Orrin.

"Probably because it wasn't a rock," said Nara with a chuckle.

*I** **w**a**s** **n**e**v**e**r** **l**i**v**i**ng that d**own.*

Vagid actually laughed at that, then said, "Come on back out, Orrin. Is there anything we can do to get into the cellar faster?"

"What's keeping us out right now?" I asked. "The acid, right?"

"Correct," said Vagid. "We can't go down there until it stops burning."

"Well, what if we tossed in buckets of dirt from up here?" I asked. "Would that soak up enough of the acid for us to get down there?"

"Interesting idea," said Vagid. "But that would take all day. Most slimes dissolve when they die. Give it a couple of hours, and the slime and its acid will be gone on their own."

While we waited for the slime to dissolve, Orrin went out past the blight and caught a rabbit. Vagid made a stew, and Nara kept a light breeze blowing the stench of the cellar away from camp so we could eat lunch without gagging.

After a couple of hours, the slime was gone, and so was most of the acid. We found a ladder leaning against a shed behind the farmhouse, lowered it through the cellar doorway, and climbed down. It took me three times as long as anyone else, and I said several words my grandmother wouldn't have liked.

We took the lanterns down with us this time. Piles of armor lay around the edges of the cellar, near the walls. Most of it looked like common adventurer's gear, but some was clearly better quality and bore the same sigil of Korrath as the packs at the campsite.

*This is the acolytes' armor. We probably slept in their beds at the chapel.*

The core looked like a giant marble, webbed with cracks. When I was a kid, I boiled a marble and then dropped it into a cup of cold water. It didn't fall apart, but it cracked all the way through. The core looked like that.

We searched the armor piles and the shelves, but we didn't find any notes.

*If I were hiding notes in this cellar, where would I put them?*

It depended on whether the slime was part of the trap or had just found the cellar and moved in. If it was happenstance, the notes were probably buried under the floor and dissolved by now. But if the slime was part of the trap, they'd want the notes as far from the floor as they could get them.

Like the ceiling.

I started looking at the beams overhead. On the side opposite the cellar entrance, between two floor joists, was a wooden box. It had been nailed to the joists to keep it from moving.

I pointed out the box to the others. Vagid's plan was simple: he climbed onto Orrin's shoulders and pried the box loose from between the joists. Orrin's face suggested this was not the first time Vagid's plans had involved standing on him. Vagid handed the box down to Orrin, who passed it on to me.

With the box in my hands, it hit me. The Otherborn who built this trap had planned to kill whoever came looking for it. The thought made me dizzy.

Would I one day kill my friends to keep a secret?

"Evan?" asked Nara. "Is something wrong?"

"I... they planned to kill them..." I said, staring at the armor piles.

"Yes," said Vagid. "But that doesn't mean you're to blame."

"No... that kind of evil is... will I become like that? Have I already started?" I asked.

"No," said Orrin. "You would never become like that. You've always tried to protect others. Me, on the stairs. The boy who fell into the river. Xavier, when Halden was going to exorcise him."

I took a deep breath. "Thanks."

I opened the box and found a bundle of papers, the top page covered in handwriting I didn't need my gift to read. English. I didn't want to go through them in the cellar, so I handed them to Nara to hold. We still needed to gather up the acolytes' armor and carry it up to our campsite.

Once Nara and I got back to the surface, she changed some of my bandages while Orrin passed the armor up to Vagid, who set it aside.

Afterward, I suggested we build a drag litter for the armor. Nara pointed out that one of the abandoned campsites had a pull cart we could use instead.

Nara and I loaded the cart with the armor Vagid had set aside.

"Keep the armor in the groups I set out," said Vagid. "I've been sorting it so all the pieces from the same spot stay together. The slime wouldn't have had any reason to move them once it was finished with the people inside, so each pile probably came from a single acolyte."

We set out for Barley Ford by mid-afternoon. It was a short walk, but it took us twice as long as the trip out, because all I could manage was a hobble, and I needed a lot of rest stops.

We arrived back at the chapel of Korrath in Barley Ford near dinnertime.

As the cart creaked up to the chapel, the door opened and Marek stepped out.

"You're back," said Marek. "What did you find out?"

"There was a large slime in the farmhouse cellar. It had been there a long time. The floorboards above it were rotted through, but they hadn't collapsed yet. When people stepped on them, they fell into the cellar and couldn't get out before the slime reached them," said Vagid.

Marek paused, then his eyes went to the cart and the sigil of Korrath on the armor. He stared at it for a long time. "Thank you," said Marek.

Marek wiped his face, then looked at me. "I guess you figured out it was a slime," said Marek. "The hard way."

"Yeah," I said. "My first slime."

Marek raised his hand toward me and said, "Lesser Heal." His hand glowed briefly, and I felt better. Not great, but better.

"Wow. Thank you," I said. "I didn't know priests of Korrath could heal."

"Healing is also part of war," said Marek, and left it at that.

For the first time since the cellar, I could stand without looking bowlegged.

Marek told us to go into the chapel and eat. He'd made a meager meal for himself, but we were welcome to use his kitchen to cook whatever we wanted. He pulled the cart around to the back of the chapel while we headed in.

I sat down in a chair, and Nara started changing my bandages. "You were very foolish," said Nara. "That one choice could have left you maimed or dead."

"I'm sorry. I was trying to protect my friends," I said.

Nara didn't answer. She tied off the next bandage a little too tightly.

"Ow," I said. "Point taken."

Vagid and Orrin were busy cooking dinner. Orrin had apparently found another rabbit somewhere, and Vagid had cut it up and started a stew.

"Oh. This will work nicely," said Vagid, rummaging through Marek's spices.

A short time later, Marek joined us at the kitchen table. He looked me over, then said, "Your clothes are having trouble keeping you covered. My acolyte would not have minded you using these to keep a modest appearance." He handed me a set of clothes like his own, but in my size.

"I'm honored. Thank you," I said.

I headed back to the barracks to change. When I pulled on the shirt, I noticed the sleeves were worn soft at the elbows, and so were the knees of the pants. Someone had lived in these.

When I returned, the meal was ready, and everyone was waiting for me. I sat in an empty chair beside Orrin. Nara's pack sat by the door with the notes inside. I didn't look at it more than five or six times.

Marek raised his hands in prayer and said, "Korrath, we eat so we may stand. We stand so others need not fall. Count the fallen among your own."

He lowered his hands, filled his bowl with stew, and began eating.

I took a bite and stopped chewing. The flavor was so much deeper and richer than at lunch. "Vagid, this stew is great!" I said.

"Thanks to Marek's spice rack, I was able to make it better," said Vagid. "You have quite a collection, Marek."

"I grow them behind the chapel, and I grew up in a household that used them often," said Marek.

Marek ate quickly and went back out to the armor.

"Evan," said Vagid. "We need to talk about this job and what happened."

"I know I slowed us down on the way back," I said quickly. "And I used up a lot of our bandages. And I couldn't help with the watches last night. You can split up my share of the pay to cover everything I used."

Vagid sat there a moment, staring at me. Then he turned to Orrin and Nara. "He really doesn't get it, does he?"

"No," said Nara. "Not at all."

"Nope," said Orrin, shaking his head.

Vagid sighed. "Do you understand why we're upset with you?"

"I just said. Slowing us down, the bandages, the watches. I'm sure there are other things too, and I'm happy to make up for those," I said. "Just... please don't kick me out of the party."

Vagid's weariness vanished. "How dare you!" He slammed his fist down on the table, then took a second and a calming breath. "What would you have said if Nara had volunteered to go into the cellar, then just started in?"

"I would have stopped her, of course," I said.

"Why?" asked Vagid.

"What do you mean, why?" I asked.

"Why would you have stopped her?" asked Vagid.

"It was dangerous!" I said.

"We didn't know how dangerous at that point," said Vagid. "So why would you have stopped her?"

"She could have been hurt, or kil—" I stopped.

Nobody said anything. They didn't need to.
