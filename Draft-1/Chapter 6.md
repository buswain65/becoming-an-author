# Chapter 6

Over the next few days, while my legs remembered how to be legs, I asked Halden everything I could think of.

"Who are the gods?"

"There are three. Korrath, the god of war. Sallow, the god of famine. And Amaranth, the god of plenty."

*Hmm. No mention of Tired Guy. Which makes sense, really. He did say he didn't have any believers.*

"What drew you to Amaranth?"

"In times of war, people are drawn to Korrath. In times of famine, they're drawn to Sallow. I liked the idea of being thankful for a good harvest, and for the other things that come in times of plenty. So I chose to follow Amaranth."

"How far away is the river?"

"About half a league."

*League? Like a football league? No, that can't be it. What the heck is a league?*

"Can you do something to let me know when you're joking? Wink, or raise an eyebrow or something?"

Halden raised an eyebrow.

*Damn it! I still can't tell.*

It took about a week before I was strong enough to walk around the chapel. When I finally made it all the way around without stopping or falling over, I returned to my room. Halden had already laid out a clean tent-robe for me, and a pair of sandals. On top of the robe was a short note:

*I'll keep your tab open.*

I got dressed and headed for the chapel door. I grabbed the handle and pulled it open, and found Orrin standing there with his arm out, reaching for it. Nara stood beside him. No sign of Vagid, though.

"Hi, Orrin. Hi, Nara. I think I'm well enough to get out of Halden's hair."

"Morning, Naked G—" Nara elbowed him. "—Evan," Orrin finished, rubbing his side. "Perfect timing. Vagid asked Nara and me to come by and bring you over to his parents' house. I think he wants to get you something to wear besides Halden's tent."

With Orrin leading the way, we headed out to Vagid's parents' house, only a few blocks away. As we walked up, a young guard was leaving the house, and an older couple waved to him from the doorway as he went.

My shoulders tensed at the uniform before my brain caught up. *Different guard. Different day. Probably Vagid's brother and parents.*

"Orrin! Nara! It's so good to see you again! You just missed Dorran. He has to get ready for an inspection. The Chancellor's people check the guards' loyalty every season now," said the guy who looked like an older version of Vagid. "And who's your friend?"

"Oh! You must be that River Fellow Vagid mentioned," said the woman beside him. "You really should be more careful not to fall into the river."

*River Fellow. Much better than Naked Guy.*

"Hello, my name is Evan. It's very nice to meet you."

"How polite!" said the woman, smiling.

"Well, hello, Evan! I'm Faren, and this is my wife, Ruta," said Faren. "It's always nice to meet more of Vagid's friends."

"Mom, Dad, where are those old clothes of mine?" Vagid said as he walked out of the house.

"They're in the attic, where they've been since you abandoned your poor mother," said Ruta, trying to sound pitiful.

Vagid stopped in his tracks. "I moved four houses over. I'm literally your neighbor."

I looked at the house. It was one story tall and about the size of a two-car garage. I looked at Ruta. "There's an attic?"

"Of course there is! And it's very spacious, too," Ruta beamed.

*Spacious? Maybe that's one of those words that doesn't translate right.*

Vagid led us into the garage-house, to a hallway just off the kitchen. In the ceiling was a small panel. Faren brought out a ladder and used the top of it to push the panel up, then let the ladder lean against the edge of the opening, propping the panel open.

I looked at the panel. Then at Nara, Orrin, and Vagid. Of all of us, only Vagid and Nara had a chance of fitting through the opening. Orrin and I might get one shoulder through, but then we'd be stuck.

Ruta came up behind me and said proudly, "Spacious!"

"Give me a minute," Vagid grunted as he climbed the ladder and squeezed himself through the opening.

*Yep. Definitely no chance for Tall Guy or me. Plus, I'm wearing a tent-robe with nothing on underneath. The last thing I need is for Vagid's parents to think I'm a pervert.*

After some grunting and the sound of boxes being shoved around, a few pairs of pants and some shirts came tumbling down through the opening.

"These are the biggest clothes I could find," Vagid called down.

I held the pants up to my waist. The waist looked like it would fit. Then I heard Nara stifle a laugh, looked down, and saw they were about a foot too short.

*Okay. So the pants are a no-go. But maybe the shirt will work.*

I held up the shirt and lined up one shoulder seam with my shoulder.

"Pfft!" came from Orrin.

I looked over. The other shoulder seam stopped about six inches short of mine.

Then Vagid poked his head down through the opening. "So? How do they fit?"

We all looked at each other. Finally, Ruta said, "They were close, but Evan is such a strapping lad, they just won't fit."

"Sorry, Vagid. I really appreciate you checking for me, though," I said, keeping a perfectly straight face.

Vagid sighed. "It was worth a try."

Vagid took the clothes back into the attic's spacious depths, then squeezed out and climbed down the ladder.

"We should check my parents' house too," Orrin chimed in. "I'm sure they have something that'll fit. If not mine, then maybe my dad's."

"Okay. Mom, Dad, we're heading over to Orrin's parents' house to look," said Vagid. Ruta opened her mouth, and he added quickly, "And Nara's parents are having Evan over for dinner tonight. Let's not overwhelm him."

"Now, honey. They're young and have their own lives. They'll come around for dinner when they can fit it in," said Faren in a very diplomatic tone.

*Fit. Everyone in this house has a complicated relationship with that word.*

Vagid gave his mother and father quick hugs, and then we headed over to Orrin's parents' house. They lived closer to the town center, in a narrow, two-story house. The first thing I thought of when I saw it was a row home in Pennsylvania. Katie is from Pennsylvania. Once, when we visited, she took me on a tour of her little hometown.

Orrin opened the door and yelled, "Mom! Dad! It's me and the party. I came by to look for some of my old clothes."

*The party. Like a D&D party? Please tell me I'm not the bard.*

I heard chairs scraping across the floor and footsteps, and then a man came around the corner. He was broad and dark-haired, with a thick beard, and he looked too young to have a grown son. He looked nothing like Orrin.

*Uncle? Neighbor? Very confident burglar?*

Then a woman came around the corner behind him, and there was Orrin's face, just older and prettier. It was like looking at one of those age-progression photos on TV.

"All of your old clothes are in your old room. Just don't leave it a mess like last time," said Orrin's mother as she walked up.

As Orrin headed for the stairs, I saw a flash of movement, and something slammed into his waist.

"OOORRRRINNNN!!!" screamed the tiny terror.

Orrin's mother sighed. "Katia, let your brother breathe."

For a moment, I froze. Her name. It sounded so close.

"Is everything okay?" Nara asked, looking concerned.

I realized my eyes were wide and my mouth was hanging open. I cleared my throat. "Yeah. She just surprised me."

*Someday that name won't hurt.*

"Is this the guy you found by the river?" asked Orrin's father.

"Yeah, this is him. Evan, this is my father, Rafe, and my mother, Lida."

"Hi. It's very nice to meet you both."

"OORRIIN!" whined Katia.

"Oh. And this little goblin is Katia," Orrin said as he picked her up and began tickling her.

Katia wriggled free from Orrin and hid behind Nara. Then she stared up at me.

"Who are you?" she asked.

"Hi. I'm Evan."

"OH! The River Guy!" she said loudly, clapping her hands.

*Word travels fast in Sunhallow.*

Orrin motioned for me to follow him up the stairs, and headed for the first door on the right. It was a nice, oversized closet of a room, with just enough space for a bed and a small desk.

*The textbook definition of cozy.*

Orrin opened a few boxes and dug out some pairs of his old pants that looked like they'd fit me, and several shirts that definitely would.

Orrin excused himself so I could try them on.

All of them fit. Some of the colors were a bit questionable, but anything was better than Halden's tent.

Finally, I could give the tent back. *If Halden doesn't want it, I'm sure I could donate it to a traveling circus.*

Holding on to the rest of the clothes, I headed downstairs. The first person to notice was Katia, who looked me over and said, "You're a lot more skinny now."

"Thank you, Katia."

Orrin went over to his parents and gave them both hugs before scooping up Katia and tickling her some more. Katia squealed with glee.

"Okay. Time to head out," Orrin said. "I'll drop by again when I get a chance."

"Thank you for the clothes," I said to Rafe and Lida. "I'll take good care of them."

"Better care than Orrin did, I hope," said Lida.

As we were heading out the door, I heard, "Bye, Evan!"

I turned around and saw Katia waving enthusiastically at me. I waved back, smiling. "Bye, Katia."

"We should head over to my parents' house too," Nara said quietly.

"No offense, Nara," I quipped, "but I'm not sure I'd look good in any of your hand-me-downs."

"Ugh! It's not for clothes. My parents want to have you over for dinner tonight."

Nara's parents lived a few streets past the town center, on the side opposite Orrin's parents' house. Nara hesitated at the first corner, and Vagid stepped ahead of her without a word, like he'd done it a hundred times. Nara fell in at the back of the group, next to me.

*Funny. It's her parents' house.*

"Though you might look good in one of my festival dresses from when I was little," Nara said, deadpan. She smiled, glancing at me.

"Bwahaha!" Orrin doubled over.

"Now that would be a sight to see," Vagid chuckled.

We arrived at a cute one-and-a-half-story cottage, like something out of an ad for living in New England. Vagid led us through the gate of a small picket fence, but instead of knocking on the front door, he took us around to the back. There, a large group sat around a table, talking.

A woman came out of the cottage as we approached the table. "Nara, love! You're early!"

"Hi, Mom."

"And you brought a handsome friend with you too!" said Nara's mother, looking me up and down like a side of beef. "I'm Isla! And you must be the wonderful Evan we've heard so much about."

"I'm handsome too, you know," Orrin muttered from the side.

"Of course you are, my dear," Isla said, giving Orrin a quick hug.

"Attention! Time for introductions," Isla announced. The table quieted down and turned toward her. "Everyone, this is Evan."

Then she added something that took me a second to process.

"Nara's boyfriend."

"Mom! He is not my boyfriend! We're just friends!"

The older gentleman at the table yelled, "BOYFRIEND?"

I could feel my face getting hot. Beside me, Nara was frantically explaining our relationship.

Behind me, Orrin and Vagid were openly laughing.

*How mature.*

Isla continued, "This is my mother and father, Fenna and Teodor."

"WHO IS THIS?" Teodor yelled.

"Nara's boyfriend," Fenna yelled back.

As Isla worked her way down the table, the man who was clearly Nara's father stared daggers at me, a deep frown on his face.

"This lovely girl is Lyra," said Isla.

"So very nice to meet you, Evan," Lyra said, with a sly look at Nara. "I'm Nara's older sister."

"Hi," I said, shaking her hand.

"This young rascal is Pip, Nara's little brother," Isla said, indicating a boy who looked to be about eleven or twelve.

"If you're dating Nara, you must be a really powerful mage! Show us your magic!" he begged.

"Hi. Sorry, I don't actually have any magical ability."

"Lame and boring," said the teenager sitting next to Pip.

"And this is Arlo, Nara's other brother," Isla continued.

"Finally, this is Nara's father, Rowan." Isla gestured to the man who had been glaring at me.

Suddenly he smiled, stood up, and shook my hand. "A non-mage boyfriend! That's wonderful news!"

*Um. Why is that wonderful news? And I'm not her boyfriend!*

"You have no idea how many 'powerful mages' Dad has chased off," Lyra murmured to me. "They all wanted Nara for her magic. You don't even have any."

"Come help me bring out dinner, girls," Isla said, and she, Lyra, and Nara went into the house.

Rowan steered me to a seat near his own.

"I'm not really Nara's boyfriend," I protested. "We only just met when they found me by the river."

Orrin and Vagid laughed some more as they sat down across from me.

"So," said Grandma Fenna, leaning across the table as if I hadn't said a word, "when's the wedding?"

"WEDDING?" Teodor bellowed.

Somewhere down the street, a dog started barking.

*I've been in this town barely two weeks, and I'm already engaged.*

Orrin, still laughing, actually fell out of his chair. Vagid just sat there smirking, waiting for my answer.

Right then, I heard "Eep!" and turned to see Nara standing in the doorway, holding a platter of what looked like pork.

Nara's eyes hardened as she stormed up to the table. She slammed the platter down in front of Rowan. "We are not dating! We are not engaged! We only met when Orrin, Vagid, and I found him naked on the riverbank!"

*Wait. Hold on. I really wish that part hadn't come out.*

Rowan, who had been cordial just a moment ago, switched back to staring daggers.

"NAKED?" Teodor bellowed. "WHY WAS HE NAKED?"

There was a small commotion across from me. Vagid had joined Orrin on the ground, laughing.

"I don't remember why I didn't have any clothes on, or why I was in the river. I must have hit my head on one of the rocks after I fell in," I said lamely.

"Oh, you poor thing!" said Isla as she and Lyra came out of the house with more food.

"Maybe a monster ate your clothes!" Pip yelled from his spot beside Arlo.

"Maybe he's just a pervert who likes swimming naked in the river," Arlo quipped.

"Arlo!" Isla said in her mom voice. "That's enough of that talk!"

Once Isla had calmed everyone down, we began dinner.

"So, Evan, you don't remember anything?" asked Rowan. "About the river? And your clothes?"

*He's definitely suspicious of me.*

"Just the river part. Everything before that is… fuzzy," I said.

"He was a mess when we found him," Orrin added, now back in his chair.

"A mess how?" asked Rowan.

"Is that really a good topic for the dinner table, *dear*?" Isla said, leaning hard on the *dear*.

"Do you have scars? Show us your scars!" yelled Pip.

I held up my left hand and wiggled my fingers. A couple of them still bent a little wrong, and there was a pale scar across the knuckles. Nothing too scary. Rocks could do that. Probably.

Pip looked deeply disappointed.

The rest of dinner was, mercifully, uneventful. I answered their questions as vaguely as I could, which did not seem to ease Rowan's suspicions.

When everyone had finished eating, Nara, Lyra, and Isla began to clear the table. I stood up and started gathering some of the dishes to help.

"Evan, don't bother with those. We'll get them," said Isla.

Across the table, Rowan's frown eased. Slightly.

"Thank you for the wonderful dinner," I said to Isla and Rowan.

"You're very welcome, dear," was Isla's response.

"Uh-huh," was Rowan's.

As we left, I waved to Nara's family. Pip, Isla, Fenna, Lyra, Rowan, and Teodor all waved back, while Arlo leaned against the fence, looking like any other bored teenager.

We headed back toward the chapel. As we passed each of their homes, my friends said their goodbyes and peeled off one by one, until it was just me.

Instead of going into the chapel, I turned and walked toward the river.

*As long as I keep my pace slow, I should be able to make it.*

By the time I reached the water, my legs were shaking and the sun was almost down. I found a beautiful Class II rapid with a small eddy and a beach at the bottom. Beside the beach sat a stone bench.

*Odd place for a bench. Someone must have wanted to sit here a long time ago.*

The stone was cool when I sat down, which was wonderful, since my legs were very sore from the walk. I took a calming breath, closed my eyes, and listened to the river.

*Tired Guy? Please be here.*

"Of course I'm here," said a tired voice.

*Well, it's not like I can see you.*

"True."

*You weren't "of course here" last week. Not even hold music.*

"You were on Amaranth's doorstep. It's… awkward."

*I need some guidance.*

"Go on."

*It doesn't feel right to lie to the people in Plan B about my past.*

"And why is that, do you think?"

*It just doesn't feel right to lie to friends.*

"Friends, is it?"

*…Shut up.*

"Then you should not lie to your friends. But be prepared for doubts and questions. And I would wait until after your meeting with the Guild."

*Yeah, the Guild. The ones who were going to send someone "in a day or two." Over a week ago.*

"I've heard that for a lie to be believed, it should closely resemble the truth."

*So for the Guild, stay close to the truth. Just not the whole truth.*

"Good night, Evan. I wish you luck with the Guild meeting."

*Thanks. Night, Tired Guy.*

I sat on the bench for a few more minutes, just listening to the river and enjoying the peace. Then I got up and started the long walk back to the chapel.

*Tomorrow is not going to be fun.*
