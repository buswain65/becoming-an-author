# Chapter 19

The next morning, Vagid arranged for the return trip by coach and paid my fare. Again.

"Another loan. This is becoming a habit. Maybe we should find you someone to manage your money," said Vagid.

"I volunteer," said Orrin. I could swear I saw little dollar signs flashing in his eyes.

"Maybe we could ask Prue to help manage Evan's... money," said Nara with a little wink.

We gathered our gear and found Marek praying at the altar. We waited for him to finish, then Vagid said, "Marek, we'll be taking our leave of you now. We're heading back to Millbrook to report on the job."

"Feel free to stop in again should you be in the area," said Marek. "Hopefully we will have more acolytes when you next visit."

The coach arrived, and we loaded our gear. I could tell the rest of Plan B was really enjoying the ride back, because they were all asleep. My ride was a bit bumpier, and with my acid burns still not fully healed, I didn't sleep at all.

We reached Millbrook that evening, just before dinnertime. Orrin stretched and yawned, then hopped out of the coach with a spring in his step. Vagid wiped his face, then climbed out and waited for Nara and me. Nara climbed out, then turned back with a smirk. "Hurry up, Evan. We don't have all day."

I climbed out of the coach like an old man bent with age. When I straightened my back, it gave a series of snaps, crackles, and pops.

"Come on, old man," said Vagid. "We need to turn this in to the Guild."

"I'm not that much older than you are," I said.

"A day or a year, it's still older," said Orrin, following Vagid and Nara toward the Guild.

By the time we got to the Guild, I wasn't as stiff or bent over as when I got off the coach. I expected Vagid to join Mara's line, but this time the whole party joined Prue's. I looked at Vagid, who just stared back at me. Then I looked at Nara, who had a slight smile. Then I looked at Orrin, who was grinning like an idiot.

*Yeah. This is a setup.*

When we got to the front of the line, Prue said, "Come to pick me up for dinner? Usually I like it one-on-one, but if you need an audience, that's fine too."

My face went hot. My ears felt hot enough to set my hair on fire.

"Um... well... not at—at the moment," I said. "We... uh... finished the job in Barley Ford. It was a slime. We brought back the core. It's, um, a little cracked."

"So, dinner is still on, just not right now?" asked Prue. "Good to know."

Vagid set the bag with the cracked slime core on the counter. It took up a large part of Prue's section.

Prue looked at the bag, then at me, then back at the bag. "Huh. And this is?" she asked.

"The slime core," I said.

"Huh," said Prue. "We're going to need to see the Boss about this."

She grabbed the bag and led the way to the Guild master's office.

Without knocking, she opened the door and walked in. "Cutty, Plan B found a huge slime at that old farmhouse in Barley Ford." Then she dropped the bag onto the Guild master's desk with a thud.

Cuthbert had his chair turned toward the window when we came in, but at the sound of Prue's voice, he began to turn it around to face us.

He looked at the bag, then at us, and said, "Slime cores are paid by weight. Prue, please take this to the monster assessment office for weighing."

Prue picked up the bag and headed out the door. "Don't forget about dinner, Evan," said Prue as she left.

Orrin was grinning. Nara snickered. And Vagid just gave me a self-satisfied look.

*This is all payback for our jokes about him and Mara.*

"It is fortuitous that you stopped in today, as we have the results of the Hollin's Cross assessment," said Cuthbert. "No undead or ghostly presences detected."

*Not anymore, there aren't.*

"Great news," I said. "When can we get paid?" *I really need some money.*

"You should be able to pick up the funds at the counter downstairs on your way out," said Cuthbert. "Thank you for your work."

We filed out of Cuthbert's office and went downstairs. This time Vagid joined Mara's line while the rest of us waited by the door.

"Hi, Vagid! It's so good to see you again!" said Mara.

"Hi, Mara. It's good to see you too. Guild master Cuthbert said our payment for the Hollin's Cross job was ready," said Vagid.

"Let me get that for you from the cashier," said Mara, heading for a little office off to the side.

Seconds later, Mara returned with four medium-sized pouches. She handed them all to Vagid and said, "Thank you for your work!"

Vagid brought the pouches over and handed them out. Per Guild rules, they all held the same amount.

I immediately took out four rays and handed them to Vagid. "I think that covers the coach rides," I said.

"Don't forget about the interest," said Nara with a little giggle.

"Uh, okay. How about I buy us dinner at the cafe for the interest?" I said.

"Done," said Nara. "Okay," said Vagid. "Free food," said Orrin.

"Come on," I said, and we left for the cafe.

It occurred to me, halfway through my soup, that this was not the dinner Prue had meant.

Dinner didn't cost me too much, just four rays and six sparks. So much for an affordable interest rate.

After dinner, we went our separate ways, agreeing to meet at the cafe in the morning for breakfast.

I made my way back to the chapel and found Halden resting in his quarters. He looked at all of my bandages and said, "Rocks?"

"Slime," I said. "I... kind of... fell into its acid."

"Hmf. I'll add it to your tab," said Halden. Then he raised his hand. "Light Heal."

The rest of the acid burns healed completely, and I could finally take off the bandages.

"New clothes you have there," said Halden. "Becoming an acolyte of Korrath?"

"Um, no. It was the only place to stay in Barley Ford. After my sliming, the priest there was nice enough to give me clothes from one of his acolytes," I said.

I remembered Marek's prayer. "Halden, does the phrase 'the Great Reset' mean anything to you?"

"Doesn't reset mean starting over?" said Halden, and he closed his eyes again.

*Yeah. But I get the feeling it has a deeper meaning than just that.*

*I'll ask Tired Guy.* With that thought, I headed down to the river, away from the chapel.

I sat on the bench beside the Class III rapid.

*Tired Guy?* I thought.

*Evan, how was your trip?* said Tired Guy.

*We found more notes, but we haven't gone over them yet. Does the phrase "the Great Reset" mean anything to you?* I asked.

I heard a mental sigh before Tired Guy answered.

*That is the plan of some of the gods,* said Tired Guy.

*Plan for what?* I asked.

*To start over.* A long pause. *Everyone.*

*Everyone? As in—*

*Ask me again when you're ready for the answer,* said Tired Guy.

I thought of Prue's wink. Orrin's grin. *Everyone.* I didn't want to be ready, so I asked something else.

*Xavier said he left Earth the same year I did. How is that possible?* I asked.

*There is a time difference between your old world and this one,* said Tired Guy.

*A difference? Like, how much of a difference?* I asked.

After a pause, Tired Guy said, *One year in this world equals one day on Earth.*

*I've been in this world for a little over a month. So if one year equals one day, I've been gone from Earth for about two or three hours. Curt is still snoring. No one has even noticed I'm gone.*

*If I stay here too long, I'll go home an old man and have to start my life over.*

*Hours. Home is only hours away.*
