# Chapter 15

"I want to get more information from Xavier. Would it work for you if I translated what he's saying?" I asked the group.

"That might make this go faster," said Halden. Vagid nodded.

"Xavier, can you give us your story? I'll translate it for the others as you go. You might need to wait for me to catch up, since I haven't done this before," I said.

"Sure. That's fine."

I nodded for him to start.

"Two hundred and twelve years ago, I was summoned. When I arrived, I was taken to Lumenhold, where they held a feast. I didn't realize it at the time, but that was part of the trap. All of the food I was served had a drug in it. It didn't take long before I was unconscious," Xavier said.

As I translated, I watched the faces of Plan B and Halden. Vagid was not happy about something, and the more he heard, the less happy he became. Orrin was completely focused on the story, his mouth hanging open just a bit. Nara had taken a seat on the floor, her knees pulled up and her arms wrapped around them. Halden stood expressionless, just taking in the story.

"What power did Xavier get?" asked Nara.

I relayed the question to Xavier, who said, "They called it Oracle Touch. All I had to do was touch something, and I'd know its past."

I passed this back to Nara and the others, then nodded for Xavier to continue.

"When I woke up, I was in a cell, chained to the wall. The only opening was a small window in the door, so that was all I could see of the outside. It started as soon as they realized I was awake. I didn't get to sleep a whole night for a long time. They would wake me every little while, then beat me with clubs. Feed me gruel. Then repeat the whole process. Over and over again."

I passed it on. My voice caught on "clubs." Vagid kept shaking his head and rubbing his face, like he was trying to figure something out.

Xavier continued, "I have no idea how long this went on. Eventually, they tested me. I'd be woken up, fed the gruel, then told, 'If you kill this mouse, we won't beat you as long.' At least, that's how the testing started. Each time, the thing I had to kill was larger. A rat. A kitten. A goblin. A kobold. A goat. A sheep. On and on. If I ever refused, or didn't kill the way they wanted, I'd be beaten until I blacked out. Then they'd wake me and beat me more."

I was having a hard time keeping my voice from cracking as I translated, but I needed to keep going, for Xavier's sake. Nara sat quietly, crying and watching. Halden was starting to show more emotion, but I still couldn't tell which one.

Xavier went on. "Eventually, they brought in a sick child. It looked like it had been tortured, and it was barely alive. They told me to kill the child, and I'd get the same meal the guards got. I hesitated, and they said, 'Look, it's almost dead already. You'd be doing it a favor.' I have no idea why, but—I did as they asked."

Xavier was crying now. Most of us were too. Halden seemed to have a better grip on his feelings than the rest of us, but his jaw muscles were clenching rapidly.

"Whenever they had me kill something, they always told me to use a tool. A knife. A club. A hatchet. A spear. It was always different. They kept me from touching the animals and people. They didn't want me to use my power. But when they had me kill the child with a club, I was able to touch him with my bare foot. And I saw. The boy had grown up in the countryside. He had a younger sister he loved. His parents loved him and his sister. They were so happy. Then I saw an ugly man kill his parents and sister and take him away. He was put into a cell with dozens of other children. They had to fight over scraps of bread. That was where all of his injuries came from. I could see that he was afraid all the time, and that more than anything, he just wanted to be with his mother, father, and sister again. I guess that's why I went ahead and killed him. I thought it would be a kindness."

I was openly crying as I relayed this part. It was hard, and I had to stop every little while—the feelings were just too raw. Eventually, I got this piece of the story out.

Orrin looked like he might be sick and kept running his hand through his hair. Nara pulled her cloak tighter around herself and kept crying. Halden looked like he wanted to choke the life out of something. Vagid had his head in his hands. Then he looked up. Tears were flowing down his face, and he looked like he might be sick too.

After wiping my eyes, I nodded for Xavier to continue.

"After that, the waking, the gruel, and the clubbing didn't matter to me. The boy had saved me. He finally helped me get my wits back. I knew they had to be putting me through this for a reason. The only reason you brainwash someone is so they'll do what you want without you having to be around. If I could make them believe I'd reached that point, without really being under their control, then I had a chance. So I hid the crack in their brainwashing. I couldn't let them know I wasn't under their control anymore. I had to keep doing as I was told. Whoever they asked me to kill, I always found a way to touch them, so I would know who they were and what kind of life they'd lived. It was often old people who begged me to end it, or people who were already at their limits. It was the only way I could hope to make amends for their deaths."

I had to take a break to get something to drink and rest my voice. I sat beside Nara and held her. Maybe she needed it. I knew I did. I needed to know I was connected to someone. I needed that right now.

After a few minutes, I nodded to Xavier again.

"As time went on, it became clear that I had passed all of their tests. I was taken to Lumenhold again. But not for a drugged feast this time. The Chancellor praised me for excelling at all their tests. He said, 'We have so much work to do, and so many who need our attention.'"

Xavier stopped, looked at me, and said, "My time is getting short. I can feel it. You need to get the notes before I pass on. It's all in there. How I got out. What I found after."

"I don't know where they are," I said.

"I scratched a stop sign near the floor, under the place I hid them. They're in a hidden cubby that you can't see from the floor. You have to climb up and feel around. You'll find it."

*That's what that symbol was that Nara found!*

As he explained where the notes were, he began to fade. Little by little, he was getting more transparent. Plan B and Halden noticed it too.

"Evan, something's happening," said Vagid.

Unlike the chimeras, Xavier was already in his real form. The only change was his clothes. The armor and helmet faded into a button-down shirt and jeans, and on his feet was a pair of black Chuck Taylors.

*That's why his shoes looked wrong with the armor. He'd been wearing Chuck Taylors the whole time.*

"He's about to pass over," said Halden.

"Has he told you where the notes are?" asked Nara.

"Yes. You showed me, and I just didn't realize it," I said, smiling at Nara.

"Goodbye, Evan, and good luck, my friend," said Xavier as he faded away.

I had to take a few deep breaths to get control of my feelings. I hadn't known Xavier for long, but I felt like we were real friends. And I worried that I would end up just like him.

Orrin came over and put his hand on my shoulder. "Come on. Let's make sure Xavier's work doesn't go to waste."

I nodded and led the group back to the room with the little stop sign scratched into the wall. I told them where the notes would be, but we'd need to work together to get them. Halden offered to lift Vagid up so he could feel around for them.

A few minutes later, Vagid yelled, "Oh! Yuck!" and pulled out a leather-wrapped package covered in bugs and mouse droppings.

We finally had Xavier's notes. Once Halden set Vagid down, he handed me the package.

I unwrapped it. Then unwrapped it again. And again. Xavier was a careful guy.

The first pages were diagrams and lists. Then I found a table. The header had columns for units, success, and notes.

| Units | Success | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | No | Dead on arrival |
| 140 | No | Dead on arrival |
| 175 | No | Lived two days |
| 230 | No | Woman, 20s, no power? |
| 310 | Yes | Man, 40s, fire, weak |
| 390 | Yes | Woman, 24, water, strong |
| 410 | Yes | Man, 31, oracle touch, strong |
| 450 | ? | MC unknown, unknown, strong |
| 475 | ? | MC unknown, unknown, strong |
| 515 | ? | MC unknown, unknown, too strong killed everyone |

I read down the list. It wasn't until I got to "Man, 31, oracle touch" that I realized this was a list of their summonings. There were a lot of attempts, with three marked as successes, and the last of those was Xavier.

Everyone crowded around, and it was hard to read in the low light.

"Let's head back to the inn. Mave is probably worried about us. And the common room will have more light than this cellar," I said.

"Good idea," said Vagid.

On our way out, Halden took a moment to bless the chapel and say a few words for Xavier. He washed the altar with holy water to remove the stains.

As we were leaving, I looked back at the chapel. The dead trees and grass were still there, but it didn't have the ominous feeling it had when we first arrived.

We got back to town just before dinnertime. When we walked into the inn, Mave saw us and dropped a tray of drinks on the floor. "By Amaranth! I hoped and prayed you'd come back, my lovelies."

She was crying as she ran up and hugged each one of us. Orrin was smiling when he bent down so Mave could give him a hug. Nara and Mave held on to each other for a moment, then both were crying and laughing.

Mave gave Halden his hug, and he looked like a little kid whose mother was hugging him in front of his friends. *Mom, you're embarrassing me.* Mave walked over to Vagid, who had a serious expression right up until she grabbed him for the hug. Then he looked surprised, and slowly, he smiled.

When I got my hug, I held on to Mave for a few moments and whispered, "We did it. The chapel is clean again."

Mave stepped back, wiped her tears with her apron, and said, "Well, just as I promised, dinner is ready!"

Mave flew back to the kitchen and quickly returned with a huge tray of food, which she unloaded at our corner table.

We all sat down and began hoovering up the food. It was just what we needed to lift our spirits again.

After we'd had our fill, I took out the notes so we could get a clear look at them in better light.

I went back to the page with the list and pointed to the row I'd noticed earlier.

"Is Oracle Touch a common ability here?" I asked.

"No. As far as I've ever heard, there was only one Otherborn who had that skill," said Halden. "But I couldn't swear to it."

"Well, let me think for a second." I closed my eyes. *Tired Guy?*

*Evan! Thank goodness you're safe,* Tired Guy said.

I guess he really was worried about me.

*Do you know how many Otherborn had the Oracle Touch ability?*

*Just Xavier.*

*Thank you. And I missed you too.*

I opened my eyes and said, "I have it on good authority that the only Otherborn who had that skill was Xavier."

Halden looked at me and raised an eyebrow.

"Just a hunch," I said.

"What do the numbers under 'units' mean?" asked Orrin.

It took me a second to answer. "People. Sacrifices."

"Oh, gods," said Orrin. He looked sick.

"No god approved this," said Halden.

Nara had her hand over her mouth, her eyes wide.

Vagid shook his head and looked at the table.

Vagid looked back at the page with the list. He had the same look he'd had in the chapel, like he was working something out. Then he pointed to the row after Xavier's and said, "MC... monster cores. They put monster cores in the summoning."

Nara gasped and whispered, "The chimeras..."

I looked at Nara and nodded.

Halden was watching me. "You don't seem surprised about the monster cores," he said. "Why not?"

"When we killed the chimera in the berry patch, Tired Guy helped him reincarnate. The man told Tired Guy what had happened to him."

"Then you've known about this the whole time?" yelled Vagid.

"Yes, and I'm sorry I didn't tell you sooner, but—" I said quickly.

Vagid took a deep breath, then said, "But we didn't believe your story to begin with. Adding the chimera part wouldn't have helped."

Vagid looked up at me, lightly punched my arm, and said, "Yeah, I get it."

Orrin nodded and punched my other arm. Nara came over and held my hand for a moment before going back to her seat. Halden looked at me, nodded, and said, "Hmf."

We were looking at the page with the list when I noticed the edge of another page sticking out beneath it. Something was written in the margin in a light pink color: "b on hs."

I pulled the page out and took a closer look. A line drawn in the same light pink color led to a circled phrase in the text: "nine months to one year." It was talking about how long it took to perform the ritual.

"I wonder what 'b on hs' means," I said.

I looked at each of them. Nara shook her head. Orrin wasn't paying attention; he was busy getting a mug of mead. Vagid said, "No idea." Halden just grunted and shook his head.

Orrin came back with his mead, looked at our faces, and said, "What'd I miss?"

Nobody answered him. Nara just patted the seat next to her.

"So what do we do now?" asked Vagid.

I looked down at the notes, at the units column. Somewhere, there was a number for me too.

"We read every page," I said.

*Nine months to a year. And they'd probably already started over.*
