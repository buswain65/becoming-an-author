# Defective: Story Bible

A quick reference for drafting. The full reasoning behind each decision is in the plan tab.

## The Core

- **Title:** *Defective*
- **Premise:** When a power-mad nation begins summoning people from other worlds, an average guy (Evan) who doesn't believe in gods, love, or even himself makes a deal with a god to find a way to stop the practice of summoning, or risk losing any chance at returning to his world and life.
- **Theme:** What do you believe in when the world tells you you're nothing?
- **Lie to truth:** "I don't matter, and I don't have a future" becomes "Everyone matters, and we make our own future regardless of what others think."
- **Want vs. need:** He wants to go home; he needs something worth believing in. He stays by choice, as Eddy's first cleric, healing anyone for free and adventuring with Plan B.
- **Satire target:** Isekai power fantasies. When stuck, ask what a typical isekai would do, then find the honest or absurd opposite.
- **Point of view:** Evan in first person; occasional kingdom-side chapters from Garrick and, sparingly, Silas Malgrave, in third person.
- **Length:** 30 to 35 chapters, about 90,000 words.

## Places

- **The Sunhallow Kingdom:** the summoning nation.
- **Wendway:** the river Evan's "corpse" floats down.
- **Thornspire:** the mountain holding the summoning chamber.
- **Greywater Keep:** the dilapidated castle near the northern border; Johan's hideout and models.
- **The Malgrave ancestral home:** outside the castle; the family archives, desecrated by sacrifice.
- **Millbrook:** Halden's town and church, on the Wendway downstream from the castle; the friends' home base, where their families live.
- **Garnerby:** the town to the east where the harvest festival is held; flooded by Johan in chapter 22.
- **The blighted farmhouse:** the plague mage's notes in a deep root cellar.
- **The desecrated church:** "the old chapel near Hollin's Cross," just over the western border, in Dravia ("That's not even in Sunhallow anymore"). Three or four days' walk from Millbrook. Listed at the Guild as "Haunted, desecrated church near the border. No takers." Xavier's ghost and notes. Its layout is identical to Amaranth's Chapel in Millbrook (it is also a chapel of Amaranth). Xavier scratched an octagon, a stop-sign shape any Earth person would recognize, into the stone to mark where his notes are hidden; to locals it is just scratches. Evan finds it familiar but can't place it at first (ch. 13).
- **Neighboring nations:** Dravia, Kethmoor, and the Altan Reach.
- **On Earth:** the Nantahala River in North Carolina; a neighborhood and grocery store in Sweden, one stop on Evan and Katie's quickie European trip (six countries in nine days), which is why he can't place the models at first.

## Other Names

- **Loric Pryce:** the lead summoner who runs the ritual.
- **"Mr. Pliers":** Evan's private name for the torturer, who otherwise stays nameless.
- **Lumenhold:** the royal castle where King Bertram and Silas live. Tired Guy cannot see into any of it ("Too much death has occurred there"). The side facing Lower Lumenhold has no gate and backs onto a hill with caves. A day and a half from Millbrook by coach, on the main road (not downstream on the Wendway); arriving at dusk on the second day. A routine trip (Vagid's brother Dorran, a guard, travels it).
- **Lower Lumenhold:** a village a short way outside Lumenhold, home to the lower-tier workers who serve the castle but aren't allowed to live in it (laundresses, kitchen porters, stable hands, carters). It sits on the gateless side of the castle, or between two gates, so the Chancellor never sees it when he rides out. Locals may call it "the Lower."
- **The Adventurers' Guild, Millbrook Branch:** where the party takes jobs.
- **Plan B:** the party's name (Nara, Orrin, Vagid, and later Evan).
- **Amaranth's Chapel, Millbrook:** Halden's church.
- **Rafe and Lida:** Orrin's (and Katia's) parents.

  Rafe is broad, dark-haired and bearded, looks too young to have a grown son, and looks nothing like Orrin (Evan's first guess: "Uncle? Neighbor? Very confident burglar?"). Orrin takes after Lida: same face, older ("like an age-progression photo on TV"). Their narrow two-story house near the town center reminds Evan of a Pennsylvania row home.
- **Faren and Ruta:** Vagid's parents. Vagid's older brother, Dorran, is a castle guard, proud and loyal to the crown (to King Bertram, not the chancellor, a difference he discovers at the climax): possibly among the Ruiner hunters, and among the soldiers Silas orders killed at the climax, so Garrick's defiance saves him.
- **Currency:** suns (gold), rays (silver), and sparks (copper). Twelve sparks to a ray (ch. 10; Evan first assumed ten, like cents), and twelve rays to a sun (144 sparks to a sun). The dark coins in Halden's offering box are old rays.

* **Guild receptionists:** Della and Prue (older, bored, efficient, unimpressed) and Mara (young, pretty, very perky, slower; the receptionist Evan's isekai brain expects). Vagid has a thing for Mara, so he always gets in her line, which is always the longest (ch. 7; Orrin: "Vagid has a thing for Mara"; Vagid: "If by 'thing' you mean respect"). Mara is just as warm to everyone ("Hi, Vagid! It's so good to see you again!" is how she greets every adventurer). She's clueless about Vagid's feelings, and he's clueless that she greets everyone the same way. If Evan shows interest in a receptionist in the epilogue, it should be Della or Prue, not Mara.

  **Prue (seed for the epilogue):** early twenties, waist-length auburn hair worn in a braid; unimpressed ("Why would I have heard about a meeting?"). Mara is younger, probably late teens. In ch. 7 Evan describes Prue in detail but gives Mara only "young, pretty, and very perky": a quiet first sign that Prue is the receptionist Evan may be interested in by the epilogue. Keep it subtle: he notices her, never says so. In ch. 9, handed a squishing sack of forty-seven goblin ears, her only words are "I hate my job" before she counts them anyway (efficient but miserable). Possible payoff: she leaves, or gets promoted out of counting ears, by the epilogue.

  **Cuthbert Mossley ("Boss"):** Guild master of the Millbrook Branch; a mouse-like beastkin, the first non-human Evan meets. Everyone calls him "Boss" (always capitalized), except Prue, who calls him "Cutty" to his face and barges into his office without knocking. Very by-the-book, not overly bright, and not pushy: he cares that the report is complete and in the proper format, not whether it's true, and stops asking once the paperwork is done. Play him straight and dignified, never cutesy; the comedy comes from the world fitting around his size. In ch. 7 he takes Evan's "hit my head on the rocks" story at face value; the tension in the meeting comes from Plan B hearing Evan's story for the first time and noticing the gaps.
* **Scythe of Destiny:** the rival party that calls Orrin "the tag along."
* **Larkmere:** the city Johan destroyed after being ordered to "find the insurgents and remove them."
* **Rachel Lin:** the fourth summon, the Plague Mage (a nurse on Earth). Summoned a hundred and seventy-five years ago (about six months before Evan in Earth time). Her notes are careful records of illnesses she was forced to cause; underlined: "I won't do it again." Built the slime trap at the blighted farmhouse; possibly stole the treasury writ from the Lumenhold archives. Fate unknown (ch. 20).
* **Grace Whitfield:** the sixth summon, author of the stone notes (suggested former job: geologist).
* **Brightwater House:** the Malgrave estate, home of the family archives, desecrated by sacrifice.
* **Otherborn:** what locals call summoned people.

- **Nara's family** (the chapter 6 meal): father **Rowan** (protective, warms up the moment he learns Evan has no magic); mother **Isla** (warm; first to assume Evan is Nara's boyfriend); maternal grandparents **Grandma Fenna** (nosy, asks when the wedding is) and **Grandpa Teodor** (hard of hearing, loudly repeats every misunderstanding); older sister **Lyra** (teases Nara); younger brothers **Pip** (begs Evan to show his magic) and **Arlo** (declares him "boring"). The family has fended off suitors who wanted Nara's power, so a man with no magic delights them. Keep it a misunderstanding: both deny it, mortified; at most one callback later.

  As written in ch. 6: the dinner is tonight, outdoors behind their New England-style cottage. Rowan warms up at "no magic," then turns cold again when Nara blurts that they found Evan naked. He asks "A mess how?" and Isla shuts it down ("*dear*"). Evan shows Pip only his left hand (bent fingers, knuckle scar; "Rocks could do that. Probably."). **Open thread:** Rowan still suspects Evan's story, and the scars on Evan's arm (knife wounds, flayed skin) stay hidden. If they're ever seen, rocks won't explain them.

  **Where Evan lives:** after recovering, he still sleeps at Amaranth's Chapel as Halden's guest ("get out of Halden's hair" meant leaving the sickbed). Halden's "tab" is only a joke: Plan B paid for the healing, and Halden charges nothing. Evan doesn't know that, so the Guild jobs (ch. 7 on) still give him a reason to earn money and find his own place. A later move (e.g., to Orrin's old room at Rafe and Lida's, near Katia) is still open.

## Chapter 9 Continuity

- **Timeline:** the morning after ch. 8. The goblin job takes one day (the farm before noon, home by dinnertime); the Guild visit is the next morning; the pay comes "tomorrow" (the day after), so ch. 10 should open by collecting it.

## Hard Rules

**Summoning**

- Only possible in the chamber inside Thornspire, built by the old god.
- Mortals power summoned abilities through human sacrifice; more sacrifices mean stronger abilities.
- The sacrifice magic consumes the bodies: nothing is left of the sacrificed. (Thirteen's ritual cost four hundred sacrifices and nine months of preparation, per Silas in K1.)
- **Where the river bodies come from:** Silas has the lead summoner (Loric Pryce) experimenting to perfect the sacrifice magic. A successful sacrifice consumes the body; when an experiment fails, the person still dies, but the body stays. Pryce is also teaching the sacrifice magic to other mages so they can assist him, and their practice attempts fail far more often. Those failures, not the ritual's sacrifices, are the bodies that go into the Wendway at Thornspire and turn up in the nets downstream (two or three every few weeks).
- Monster cores were tried instead; they turned the summoned person into an amalgamation of the monsters (chimeras). Only chimeras are failed summonings.
- The chamber works through the composition and positioning of its floor stones and tarnished silver inlay. Grinding them to sand ends summoning forever.
- Preparation officially takes nine months to a year; with enough sacrifices, about six.

**The wall between worlds**

- Every summoning punches a new opening; the wall is one summon from collapse, which would destroy both worlds.
- A reverse summoning requires all the gods together, and must be done with the sealing in one act. After the seal, no one can cross again.
- A god can send a soul home through reincarnation: new body, no memories.

**The reset**

- Requires all gods; they lose everything they know.
- Kills all animals and people. Plants, buildings, books and objects survive. Monsters survive because of their cores.
- Nobody, not even the gods, knows where new people come from.
- It happened last cycle, for the same reason: summoning.

**Gods**

- No miracles: open, visible acts are forbidden. Hidden coincidences and acts through mortals are fine. Acts on the wall are unseen and allowed.
- The rule is a misunderstanding: the last calamity came from the chamber, not intervention itself.
- Sites of mass sacrifice or desecration are blind spots; spirits who die there don't reach the gods.
- Evan's god: rare contact without believers; memories too vast to search without the right question.

**Language:** the summoning gives summoned people the ability to speak, read and write the Sunhallow Kingdom's language, **Hallish** (first named in ch. 4). Each region (Dravia, Kethmoor, the Altan Reach) has its own language, but Hallish is the common language everyone in the region learns, so travelers and traders can get by with it. The summoning gives only Hallish: Evan does not understand the regional languages. The gift does not survive death: Xavier's ghost lost his Hallish ("When I died, it went with the rest of me," ch. 14). Hallish is named on the page in ch. 4 (Vagid), ch. 8 (Orrin), ch. 14 (Xavier) and ch. 16 (Evan's transcription). Summoned people can still use their own Earth languages, which no one in this world understands. Evan mumbled English in chapter 4 only because he was delirious from his injuries. Earlier summons (Xavier, Rachel Lin, Grace Whitfield) chose to write their notes in English so the kingdom couldn't read them, which is why only Evan can.

**Time:** one year in the fantasy world is one day on Earth. Evan learns this rule from Tired Guy in ch. 19. Earlier thread (ch. 14): Evan does not know this rule. When Xavier (here about 212 years) learns Evan left Earth the same year he did, both are baffled ("That's impossible. That's the year I left." / "Two hundred years for him. The same year for me."). The explanation should come later (Tired Guy, Xavier's notes, or the archives). The Earth year is never stated; Evan's answer is reported as "I told him."

**Magic:** some locals have small abilities; a few are near summon-level. No levels or status screens. Monster cores are real, and valuable.

**Races:** Sunhallow is mostly human; other races are rare there but more common in the neighboring nations (Dravia, Kethmoor, the Altan Reach). The first non-human Evan meets is the Millbrook Guild master, a mouse-like beastkin everyone calls "Boss" (ch. 7).

## Tone Rules

- **Never a joke:** the massacred city, the sacrifices, Johan's grief, the chimera victims, Evan's darkest moments.
- **Only Silas jokes about people being worthless,** and readers should wince. Evan jokes about his own worthlessness early, then stops.
- **Comedy sources:** failed isekai expectations (strongest early), the kingdom's bureaucratic logic (constant, aimed at the powerful), friends (growing), the god's Earth fragments (occasional).
- **Voices:** Evan dry and quiet; Orrin loud and frequent. When Evan stops joking, it should carry weight.
- **Themes are shown, not stated.** Keep emotional lines short.
