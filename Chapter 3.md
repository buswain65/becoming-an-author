# Chapter 3

*Could have fooled me.*

"A man who does not know his own worth is surely a fool," said the tired voice in my head.

*Okay, Tired Guy.* Then: *Wait. Was that from someone famous?*

"I was paraphrasing. But yes."

*Why?* I thought, after a pause.

"Why? Because I need you. And you matter to me."

*How am I supposed to do anything here? Like this?*

"You will not always be in this condition. And you will not always be in this place." He paused. "Soon, you will be moved somewhere... different."

The thudding had stopped. In its place, I heard the guards huffing like they'd just finished a marathon.

"Okay. We won't get anything else out of him," Mister Pliers told them. "Get rid of this trash."

*Why can't I feel the pain anymore? And why does it feel like I'm floating?*

"Because I have brought your mind closer to me, so that we could talk."

*And who are you?*

"I don't have a name at the moment. Please, keep calling me Tired Guy for now."

*This floating feeling is super weird.*

"It is what it is. Look. They're taking you out. You will be somewhere different soon. Not necessarily better, but different."

*Cryptic much?*

I watched them drag my body across the chamber floor. From up here, I could finally see the black swirls properly. They weren't random. They made one enormous design that covered the entire floor.

They pulled me out through the cave entrance, and for the first time I could see where I was. The chamber was inside a mountain, and the mountain was surrounded by forest. Somewhere close by, I could hear white water. The guards dragged me toward the sound.

They stopped at a short drop-off above a rapid. Class III, by the look of it. Then, without any fanfare, they flung my body in.

*Well. At least I missed the rocks.*

*Tired Guy, I'm not seeing much of an improvement here.*

"I never promised an improvement. Only something different. Be patient."

My body was drifting down the rapid headfirst, its feet pointing upstream.

*Tired Guy! You have to turn me around. Feet downstream!*

"Does it matter?"

I gave him Walt's story as fast as I could: the kid, the shoelaces, the rocks. *The river will hold on to anything you give it.*

"I see. I have very little ability to influence things in this world directly. But I will do what I can."

I watched my body brush against a low-hanging branch and slowly swing around until its feet pointed downstream.

*Thank you.*

My body made it through the Class III rapid no worse for wear than it already was, and drifted into a quieter stretch of river.

"We need to talk."

*Aren't we?*

"This is more like talking on the phone. I would like a face-to-face, as your boss at work would say. We have a little time now. I will bring you to me."

The floating feeling grew stronger, and my surroundings faded to white. Then a man slowly appeared, as if through fog.

"Greetings. I am very happy that I could meet you, and help you," he said.

*This must be what Tired Guy looks like in person.*

He had a kind face, light brown hair, and an average height and build. Most of all, he looked like he had just pulled three all-nighters in a row. I couldn't remember ever seeing anyone who looked so tired.

"Hi. I'm Evan McIntyre, and I'm guessing you're Tired Guy," I said.

"Correct. And I want to apologize to you for how this turned out."

"Why would you apologize for what those jerks did?" My voice rose a little.

Tired Guy sighed. "Because I am the reason you did not receive any abilities."

"Um. What?" My mouth hung open.

"You need to understand how you would have received an ability, and what it would have cost."

Patiently, he explained. When humans perform a summoning, the only way to power it is human sacrifice. The more people they kill for the ritual, the more power the summoned person receives. This time, Tired Guy had redirected those souls, so they could be reincarnated instead of being burned as fuel to give me a fancy ability.

"People *died*," I said slowly. "So I could get a superpower?"

"They did. But they are not gone. I made sure of that."

It took me a second to catch up with the rest of what he'd said. "Wait. Back up. Redirected souls? Who *are* you?"

"I am a god."

"I don't believe in gods."

"You're talking to one."

"That's not the same thing."

"Okay. Why did you do all this? Why me?"

"The summoning ritual has been going on for a long time," Tired Guy said.

"Yeah. Mister Pliers said it had worked for centuries. Until me."

"Yes. Each time the ritual is performed, it punches a hole through the boundary between your world and this one. Think of each world as a room in a building. The rooms sit side by side, and the wall between them is load-bearing. Every summoning cuts a new hole in that wall. After enough of them, the wall looks like Swiss cheese."

*Swiss cheese? Since when do gods know about Swiss cheese?*

"And the building collapses," I said.

"Yes. In the analogy, the building is ruined. For our worlds, it would mean the total destruction of both."

"Well. That would suck."

"Indeed."

"If both worlds are going to survive, we must patch the wall, and prevent any more holes," Tired Guy said. "And the wall cannot survive another summoning."

"Still not seeing where I come into this plan."

"I cannot act directly in this world. I want you to be my acolyte here, so that together we can save both worlds."

I thought for a moment. "That sounds like a really long-term commitment. And honestly, I just want to go back home."

"Like the souls that were sacrificed, you too can be reincarnated in your world," Tired Guy said reluctantly.

"What exactly does being reincarnated mean, in this case?" I asked.

"Your soul would be sent back to your world. You would lose your memories, and start over as a baby."

A clean slate. No memories. No Katie. No *maybe both*. It should have sounded great.

It didn't. For some reason, the idea of nobody remembering being me made my chest tight.

*Huh.*

"Is there any guarantee I won't be born into a bad family, or die young?"

"Unfortunately, no. I can offer no such guarantees."

"I'd really like some degree of certainty," I pressed.

"The past is set in stone. The future is like a river."

"I've been in a river," I said, trying to stay calm. "It flipped me over and tried to drown me."

"Then you understand it better than most." He looked sad when he said it, the way you look when a friend is going through something bad.

"What other options do I have?" I sighed. "Is there any way I can go back as *me*? Body, memories, everything?"

"There might be. A reverse summoning. But it is as dangerous as the ritual itself, and without the right knowledge, it could bring down the wall. I can promise to try. I cannot promise it will work."

"What knowledge?"

"That is what I need your help with. Help me find the information we need, so we can repair the boundary and prevent the collapse. The same knowledge may be what brings you home."

"What does it mean to be your acolyte?"

"An acolyte acts for a god in the world, where the god cannot. You would be my hands." He paused. "I would ask three things of you. Stop the next summoning. Find what the earlier summoned people learned. Some of them left records, and some of those are in places I cannot see."

"Places a god can't see?"

"Ground that has been desecrated. Where the blood of sacrifices was spilled. The kingdom has made more of those than you would like to know."

"And the third thing?"

"Belief. A god's strength comes from the people who believe in him. I have none."

"I told you. I don't believe in gods."

"I know." He gave me a tired smile. "I'm not asking you to believe in me today."

*Work with a god, or go through diapers, teething and adolescence all over again. Put that way, the choice was obvious.*

"Okay," I said. "I'm in."

Tired Guy smiled, and for a moment he looked a little less tired.

"Now comes the unpleasant part," Tired Guy said. "I need to put you back into your body. That means all the pain will come flooding back."

*Wonderful.* "Yeah, I was afraid of that."

"I can help you some, but right now I am very limited, and I cannot do anything while you are still in danger. Once you are back in your body, you will need to get to shore. Are you ready?"

"As ready as I'll ever be, I guess."

Then came an intense falling sensation, and the cold of the water.

"Huh. I guess the pain wa— AAAAHH!" I gasped as it slammed back into me. The cold water numbed it a little, but it carried its own dangers.

At least I didn't pass out again.

*End of chapter 3 draft.*
