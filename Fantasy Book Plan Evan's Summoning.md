## Stage 1: Premise and Theme

**Premise:** When a power-mad nation begins summoning people from other worlds, an average guy (Evan) who doesn't believe in gods, love, or even himself makes a deal with a god to find a way to stop the practice of summoning, or risk losing any chance at returning to his world and life.

**Theme:** What do you believe in when the world tells you you're nothing?

- **Want vs. need:** He wants to go home; he needs to find something worth believing in. A strong ending lets him choose what he needed over what he wanted.
- **Satire target:** Isekai power fantasies. The setup inverts the staples: no cheat skill, a god who removes powers, disappointed summoners, a "chosen one" thrown in a river.
- **Love arc:** The god's compassion first, then friendship and found family. No romance in book one.
- **Bigger threat:** If summoning isn't stopped, the god may be forced into a fix that triggers a world-ending reset.
- **Plotting test:** When stuck on a scene, ask what a typical isekai would do, then find the honest or absurd opposite.

## Stage 2: Evan

Evan's arc runs from believing he doesn't matter to acting as though everyone does, himself included.

- **Who:** Mid-twenties, junior software developer in his first job. Recently moved to a new city where he knows few people. Trying kayaking, climbing, mountain biking, hiking and camping through a local college outdoor club.
- **Wound:** Katie ends their relationship: "I need to invest my time and energy into things that actually matter and have a future." Evan asks, "I don't matter, or I don't have a future?" She answers, "I don't know... maybe both..." Her uncertainty leaves a gap, and he fills it with the worst answer.
- **Lie:** "I don't matter, and I don't have a future."
- **Want:** To go home.
- **Need:** Everyone matters, and we make our own future regardless of what others think. He may learn that others matter before he believes it about himself. Show it through action, never a speech.
- **Flaw:** He knows what needs to be fixed or done but doesn't speak up, or starts hesitantly and gets overridden by louder voices. He lets small things go wrong because they don't seem to matter, but small things grow into larger ones.
- **Strengths (each with a limit):**
  - Noticing things (signature strength): he spots problems but doesn't speak up.
  - Beginner outdoor skills: a few club trips, so he knows some things and gets others wrong.
  - Modern knowledge: he knows germs exist but can't make antibiotics.
- **Lens:** An isekai reader with deadpan delivery. He keeps expecting the tropes to save him; they never do. His arc mirrors this: he stops waiting for the universe to make him special. Jokes must land for readers who don't know isekai.

## Stage 3: The God and the Summoners

Each main character answers the theme differently, and only Evan's final answer is true.

| Character | Belief about worth |
| --- | --- |
| The chancellor | Only *I* matter. |
| The previous summon | *Nobody* matters. |
| Evan (start) | *I* don't matter. |
| Evan (end) | *Everyone* matters. |

**The kingdom**

- Summons people to brainwash into weapons to conquer other nations.
- 12 to 15 summons over a few centuries; each is enormously expensive. Only the summon before Evan is still alive, but earlier summons left documents and clues.

**The chancellor**

- From a family that has held the office for generations and driven the summoning program.
- A true believer, raised to think only his family deserves to rule the world.
- Built an ROI case for replacing the previous summon after he killed everyone in a large city.
- Keeps the king as a figurehead and spreads rumors of "the king's decision" when things go wrong.

**The king:** A figurehead trying to stay alive. Also treated as disposable; a possible reluctant ally or comic figure.

**The previous summon**

- Swedish; early thirties when summoned decades ago; had just been promoted to grocery store manager; had a girlfriend.
- Loved building detailed scale models of houses in his neighborhood. Still builds them in secret, hidden from the chancellor.
- Water mage: abrasive water-jet cutting, ice spears, freezing targets solid. Draws water from the air; in dry places, from nearby living creatures.
- Brainwashing works but degrades; he is now unstable, tired of life, and values no one. Ordered to "find the insurgents and remove them," he killed a whole city.
- Line: "Why search for the needle when you can flood the field?"
- Evan was summoned to replace and dispose of him. Evan wins by reaching him, not out-fighting him.

**The god**

- Born from the suffering of earlier summons; carries their memories and knows all past events, but not the future.
- Motto: "The past is set in stone; the future is like a river." Use once early, once at the end with new meaning.
- Limits: rare contact without believers, memories too vast to search without the right question, and blind spots the kingdom has hidden (possibly the chancellor family's archives).

**The deal and stakes**

- The god explains why summoning must stop but can't promise to send Evan home. A reverse summoning is as dangerous as a normal one and could destroy both worlds.
- The god will try, but needs information from earlier summons to reduce the risk. Evan gets it by stopping the next summoning and investigating.
- Failure means more people summoned and broken, more destruction, the chancellor's conquest, and no way home.
- If Evan stays at the end, it must be by choice, not only fear.

**Ticking clock:** The next summoning takes six to nine months to prepare. Options to add urgency: ritual milestones Evan can disrupt, a shorter second clock (the water mage hunting him), or a midpoint jump where the timeline shrinks.

## Stage 4: World and Magic Rules

The wall between worlds is one summon from collapse, so the chancellor's next ritual would end both worlds.

**Summoning**

- Only possible in one chamber inside a mountain, built by an older god to bring over people as his believers.
- When that god fell out of favor, the chamber's location was lost. The chancellor's ancestors later found it again.
- The old god powered summoned people's abilities himself. Mortals can only power them through human sacrifice.
- The god took the power meant for Evan and used it to save the sacrifices' souls, reincarnating them in another world.

**Going home**

- The god can send Evan's soul to Earth by reincarnation, but his body stays and his memories are wiped.
- Going home as himself requires a reverse summoning, which is still a summon and could collapse the wall unless made safe.
- The god plans to use Evan's information to send him home, then strengthen the wall so summoning becomes impossible. After that, no one can ever cross again.

**The wall and the reset**

- Every summon weakens the wall between worlds. If it collapses, the worlds are crushed into each other.
- The only emergency fix is a reset: all gods working together, losing everything they know and have, and all life in the world wiped out. It has happened a very small number of times.
- Evan's god argues a reset is "only a patch, not a fix": the chamber survives, and the problem returns.

**Gods**

- Three other gods (war, plague/famine, plenty; see Stage 7). They are weighing a preemptive reset before the chancellor's ritual.
- Evan's god asks for time to get the information needed for a real fix.
  - By mutual agreement, gods keep their presence hidden. Any help must look natural, so they need mortals. (Why they agreed is still open.)

**Local magic**

- Some ordinary people have small abilities; a few are close to summon-level, about a grade down. Evan has none.
- No game-like systems (levels, status screens), which keeps disappointing Evan's isekai expectations.

**Setting placeholders:** the Kingdom, the Castle, the River, the Mountain (summoning chamber), Neighbor Nations 1 to 3.

## Stage 5: Tone Plan

The book opens dark with a thread of deadpan humor, lightens as Evan finds friends, darkens again at the midpoint and climax, and ends warm.

**Opening chapter**

1. Dinner after an easy whitewater run on the Nantahala in North Carolina. The group are college students; Evan is the only one who didn't attend the college. They talk about the last rapid; instructors tell stories of past trips. Keep it brisk, a few pages.
2. Plant: an instructor repeats the river rule, "if you swim, float on your back, feet downstream."
3. Show his flaw: Evan almost speaks up about something and gets talked over.
4. Evan crashes early. He half-wakes needing the bathroom; his sleeping bag feels hard. He's naked, strapped to a table in a dim stone room. "For a dream, it could be worse..." It immediately gets worse.
5. Possible beat: his isekai brain hopes for a status screen, then the summoners realize he has nothing.
6. Once the torture starts, after a beat of real pain: at least he doesn't need the bathroom anymore.

**The first relief**

- The god speaks in Evan's mind, explains he's faking Evan's death, and lifts his consciousness out of his body, so the pain stops.
- Thrown in the river, Evan tells the god to point his feet downstream and why. Something bumps the body so it slowly turns, looking natural. His first real choice: he speaks up, and a god listens.
- Out of sight, the god warns the pain will return when he pushes Evan back in. He can help Evan, but not until Evan's body is out of danger. By mutual agreement, the gods must keep their presence hidden, which is why the feet-downstream turn had to look natural.

**Never a joke**

- The massacred city, the sacrifices, the water mage's grief, and Evan's darkest moments.
- Only the chancellor jokes about people being worthless, and readers should wince. Evan jokes about his own worthlessness early, then stops.
- Late beat: a friend jokes that he's worthless. Evan asks if he'd say that about Evan. "Never, you're my close friend!" Evan: "Then don't say it about mine."

**Comedy sources**

| Source | When it leads |
| --- | --- |
| Failed isekai expectations | Strongest early; fades as Evan stops waiting to be made special |
| Kingdom's bureaucratic logic | Constant; aimed at the powerful, never the victims |
| Friends | Grows once allies appear; the warmest humor |
| The god's Earth fragments | Occasional seasoning |

**Voice note:** Evan's humor is how he copes. A moment where he stops joking will carry great weight.

## Stage 6: Plot Structure

Evan wins by understanding, not power, and ends the book by giving away the thing he wanted most.

| Beat | Your story |
| --- | --- |
| Opening | Nantahala dinner; Evan the outsider |
| Inciting incident | Summoned, tortured, "killed," floated down the river |
| First turning point | The deal: stop the summoning, gather information; belief left open |
| Midpoint | The notes reveal the clock is six months, not nine |
| Low point | The water mage floods an area hunting the Ruiner; people Evan cares about are hurt |
| Climax | Evan reaches the water mage, the chamber is ground to sand, the chancellor falls |
| Resolution | Evan believes in the god, the gods unite, and Evan gives the last crossing to the water mage |

**First turning point: the deal**

- The god offers reincarnation in either world. Evan leans toward Earth, then learns it means starting over as a baby with no memories.
- The house analogy: summonings punch new openings in a load-bearing wall until it looks like Swiss cheese and the roof collapses, destroying both worlds. "Well, that would suck." "Indeed."
- Evan asks for certainty. God: "The past is set in stone. The future is like a river." Evan: "I've been in a river. It flipped me over and tried to drown me." God: "Then you understand it better than most."
- The god asks for three things: stop the next summoning, gather information from earlier summons, and belief. Belief is left open ("I'm not asking you to believe in me today").
- Drafting notes: show Evan surprised he cares about staying Evan; let him notice the god knows "Swiss cheese"; ground the dialogue in setting.

**First half to midpoint**

1. Evan finds the first of the earlier summons' hidden notes.
2. Midpoint: from that information, the god realizes the ritual is closer to six months away, not nine. Possible strengthener: the sacrifices are already being gathered, including people Evan knows.
3. Evan's desperate push for more information alerts the chancellor that someone is trying to stop the ritual. He calls this unknown enemy "the Ruiner."
4. The chancellor sends groups to stop or kill the Ruiner.

**Second half to low point**

- The chancellor withholds the water mage at first, fearing he'll destroy the kingdom ("flood the field").
- Escalation: ordinary hunters fail; Evan evades them with noticing, outdoor skills and friends.
- Low point: out of desperation, the chancellor unleashes the water mage, who floods an area to flush out the Ruiner. People Evan cares about are hurt or killed. The lie twists into "everyone near me gets hurt." Evan is tempted to give up or take reincarnation. His jokes stop.

**Climax**

1. The water mage blows a hole in a mountain; Evan realizes he can't defeat him and tries to understand him instead.
2. Evan asks about the models, which are of places in Sweden. Evan visited them on a vacation with Katie before the breakup. He tells the mage what he saw: the store's abrupt, poorly chosen changes after he vanished, and a missing-person poster by the door. Home barely changed; for the people there, he's been gone about a month.
3. Evan sees the mage builds them because he wants to go home and stop being a tool of this evil. He gets the mage to see that people are precious to others, as his models are to him.
4. Evan, the mage and the god work out how the chamber works: the composition and positioning of the floor stones. Grinding them to sand ends summoning forever; no one knows how to rebuild it.
5. At the chamber, Evan tries to reach the chancellor and fails. The chancellor orders hundreds killed for the ritual; the water mage gets the sacrifices out.
6. Out of sacrifices, the chancellor orders his highest-ranking officer to kill the soldiers for the ritual. The officer kills the chancellor on the spot.
7. The king, present unwillingly, squeaks at the bloodshed, then realizes he is safe and back in power, though he doesn't want it.
8. The chamber is ground to sand by the mage's abrasive water before the ritual completes.

**Resolution**

- Comforting the despairing mage, Evan realizes even the mage matters. Loneliness and desperation to get home twisted him. Evan thinks, "There but for the grace of God go I," literally true, since the god took his powers.
- That realization is Evan's belief moment: he believes in the god.
- A single god can't do a reverse summoning, but all the gods together can. Evan convinces them, speaking up when it matters most. The crossing and the sealing of the wall happen in one act.
- Evan tells the god he's decided: send the water mage home. Evan stays by choice, with friends here. The motto returns: the future is a river, and he's steering.
- Epilogue: the water mage, back on Earth, carries his guilt and works to make up for his past. Possibly flood relief or water rescue, with a new model on his workbench.

**Plants for the climax**

- Chapter 1: strapped to the table in the chamber, Evan notices the floor stones' odd pattern and thinks nothing of it.
- Early: a mention of travel stings; Evan deflects from the trip with Katie.
- Middle: Evan recognizes a model or a place name from the trip and says nothing.
- Earlier hints the mage can be reached: the hidden models, a hesitation during the hunt, the god's memory of him weeping after first draining an animal.

## Stage 7: Subplots and Supporting Cast

Evan's friends each struggle with their own worth, and Evan's experience of worthlessness is what lets him help them.

**Finding Evan**

- After accepting the deal, Evan feebly swims to shore, still naked, and lies in the sand at the river's edge.
- Adventurers on a job find him, patch him up, and carry him, unconscious, to the nearest town. Possible reason for their job: investigating bodies turning up downriver.
- He wakes in a church, tended by Halden, a cleric of the god of plenty.
- Halden is wary; Evan knows nothing about the gods or the world. "Was he dropped on his head or something?" Evan can adopt it as cover: he hit his head on a rock in the river.
- When Evan is well enough to leave, they stop at Vagid's house for clothes (everything is too short, and Vagid shrugs it off), then Orrin's (they fit), and at another home for a quick meal. Evan's first taste of belonging.

**The adventurers**

| Character | Role | How they see their worth | What they want |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nara | Wind mage with near-summon-level power | People only ever talk about her magic | To use her magic to create beauty instead of destruction |
| Orrin | Ranger with exceptional bow and hunting skills | Other parties call him the "tag along"; he believes them over his friends | To believe he belongs |
| Vagid | Leader; shorter than average and mocked for it | Secure; cares only what his friends think | To help his friends see their worth, but he can't, because he's never felt worthless |

- Nara mirrors the water mage: a creator treated as a weapon. Possible art: sand sculpture, shaped clouds, wind music. Possible epilogue image: art made from the chamber's sand.
- Orrin makes the "I'm worthless" joke late in the book. Differentiate his loud, frequent self-deprecation from Evan's dry, quiet humor.
- Evan can help where Vagid can't, because he's been there.

**Halden:** Cleric of the god of plenty, based at the town church. Friends with the adventurers but not in their party. Wary of Evan at first; his growing trust mirrors, and helps cause, his god's shift at the climax.

**The gods**

Each god was born from a collective experience that touched the whole world.

| God | Born from | Personality | Stance on the reset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evan's god | The suffering of the summoned | Honest, dry, compassionate | Against: "a patch, not a fix" |
| War god | A great war | Pragmatic, accepts losses | For |
| Plague/famine god | A plague or famine | Fearful, cautious | For |
| Plenty god | A time of plenty, before summoning caused trouble | Nostalgic | Torn: sees the need, but wants the time of plenty back |

At the climax, each needs a different argument. Evan reaches the plenty god as someone who also wanted the past back and chose the future.

**The general**

- Appears in the summoning chamber in chapter one, uneasy, and is ordered to dispose of Evan's body.
- Later commands the hunt for the Ruiner. He has seen Evan's face; if he recognizes the Ruiner and keeps quiet, that's a step in his turn.
- Layered doubts: soldiers lost in the hunt and treated as numbers; the massacred city; the flood; finally, the order to kill his own soldiers.

**The low point, refined**

- The flood injures members of Orrin's and Vagid's families. No one dies.
- The families are met earlier (the clothes and the meal) so readers feel it.
- Option: the group briefly fractures; Orrin lashes out in grief, or Evan pulls away. Healing the rift is part of his climb out.

**Subplots**

1. Evan's belief in the god: from "I don't believe in gods" to choosing to believe at the climax.
2. The general's quiet turn, seen from the kingdom's side.
3. Halden's wariness turning into trust, connected to the plenty god's swing vote.

Lighter threads: Nara's art, Orrin learning to believe his friends.

**Point of view:** Evan's, with occasional kingdom-side chapters. Suggested viewpoints: the general (his turn) and, sparingly, the chancellor (the Ruiner panic and ROI meetings).

### Xavier

- The third summon; a crime scene technician. The brainwashing worked at first, but his training helped him recover.
- Wanted to understand summoning to find a way home. Traveled to other countries, searching for people with suspiciously strong abilities from the Kingdom; found one or two summons before him, each with different abilities.
- Documented the chamber's strange look and the composition of its stones, and a correlation between abilities granted and number of sacrifices.
- Found a document saying preparation takes nine months to a year, with "6months" written in the margin in red, in English, by someone else from Earth (left a mystery). The red faded to something like "b on hs."
- Killed by the Kingdom's assassins in an old desecrated church outside the Kingdom, where he hid the notes. Because he died on desecrated ground, the god never received his memories.
- Now a ghost, bound by unfinished business; hostile because centuries of visitors never understood him and tried to force him to move on.
- The god's memories are vast; the notes give him the right questions.

### New rules and story changes

- **Time:** one year in the fantasy world is one day on Earth. Centuries of summoning cover about ten months of Earth time, so all summons come from roughly the last year, and the water mage was taken about a month before Evan.
- **The water mage and Evan's trip:** Evan and Katie took a four- to five-day trip to Sweden and toured the water mage's neighborhood. Since the mage vanished right after his promotion, someone was promoted too quickly, and the store made abrupt, poorly chosen decor changes. Evan noticed.
- **Missing-person poster:** in Evan's early memory of the trip, a faded poster by the store door, "a guy about my age." At the climax he realizes whose face it was.
- **Desecrated ground** is a blind spot for gods; spirits who die there don't reach them.

## Opening Events and Open Questions

The opening is sketched; the questions below feed Stages 3 and 4.

**Opening as planned:**

1. Evan falls asleep at camp on an overnight kayaking trip with friends and is summoned in his sleep.
2. A new god, with no acolytes yet, notices the summoning and makes sure Evan gets no special abilities, to buy time to find a permanent way to stop summoning. Each summon weakens the wall between worlds, which is now one summon from collapse (see Stage 4).
3. Evan wakes to torture. The summoners spent vast resources and expected some ability; they conclude he was so far below normal that his power only raised him to their level.
4. They kill him and dump his body in a river. The god has only made him appear dead.
5. As he floats out of the castle, the god apologizes and asks for his help to stop this happening to others.

**Open questions:**

- [ ] How can the opening hint at the humor so the dark-to-funny shift feels earned? (Answered in Stage 5.)
- [ ] Evan's first real choice (the deal) must come soon and cost him something.
- [ ] Could the water mage sense something odd about Evan's "corpse" in the river?
- [ ] Are the water mage's models of his Swedish neighborhood or of this world's town? (Answered: Sweden.)
