## Stage 1: Premise and Theme

**Premise:** When a power-mad nation begins summoning people from other worlds, an average guy (Evan) who doesn't believe in gods, love, or even himself makes a deal with a god to find a way to stop the practice of summoning, or risk losing any chance at returning to his world and life.

**Theme:** What do you believe in when the world tells you you're nothing?

- **Want vs. need:** He wants to go home; he needs to find something worth believing in. A strong ending lets him choose what he needed over what he wanted.
- **Satire target:** Isekai power fantasies. The setup inverts the staples: no cheat skill, a god who removes powers, disappointed summoners, a "chosen one" thrown in a river.
- **Love arc:** The god's compassion first, then friendship and found family. No romance in book one.
- **Bigger threat:** If summoning isn't stopped, the god may be forced into a fix that triggers a world-ending reset.
- **Plotting test:** When stuck on a scene, ask what a typical isekai would do, then find the honest or absurd opposite.

## Stage 2: Evan

Evan's arc runs from believing he doesn't matter to acting as though everyone does, himself included.

- **Who:** Mid-twenties, junior software developer in his first job. Recently moved to a new city where he knows few people. Trying kayaking, climbing, mountain biking, hiking and camping through a local college outdoor club.
- **Wound:** Katie ends their relationship: "I need to invest my time and energy into things that actually matter and have a future." Evan asks, "I don't matter, or I don't have a future?" She answers, "I don't know... maybe both..." Her uncertainty leaves a gap, and he fills it with the worst answer.
- **Lie:** "I don't matter, and I don't have a future."
- **Want:** To go home.
- **Need:** Everyone matters, and we make our own future regardless of what others think. He may learn that others matter before he believes it about himself. Show it through action, never a speech.
- **Flaw:** He knows what needs to be fixed or done but doesn't speak up, or starts hesitantly and gets overridden by louder voices. He lets small things go wrong because they don't seem to matter, but small things grow into larger ones.
- **Strengths (each with a limit):**
  - Noticing things (signature strength): he spots problems but doesn't speak up.
  - Beginner outdoor skills: a few club trips, so he knows some things and gets others wrong.
  - Modern knowledge: he knows germs exist but can't make antibiotics.
- **Lens:** An isekai reader with deadpan delivery. He keeps expecting the tropes to save him; they never do. His arc mirrors this: he stops waiting for the universe to make him special. Jokes must land for readers who don't know isekai.

## Stage 3: The God and the Summoners

Each main character answers the theme differently, and only Evan's final answer is true.

| Character | Belief about worth |
| --- | --- |
| The chancellor | Only *I* matter. |
| The previous summon | *Nobody* matters. |
| Evan (start) | *I* don't matter. |
| Evan (end) | *Everyone* matters. |

**The kingdom**

- Summons people to brainwash into weapons to conquer other nations.
- 12 to 15 summons over a few centuries; each is enormously expensive. Only the summon before Evan is still alive, but earlier summons left documents and clues.

**The chancellor**

- From a family that has held the office for generations and driven the summoning program.
- A true believer, raised to think only his family deserves to rule the world.
- Built an ROI case for replacing the previous summon after he killed everyone in a large city.
- Keeps the king as a figurehead and spreads rumors of "the king's decision" when things go wrong.

**The king:** A figurehead trying to stay alive. Also treated as disposable; a possible reluctant ally or comic figure.

**The previous summon**

- Swedish; early thirties when summoned decades ago; had just been promoted to grocery store manager; had a girlfriend.
- Loved building detailed scale models of houses in his neighborhood. Still builds them in secret, hidden from the chancellor.
- Water mage: abrasive water-jet cutting, ice spears, freezing targets solid. Draws water from the air; in dry places, from nearby living creatures.
- Brainwashing works but degrades; he is now unstable, tired of life, and values no one. Ordered to "find the insurgents and remove them," he killed a whole city.
- Line: "Why search for the needle when you can flood the field?"
- Evan was summoned to replace and dispose of him. Evan wins by reaching him, not out-fighting him.

**The god**

- Born from the suffering of earlier summons; carries their memories and knows all past events, but not the future.
- Motto: "The past is set in stone; the future is like a river." Use once early, once at the end with new meaning.
- Limits: rare contact without believers, memories too vast to search without the right question, and blind spots the kingdom has hidden (possibly the chancellor family's archives).

**The deal and stakes**

- The god explains why summoning must stop but can't promise to send Evan home. A reverse summoning is as dangerous as a normal one and could destroy both worlds.
- The god will try, but needs information from earlier summons to reduce the risk. Evan gets it by stopping the next summoning and investigating.
- Failure means more people summoned and broken, more destruction, the chancellor's conquest, and no way home.
- If Evan stays at the end, it must be by choice, not only fear.

**Ticking clock:** The next summoning takes six to nine months to prepare. Options to add urgency: ritual milestones Evan can disrupt, a shorter second clock (the water mage hunting him), or a midpoint jump where the timeline shrinks.

## Stage 4: World and Magic Rules

The wall between worlds is one summon from collapse, so the chancellor's next ritual would end both worlds.

**Summoning**

- Only possible in one chamber inside a mountain, built by an older god to bring over people as his believers.
- When that god fell out of favor, the chamber's location was lost. The chancellor's ancestors later found it again.
- The old god powered summoned people's abilities himself. Mortals can only power them through human sacrifice.
- The god took the power meant for Evan and used it to save the sacrifices' souls, reincarnating them in another world.

**Going home**

- The god can send Evan's soul to Earth by reincarnation, but his body stays and his memories are wiped.
- Going home as himself requires a reverse summoning, which is still a summon and could collapse the wall unless made safe.
- The god plans to use Evan's information to send him home, then strengthen the wall so summoning becomes impossible. After that, no one can ever cross again.

**The wall and the reset**

- Every summon weakens the wall between worlds. If it collapses, the worlds are crushed into each other.
- The only emergency fix is a reset: all gods working together, losing everything they know and have, and all life in the world wiped out. It has happened a very small number of times.
- Evan's god argues a reset is "only a patch, not a fix": the chamber survives, and the problem returns.

**Gods**

- Two to four other gods, details to come in Stage 7. They are weighing a preemptive reset before the chancellor's ritual.
- Evan's god asks for time to get the information needed for a real fix.

**Local magic**

- Some ordinary people have small abilities; a few are close to summon-level, about a grade down. Evan has none.
- No game-like systems (levels, status screens), which keeps disappointing Evan's isekai expectations.

**Setting placeholders:** the Kingdom, the Castle, the River, the Mountain (summoning chamber), Neighbor Nations 1 to 3.

## Opening Events and Open Questions

The opening is sketched; the questions below feed Stages 3 and 4.

**Opening as planned:**

1. Evan falls asleep at camp on an overnight kayaking trip with friends and is summoned in his sleep.
2. A new god, with no acolytes yet, notices the summoning and makes sure Evan gets no special abilities, to buy time to find a permanent way to stop summoning. Each summon weakens the wall between worlds, which is now one summon from collapse (see Stage 4).
3. Evan wakes to torture. The summoners spent vast resources and expected some ability; they conclude he was so far below normal that his power only raised him to their level.
4. They kill him and dump his body in a river. The god has only made him appear dead.
5. As he floats out of the castle, the god apologizes and asks for his help to stop this happening to others.
